# External Authority Plan

**Status: plan only. No outreach has been performed, no one has been contacted, and no
contact names appear in this document.** Every action below requires a human to
execute it. Nothing here should be automated.

Written 2026-08-13 against a catalogue of 21 published apps and 21 published
articles (54 further articles written and gated as drafts).

---

## 1. The rules this plan operates under

These are constraints, not suggestions. Breaking them risks a manual action that
would cost more than every link gained.

**Never do any of these:**

| Prohibited | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Buying links, or paying for "guest post placement" | Link schemes under Google Spam Policies. Paid links must be `nofollow`/`sponsored`, at which point you are buying traffic, not authority — price it as advertising. |
| Private blog networks, link farms, "1,000 backlinks" gigs | Detectable, worthless, and the cleanup is worse than the original problem. |
| Reciprocal link swaps ("link to me and I'll link to you") | Explicitly named as a scheme. Occasional natural mutual linking is fine; systematic exchange is not. |
| Automated or templated mass email | Gets the domain flagged as a spam sender and burns the addresses permanently. |
| Fake personas, invented credentials, or fabricated press coverage | Fraud, and trivially disproven. |
| Comment/forum links dropped purely for SEO | Nofollowed anyway, and it gets accounts banned. |
| Directory submissions in bulk | Near-zero value since roughly 2012. A handful of *relevant, curated* directories are fine (§5). |

**Always:** disclose who you are, link to `/press/` rather than retyping company
facts, and accept "no" the first time.

## 2. An honest read on what will actually work here

Worth being blunt, because it shapes everything below.

An indie Android studio with no press history has a weak hand for classic link
outreach. Editors are not looking for another app studio. Three things,
specifically, are the exceptions — and the plan concentrates on them:

1. **Less-common language apps.** Cantonese, Thai, Malay, Khmer and Russian. This
   is the genuine differentiator: the language-learning market is saturated for
   Spanish and French and thin for Khmer. People maintaining resource lists for
   these languages have few Android options to point at. **This is the strongest
   asset the site has and should absorb most of the effort.**
2. **Free with no paywall.** Every app is free to download. Curators of "free
   tools" and "no-subscription" lists filter on exactly this, and it is verifiable
   from the Play listing rather than a claim.
3. **A studio that publishes an editorial policy and a press kit.** Rare at this
   size. It makes the site cheap to cite, because a journalist can verify company
   facts without emailing anyone.

What will *not* work: pitching general tech or Android blogs on "we made an app."
They receive that daily and it is not news.

## 4. Do this first: properties you already control

Highest certainty, zero rejection risk, and mostly unfinished. Work through this
before contacting a single stranger.

- [ ] **Play listing "Website" field, per app.** Confirm each of the 21 listings
      points at its own app page (`reigncreativellc.com/apps/{slug}/`) rather than
      the bare domain. A per-app URL is a stronger entity association and sends
      store visitors somewhere relevant.
- [ ] **Play listing "Privacy Policy" field.** Should resolve to `/privacy/`.
- [ ] **Google Play developer page.** Confirm the developer name and description
      match `/press/`. The site already declares this link in `Organization.sameAs`;
      the association is stronger when it is consistent in both directions.
- [ ] **Google Search Console.** Verify the property if it is not already, submit
      `https://reigncreativellc.com/sitemap.xml`, and confirm 66 URLs are
      discovered. This is the measurement baseline for the whole plan.
- [ ] **Google Business Profile**, only if the LLC has a genuine business address
      you are willing to publish. Skip otherwise — do not invent a location.
- [ ] **Social profiles.** *Confirm which of these actually exist before acting —
      this document does not assume any do.* For each one that does, set the
      website field to `reigncreativellc.com` and the bio to the short boilerplate
      on `/press/`. These are `nofollow` and will not pass authority directly; they
      matter because they corroborate the entity.
- [ ] **GitHub.** The site deploys from a GitHub repository. If that account or
      repo is public, its website field is a legitimate owned link.
- [ ] `app-ads.txt` — already live at `/app-ads.txt` with publisher
      `pub-4872012511804466`. Verified present; no action.

## 5. Legitimate site categories to approach

Categories, not a target list — you must qualify each individual site against §6.

**Tier A — best fit, start here**

- Language-specific learner resource pages (university language department
  resource lists, "learning Khmer" personal blogs, Southeast Asian and Slavic
  studies reading lists). Often maintained by one person who welcomes a genuine
  addition.
- Language-learning community wikis and curated resource repositories.
- "Free / no-subscription app" roundups where free is the actual filter.
- Android app roundups scoped to a *category* ("best apps for learning Thai"),
  never generic "best Android apps."

**Tier B — plausible**

- Sports simulation and franchise-mode enthusiast sites and blogs.
- Expat and travel resources for Cambodia, Thailand, Malaysia — practical
  language-tool sections.
- Accessibility and offline-use roundups, if and only if an app genuinely works
  offline. Verify before claiming.

**Tier C — low yield, do not prioritise**

- General tech news. Not news.
- App review sites that charge for review. That is paid placement.
- Generic business directories.

## 6. Qualification checklist

Run before contacting any site. If you cannot answer yes to the first five,
do not send anything.

1. Does the site cover this specific topic, or only tangentially?
2. Is it maintained? Check the most recent post or update date — abandoned
   resource lists pass nothing and nobody reads them.
3. Would a reader of that page genuinely benefit from the link, if you remove
   your own interest from the question?
4. Does the site have real, non-spun writing by an identifiable person or team?
5. Is it free of "write for us — $X per post" pages and casino/pharma outbound links?
6. Does the page you want to be on already list competitors? (A good sign — it is
   a real resource list, not a lead magnet.)
7. Would you be comfortable if the site owner published your email verbatim?

**Automatic disqualifiers:** the site sells links; the content is AI-spun or
scraped; outbound links go to unrelated commercial spam; the domain is a
lookalike of a known brand; they lead with pricing.

## 7. Angles, per asset

One angle per asset. Each is a factual claim you can substantiate, not a pitch.

**Language apps and beginner guides — the main effort**

- *The angle:* free Android apps for languages that most app roundups omit
  entirely, plus beginner guides that cite FSI difficulty categories from
  state.gov and are honest about what the apps do not teach.
- *Ask:* inclusion in an existing resource list, alongside whatever is already
  there. Not a review, not a feature.
- *Substantiation:* the Play listing (free, verifiable), and the beginner guide
  showing the depth of the material.
- *What makes this work:* you are solving the curator's problem — a thin section
  on their own page — not asking them to solve yours.

**`/press/`**

- *The angle:* company facts verified against live Play listings, with a stated
  verification date, plus explicit statements about what the studio does *not*
  claim (no download counts, no awards, no press coverage).
- *Ask:* nothing. Send it when someone requests details, and let it do the work.

**Sports GM apps**

- *The angle:* seven free franchise/draft simulators for Android, a genre that is
  mostly paid or desktop-only.
- *Ask:* inclusion in mobile sports-sim roundups and enthusiast community
  resource threads.
- *Caution:* this space overlaps with heavily commercial affiliate content. Apply
  §6 strictly — much of it will fail.

**Editorial policy**

- Not an outreach target. Use it as evidence when a curator asks how the content
  is produced.

## 8. Communities

Participation, not link-dropping. Most of these links will be `nofollow` — the
value is that people who maintain resource pages read them.

- Language-learning forums and subreddits, particularly the **per-language**
  communities for Khmer, Thai, Malay, Cantonese and Russian, which are small
  and unusually receptive to a genuinely free tool.
- Android app-discovery communities that permit self-promotion in a designated
  thread or on a designated day. Read the rules first; several ban it outright.
- Sports simulation and franchise-mode communities.

**Rules:** use a real identity, disclose that you are the developer *every single
time*, answer questions where you are not promoting anything, and never post the
same text in two places. If a community has a self-promotion thread, use it —
posting outside it is how accounts get banned and how a domain earns a reputation
that follows it.

---

## 9. Tracking

Keep one file with: date, site, page targeted, angle used, outcome. That is
enough. It exists so you do not contact the same person twice and so you can
tell which angles work.

Measure results in Google Search Console under **Links → External links**, not in
a third-party backlink tool. GSC is free and authoritative for this domain.

**Do not set a link target.** Counting links you must acquire is what pushes
people toward buying them. The measurable goal is that the language category and
its five app pages acquire *any* genuine referring domains from Tier A over the
next two quarters — plus every item in §4, which is entirely within your control
and should be complete regardless of what outreach achieves.

---

## 10. Honest expectations

Most outreach gets no reply. A 5–10% response rate on genuinely well-qualified,
individually written emails is normal, and a single link from a maintained
university language resource page is worth more than fifty directory listings.

The §4 owned-property work will almost certainly produce more measurable benefit
than the outreach in §5–§8, and it costs only an afternoon. Do it first.
