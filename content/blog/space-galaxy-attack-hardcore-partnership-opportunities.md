[Space Galaxy Attack Hardcore](/apps/space-galaxy-attack-hardcore/) offers bosses with weak points, a connected campaign and a Hardcore mode that its Google Play listing says is designed for experienced shoot 'em up and bullet hell players. This page is for **mid-core publishers** considering an acquisition or publishing deal, and for **localisation partners** looking at sci-fi titles.

## The campaign structure

The listing describes a **vertical scrolling shoot 'em up** with **anime and manga-inspired artwork**. Its campaign runs through **connected sectors**, which the listing names as:

1. orbital stations;
2. neon cities;
3. asteroid fields;
4. enemy factories;
5. the Galactic Core.

Each sector brings its own **enemy movement and attack patterns**, **minibosses**, and **multi-phase galaxy bosses** with **destructible modules and weak points** that must be found and hit. Runs can be **paused, saved and continued**, so the campaign survives being put down mid-sector.

## Ships and the upgrade tree

Players choose from **multiple ships**, each with its own primary weapon, alternate attack, shield and ultimate. Gold collected in missions buys **permanent upgrades**: damage, firing speed, piercing, shields, hull durability, movement handling, cooldowns and ultimate charge. A failed run still moves the fleet forward. The game is free with ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases.

## Three modes for three appetites

| Mode | What it offers |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | Classic arcade progression |
| Hardcore | Higher enemy durability, faster projectiles, more complex formations, extra boss patterns with fewer openings |
| Endless | No campaign; survive as long as possible |

The app's page on this site is direct: Hardcore is **built for a steep difficulty curve**, and the listing calls it "a demanding but fair arcade challenge". A publisher's marketing should say so rather than promise an easy ride.

## What a mid-core publisher would evaluate

From the public listing, a publisher can see the content footprint: the named campaign sectors, multiple ships, a deep upgrade tree, three modes and boss design built on weak points. Questions that follow, such as technical architecture, live-content plans or performance, are for a later stage under confidentiality. Arrangements Reign Creative LLC is open to include **publishing and distribution**, **licensing**, **co-development** of new sectors or ships, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, and **acquisition**.

## Localising a vertical shooter

A vertical shooter is likely to carry less text than a story game, but it still has menus, upgrade descriptions, mode explanations and store copy. A localisation partner would also review whether the anime-inspired presentation suits each market. Because Hardcore's appeal depends on players understanding exactly what each upgrade does, precise translation of the upgrade tree matters more than it might seem.

## Its relationship to Space Galaxy Attack Arcade

The two games share a name but are **separate games**. The app's page on this site positions Hardcore around a **harder difficulty curve**, while the Arcade edition centres on four abilities and readable patterns. A buyer could consider them as a pair, and Reign Creative is open to discussing either combination.

## A worked example: scoping a new sector

A publisher wants a new sector for a seasonal update. Scope would cover new enemy formations, at least one miniboss and a multi-phase boss with destructible modules, matching the existing sectors. If each sector in the plan needs three formation sets, one miniboss and one boss, the new sector adds five pieces of combat content to test across three modes: fifteen mode-and-content combinations to balance before release. That kind of estimate shapes whether co-development or a licence makes more sense, and it is worked out together with the publisher's own production team rather than assumed from outside.

## The transfer, practically

A purchase completes through Google Play's **app transfer**. Google's Play Console help asks both accounts to be registered and active and to supply registration transaction IDs, and requires an active payments profile for a game with in-app products. The help also covers app signing: the buyer **can request a new upload key**, and there is a key-upgrade route for the app signing key for new installs. Users, ratings and reviews move with the game, while earnings reports stay with the seller.
