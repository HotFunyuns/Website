**Handball in soccer (association football) is not "any touch on the arm".** Under Law 12 of the IFAB Laws of the Game 2026/27, a player commits a handball offence by deliberately touching the ball with a hand or arm, by touching it with an arm that has made the body "unnaturally bigger", or by scoring in the opponents' goal directly from their hand or arm — or immediately after it touched them — even if the touch was accidental. The Law says in as many words: "Not every touch of a player's hand/arm with the ball is an offence."

The restart is a direct free kick, or a penalty kick if the offence happens inside the offender's own penalty area. Whether a card follows is a separate question with its own answers. Everything below follows the [current Law 12](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/fouls-and-misconduct/), in force since 1 July 2026.

## Four questions a referee works through

### 1. Was it the arm at all?

For handball purposes, the upper boundary of the arm is in line with the bottom of the armpit. The shoulder is therefore not part of the arm; the IFAB's own guidance in Law 12 says a goal scored off the shoulder stands, whether the use of the shoulder was deliberate or accidental. The IFAB confirmed in 2021 that the same armpit line is used when judging offside positions — [how the offside rule works](/blog/how-the-offside-rule-works/) covers that side.

### 2. Was the touch deliberate?

The Law's example is "moving the hand/arm towards the ball". A deliberate handball is an offence wherever it happens on the field, and the restart is a direct free kick even if nothing else came of it — a defender who deliberately palms a ball to stop it going out for a throw-in, with no opponent near, concedes a free kick but no card.

### 3. Did the arm make the body unnaturally bigger?

This is the judgment test. A player has made their body unnaturally bigger when the position of the hand or arm is "not a consequence of, or justifiable by, the player's body movement for that specific situation". The Law adds that a player who holds an arm in such a position takes a risk of being hit by the ball and penalised. There is no fixed angle or distance; the referee asks whether the movement explains the arm.

### 4. Did the player score from it?

Even an accidental touch is an offence if the same player then scores in the opponents' goal directly from the hand or arm, or immediately after the ball touched it. That applies to goalkeepers too: under [Law 10](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/determining-the-outcome-of-a-match/), a goalkeeper who throws the ball directly into the opponents' goal concedes a goal kick rather than scoring.

## Common situations, decided

| Situation | Offence? | The reason in Law 12 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defender moves an arm towards a cross and blocks it | Yes | Deliberate touch |
| Defender's arm is outstretched away from the body when a shot strikes it, and the movement does not explain the position | Yes — a judgment call | Unnaturally bigger |
| Defender pulls the arm in towards the body to avoid the ball, which still clips it | No | Neither deliberate nor unnaturally bigger |
| Ball strikes the top of the shoulder | No | The arm starts below the armpit |
| Attacker's arm is accidentally hit, and a team-mate scores moments later | No | Only the toucher's own goal is penalised |
| Attacker's arm is accidentally hit and the same attacker scores immediately | Yes | "Even if accidental" |
| Goalkeeper handles inside their area after a team-mate's deliberate kick to them | Yes — indirect free kick | Goalkeeper handling restriction |
| Goalkeeper handles outside their penalty area | Yes — as for any player | Same restrictions as everyone else |

The third row comes directly from one of the IFAB's worked examples in Law 12: a defender who moved the arm towards their body to avoid the ball did not commit an offence, and play restarted with a corner kick.

## Direct, indirect or penalty?

Almost every handball offence is a **direct** free kick, which means a goal can be scored straight from it, or a **penalty kick** when it happens inside the offender's own penalty area. The exception is the goalkeeper. Inside their own penalty area, a goalkeeper may handle the ball, but not when it has been deliberately kicked to them by a team-mate or thrown to them directly from a team-mate's throw-in, unless they have clearly kicked or tried to kick it to release it into play, and not again after releasing it before another player touches it. Those goalkeeper offences are **indirect** free kicks, normally with no card. Outside the area, a goalkeeper is just another player.

## When a handball brings a card

| Handball that… | Card |
| --- | --- |
| Stops nothing in particular | None — just the free kick |
| Interferes with or stops a promising attack | Yellow, unless a penalty is awarded for a non-deliberate handball |
| Is an attempt to score, successful or not | Yellow |
| Denies a goal or an obvious goal-scoring opportunity, deliberately | Red, wherever it happens (goalkeeper in own area excepted) |
| Denies a goal or an obvious chance, non-deliberately, outside the own penalty area | Red |
| Denies a goal or an obvious chance, non-deliberately, inside the own penalty area, penalty awarded | Yellow |
| Would have denied a goal, but the referee plays advantage and the goal is scored | None (since 2026/27) |

An attacker who deliberately controls the ball with a hand in the opponents' penalty area concedes a direct free kick; the Law's guidance says no caution is needed unless the action was an attempt to score.

## Why the handball Law keeps changing

The short answer is the one the IFAB gave itself: consistency. The wording has been clarified repeatedly because referees, players and spectators kept reading it differently.

- **November 2018.** The IFAB's advisory panels held "a detailed and constructive discussion" on improving the handball Law, aiming for "a more consistent application", according to the [IFAB's report of the meeting](https://www.theifab.com/news/ifab-advisory-panels-make-progress-with-handball/).
- **March 2021, for 2021/22.** The 135th Annual General Meeting [clarified the Law](https://www.theifab.com/news/annual-general-meeting-2021/) because "the interpretation of handball incidents has not always been consistent". It confirmed that not every touch is an offence, that referees should keep using judgment about the arm's position relative to the player's movement, and that accidental handball leading to a team-mate's goal or chance would no longer be an offence. It also applied the armpit definition to offside.
- **March 2024, for 2024/25.** The 138th meeting decided that handball offences that are not deliberate, and for which penalties are awarded, [are treated in the same way as other fouls](https://www.theifab.com/news/the-ifab-approves-permanent-concussion-substitutes-among-several-changes-to-the-l/) for discipline — the source of the yellow-not-red row in the table above.
- **February 2026, for 2026/27.** After the IFAB's [140th Annual General Meeting](https://www.theifab.com/news/the-ifab-introduces-further-measures-to-improve-match-flow-and-player-behaviour/), the [2026/27 change log](https://www.theifab.com/law-changes/latest/) removed the caution after advantage when a denied goal is scored anyway, and deleted handling "in an unsuccessful attempt to prevent a goal" from the caution list, explaining that there is no caution when advantage is played after a player has unsuccessfully tried to stop a goal with a hand or arm.

The pattern is a trade-off between two kinds of rule. A purely mechanical rule — any contact is an offence — is easy to apply but penalises contact a player could not have avoided. A purely intent-based rule is fair in principle and impossible to apply consistently, because intent is invisible. The current Law sits in between: deliberate touches are offences, an arm that the movement cannot justify is a risk the player carries, and accidental touches only matter when they produce the toucher's own goal.

## What it changes for players

The "unnaturally bigger" test explains a habit anyone watching will recognise: defenders blocking shots and crosses with their arms tucked in or held behind the back. The Law makes an unjustified arm position the player's risk, and it treats an arm drawn in towards the body as no offence, so the safest arm is the one the movement explains. For attackers, the 2021 clarification means an accidental touch in a crowded penalty area does not cancel a team-mate's goal — only their own.

Handball is most expensive inside a team's own penalty area, where it becomes a penalty kick, and the players doing the blocking there are mostly defenders.
