Rugby union has been a professional sport only since **August 1995**, when, as World Rugby's history records, its governing body oversaw the advent of professionalism. The route from junior rugby to a paid contract differs between national unions, so this guide follows one in detail: England's, because the Rugby Football Union (RFU) publishes its structure. It then covers the rule that applies everywhere, World Rugby's eligibility regulation for national teams.

## England's pathway, stage by stage

| Age | Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 18 and under | Age-grade rugby | Club, school and college rugby, played to RFU Regulation 15 |
| 15–16 | Foundation phase | Regional academy sessions after nomination by a club or school |
| 17–18 | Development phase | A smaller group, Individual Development Plans and an annual U18 academy league |
| 19–20 | Confirmation phase | The last academy phase before the senior game |
| Senior | Professional contract | Premiership clubs, with England squads above them |

The RFU describes **age-grade rugby** as the game for all girls and boys aged 18 and under in clubs, schools, colleges and the representative pathway. The professional pathway grows out of it. The boys' pathway is governed by the Professional Game Partnership between the RFU and Premiership Rugby, and the RFU states plainly that the purpose of each academy is **to produce players for the Premiership and England**. The Men's Professional Game Partnership set out the three academy phases: Foundation (ages 15 to 16), Development (17 to 18) and Confirmation (19 to 20).

## Getting into an academy

Academies are regional. Their boundaries are aligned to the Premiership clubs, alongside a Yorkshire academy run by the RFU, and the RFU's criteria for which academy a player belongs to include the address of the rugby club they were registered with at under-13.

The RFU explains how players enter the Foundation phase:

1. **Nomination.** Players are usually nominated through their club or school.
2. **Showcase events.** Each academy runs events at which nominated players show their skills, and players are then told whether they have been invited in.
3. **Regional sessions.** Sessions run through the season, led by full-time academy staff and part-time coaches, with internal games and festivals at under-15 and some external games added at under-16.

Selection for the Development phase usually comes from the Foundation phase rather than open trials. But the RFU is emphatic that the door stays open: "development is non-linear, and players thrive at different points", so programmes accept new nominations at various times, aiming for **multiple entry and exit points**. A late developer is not ruled out at 15. The partnership also requires each academy to establish at least two state school or college partnerships.

## The girls' and women's pathway

The girls' route is organised differently. The RFU's Girls U18 Player Development Group is an England Rugby programme delivered in nine regional centres, each named after a Red Rose, an England player, from that region. Players are nominated mainly through their county or constituent body, are invited to an assessment usually in August or early September, and are expected to keep playing for their school, college or club. Each has an Individual Development Plan and completes athletic profiling at least twice a season, and players in the programme may be selected for the England under-18 squad.

At senior level, Wikipedia describes **Premiership Women's Rugby** as a semi-professional competition at the top of English women's club rugby, run by the RFU and contested by nine clubs. The full-time contracts sit with the national team: Wikipedia records that on 31 July 2026 the RFU awarded **full-time central contracts to 32 England senior players** for 2026–27, with six more on transition contracts.

## Contracts at the top of the men's game

For men, the academies feed the Premiership clubs. The Men's Professional Game Partnership, an eight-year agreement between the RFU, Premiership Rugby and the Rugby Players' Association, shapes the elite level above them:

- the England head coach can select **up to 25 players** into an Enhanced Elite Player Squad;
- the under-20 elite squad expands to **50 players**, with up to four England A matches a season drawn from the senior and under-20 squads;
- the parties committed to a salary cap formula from the 2025–26 season.

## Playing for a country

International eligibility follows World Rugby's **Regulation 8**. A player may represent the union of a country in which:

- they were born; or
- one parent or grandparent was born; or
- they have been registered exclusively with a union or rugby body for the **60 months** immediately before playing; or
- they have completed **10 years** of cumulative residence.

Since 1 August 2024, World Rugby explains, the 60-month route is shown through rugby registration with a union or club rather than unbroken residency. Playing for a union's senior fifteens team, its next senior fifteens team or its senior sevens team "captures" a player for that union, and the regulation's guidelines count a match towards capture only once the player has reached the age of majority, which they set at the 18th birthday.

**A worked example.** A player is born in Australia, has a father born in Tonga and moves to England at 17, registering with English clubs. They are eligible for Australia by birth and Tonga by parentage from the start, and for England once they have been registered there for 60 months, at 22. If they play for Australia's senior team at 21, Australia captures them. From 1 January 2022, World Rugby allows a single transfer: after at least three years away from international rugby, and with World Rugby's approval, they could switch to Tonga, which their father's birthplace links them to. They could not transfer to England, because a transfer requires a link by birth or by a parent or grandparent, not by registration or residence.
