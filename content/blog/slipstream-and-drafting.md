Watch any race on a long straight and you will see a car tucked in behind another, gaining with every metre, then pulling out to pass. That is **slipstreaming**, or **drafting**. Wikipedia defines drafting as an aerodynamic technique in which two moving objects align closely to exploit the lead object's slipstream and reduce the overall effect of drag. Wikipedia dates its discovery by stock car racers to the 1960 Daytona 500, when Junior Johnson used it to make up for a car that could not keep up with the others.

## The physics in three steps

1. **The lead car leaves a hole in the air.** Pushing through the air at speed creates a low-pressure wake behind it.
2. **The follower meets less resistance.** Wikipedia explains that the low-pressure wake reduces the aerodynamic resistance on the front of the trailing car, letting it pull closer at the same power.
3. **The pair can both go faster.** As the second car nears the first, it pushes high-pressure air forward, so less fast-moving air hits the lead car's rear spoiler. Wikipedia's summary: the result is less drag for both cars.

Step 3 is why cars in a pack, especially on ovals, can lap faster than a car running alone, and why rivals sometimes cooperate in the draft for a while before fighting.

## The tow on a straight

In circuit racing, the slipstream is a tool for one moment. Wikipedia describes the classic move: along a long straight, the following car uses the slipstream to close the gap, hoping to overtake under braking for the next corner, or to pass on the straight if it has a straight-line speed advantage.

Qualifying is a different contest, run against the clock under its own session rules; [how racing qualifying works](/blog/how-racing-qualifying-works/) covers it.

## Dirty air: the other side of the wake

The same disturbed air that helps on a straight hurts in corners. Wikipedia notes it is very difficult for cars to follow each other closely in fast corners, because the **dirty**, turbulent air coming off the lead car unbalances the trailing car as its aerodynamic devices provide less grip. Drafting also shifts balance: the leading car keeps normal front downforce but loses some at the rear, while the trailing car loses front downforce but keeps normal rear downforce.

There are practical side-effects too. Wikipedia warns that if the rear car runs too close, the air supply to its radiator is reduced and the engine can overheat. [Racing car setup basics](/blog/racing-car-setup-basics/) explains how downforce and drag trade off in a car's setup.

## Drafting on the ovals

Wikipedia notes drafting is most important at NASCAR's superspeedways, such as Talladega, Daytona and Atlanta, where restrictor plates or reduced power mean much less power is available to push the large cars through the air. Packs form, and the draft decides races. After Ryan Newman's crash in the 2020 Daytona 500, NASCAR changed its superspeedway package to reduce tandem drafting and closing speeds.

## Formula 1 in 2026: Overtake mode

Formula 1's 2026 regulations add an electrical boost to the aerodynamic one. Under Article B7 of the FIA's sporting regulations:

- each circuit has a **Detection Line** and an **Activation Line**, and the FIA publishes a **Detection Gap**;
- a car that crosses the detection line **less than the detection gap behind another car** has **Overtake** activated at the activation line, allowing extra electrical energy deployment;
- Overtake is **disabled** at the start or a restart until the leader crosses the detection line, while the safety car is deployed, and in low-grip conditions.

The same article governs the adjustable front and rear wings, which drivers may move between "corner mode" and "straight mode" positions in activation zones the FIA defines for each circuit, with only partial activation allowed in low-grip conditions.

## Using the draft well

- **Time the pull-out.** The classic pass Wikipedia describes is completed under braking for the next corner, so the move has to be made before that braking zone arrives.
- **Watch the temperatures.** Running too close cuts the air reaching the radiator, as Wikipedia notes.
- **Pick the straight.** The slipstream pass happens along a long straight, so the corner that leads onto one is where it is set up; [the racing line explained](/blog/racing-line-explained/) covers corner exits.
