**"Keto flu" is an informal term, not a diagnosis.** It describes a cluster of symptoms that some people report in the first days of substantially reducing carbohydrate intake — commonly fatigue, headache, irritability and difficulty concentrating.

The term is useful as shorthand and risky as a category, and the risk is the reason this article is worth reading rather than skipping.

## What people describe

The symptoms reported most often:

- Fatigue and low energy
- Headache
- Irritability
- Difficulty concentrating
- Light-headedness
- Muscle cramps
- Nausea

Reported duration varies widely, from a couple of days to a couple of weeks. Because the term is informal and the symptoms are non-specific, there is no reliable general figure, and anyone quoting a precise duration is going beyond what is known.

## The explanations usually offered

Two are common, and both are plausible rather than established as *the* cause of the whole cluster.

**Fluid and electrolyte shifts.** Reducing carbohydrate intake is associated with changes in how the body handles fluid and sodium. That shift is the standard explanation for the rapid early weight change on low-carb diets, and it is the mechanism most often invoked for the symptoms too. [Electrolytes on a low-carb diet](/blog/electrolytes-on-low-carb/) covers this in more detail.

**Fuel adaptation.** The body adjusting to deriving more energy from fat and ketones and less from carbohydrate. Clinical reviews of ketogenic diets, such as [The ketogenic diet: Pros and cons](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31805451/) in *Atherosclerosis*, discuss the physiological changes and the populations for whom caution is advised.

We are describing these as explanations that are offered, because that is what they are. The symptom cluster is self-reported and informally defined, which makes establishing causation for it as a whole difficult.

## The part that matters most

Here is why the informal label is risky.

**Fatigue, headache, difficulty concentrating and light-headedness are non-specific symptoms with many possible causes.** Some of those causes need medical attention.

If you have started a low-carb diet and you feel unwell, "it's just keto flu" is a hypothesis, not a finding. Treating it as a finding means you might not investigate something else.

Symptoms that should prompt medical attention rather than patience:

- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Confusion, fainting or severe light-headedness
- Unusual thirst combined with frequent urination
- Nausea and vomiting that persist
- Severe or worsening symptoms of any kind
- Any symptom that continues beyond the couple of weeks people describe

**Anyone with diabetes should be under medical supervision before making major dietary changes.** Diabetic ketoacidosis is a serious medical emergency that is distinct from nutritional ketosis, and the [CDC's page on diabetic ketoacidosis](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/about/diabetic-ketoacidosis.html) sets out its warning signs. [What is a ketone reading](/blog/what-is-a-ketone-reading/) covers the distinction between the two in more detail.

## Why we are not telling you how to prevent it

The approaches usually recommended involve changing sodium intake, fluid intake or both. Those interact directly with:

- Blood pressure medication
- Diuretics
- Kidney function
- Heart conditions

That makes "increase your salt" a genuinely unsafe piece of general advice, however common it is online. It is a reasonable conversation to have with a clinician who knows your history; it is not something an article should tell a stranger to do.

## What is reasonable to do yourself

**Keep a record.** What you ate, what you drank, how you felt, on which day. That record is far more useful to a clinician than a recollection, and it also lets you see whether a pattern is real.

**Change one thing at a time.** People frequently start a diet, an exercise programme and a fasting schedule in the same week and then cannot tell what caused what.

**Do not push through.** Persistent symptoms are information, not weakness.
