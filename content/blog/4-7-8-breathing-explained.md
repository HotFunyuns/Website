4-7-8 breathing is a slow breathing pattern with a long hold and an even longer exhale. Cleveland Clinic notes that it was popularised in 2015 by the integrative medicine specialist Dr Andrew Weil and has ancient roots in the yogic practice of *pranayama*. This guide follows Dr Weil's own patient handout, published by the Andrew Weil Center for Integrative Medicine at the University of Arizona, works out the timing and sets out what research has found.

## The four steps in Dr Weil's handout

Dr Weil's handout starts with posture and the tongue. Ideally, **sit with your back straight**, and place the **tip of your tongue against the ridge of tissue just behind your upper front teeth**, keeping it there throughout. You exhale through your mouth around your tongue; pursing your lips slightly can help.

1. **Exhale completely** through your mouth, making a whoosh sound.
2. **Inhale** quietly through your nose to a mental count of **4**.
3. **Hold** your breath for a count of **7**.
4. **Exhale** completely through your mouth, with a whoosh, to a count of **8**.

That is one breath. The handout repeats the cycle three more times, for **four breaths** in all. Cleveland Clinic adds that if you are using the technique to fall asleep, you can do it lying in bed.

## The ratio, not the clock

The handout is explicit: the absolute time you spend on each phase is not important; **the ratio of 4:7:8 is**. If you have trouble holding your breath, speed the whole exercise up but keep the ratio. With practice, you can slow it down.

**A worked example.** If one count is one second, a cycle lasts 4 + 7 + 8 = **19 seconds**, so four cycles take 76 seconds, just over a minute. That is about 60 ÷ 19 ≈ **3.2 breaths a minute**, against the 12 to 15 a minute Wikipedia gives as typical for a resting adult. If the hold feels too long, count faster, for example at two counts a second: the cycle drops to 9.5 seconds, still in the 4:7:8 ratio, and four cycles take 38 seconds. Either way, the exhale is twice as long as the inhale.

## How often, and how many

Dr Weil's handout suggests practising **at least twice a day**. It also sets a limit for beginners: **no more than four breaths at one time for the first month**; later, if you wish, you can extend to eight. Cleveland Clinic's specialist suggests three cycles twice a day, and attaching practice to fixed moments, such as waking up or going to bed, to build the habit.

## Lightheadedness and other cautions

Both sources mention that you may feel a little lightheaded at first. Dr Weil's handout says not to be concerned because it will pass; Cleveland Clinic attributes it, along with any shortness of breath, to learning to breathe more slowly. If the feeling does not pass, or you feel dizzy or uncomfortable, stop and breathe normally, and keep to the handout's four-breath limit. A breathing exercise is a relaxation practice, not a treatment for insomnia, anxiety or any other condition: if you have a breathing condition, or sleep or anxiety problems persist, speak to a health professional.

## What the 2022 Physiological Reports study measured

One study of the technique, published in *Physiological Reports* in 2022, tested it on **43 healthy people aged 19 to 25**, some of them sleep-deprived. Participants did six cycles per set for three sets, with a minute of normal breathing between sets. Straight afterwards, **heart rate and systolic blood pressure fell** in both groups, and measures of heart rate variability changed, particularly in those who were not sleep-deprived.

That is a small study of immediate effects in young, healthy adults. It did not measure sleep, so it does not show that 4-7-8 breathing helps you fall asleep, even though Cleveland Clinic's article presents it as a method for sleep and relaxation. Broader reviews of slow breathing are summarised in [slow breathing and relaxation](/blog/slow-breathing-and-relaxation/).

## 4-7-8 compared with box breathing

| | 4-7-8 | Box breathing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counts | In 4, hold 7, out 8 | In 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4 |
| Cycle at one count a second | 19 seconds | 16 seconds |
| Emphasis | A long exhale | Equal phases |
| Exhale | Through the mouth, with a whoosh | Gentle, without straining |
