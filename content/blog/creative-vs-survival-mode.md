Minecraft and Block World RPG both offer the same choice: creative or survival? The answer changes almost everything about how the game plays. This guide explains what each mode does, where the split came from and how to choose, using Wikipedia's articles on sandbox games, survival games and Minecraft, which Wikipedia calls the most successful sandbox game.

## Two modes, one world

Wikipedia's glossary of video game terms describes **creative mode** as a mode that turns a game with a construction mechanic into a kind of virtual toy, stripping away restrictions such as progression-gated content and dangers such as enemies or hazards. **Survival mode** is the opposite: the challenge is in staying alive while gathering what you build with.

| | Survival | Creative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building materials | Gathered from the world | Available without limit |
| Crafting | Needed to make tools and items | Largely unnecessary |
| Danger | Enemies and hazards that cost health | Usually none |
| Movement | On foot | Flight, in Minecraft and Block World RPG |
| Main question | "Can I last and progress?" | "What can I make?" |

Minecraft shows the split clearly. In its survival mode, Wikipedia explains, players gather resources such as wood and stone to craft blocks and items, monsters spawn in darker areas depending on difficulty, and a health bar and a hunger bar must be managed. In creative mode, players have access to an infinite number of almost all items, can place or mine them instantly, can fly, and usually take no damage and feel no hunger.

## Where creative mode came from

Creative mode began as a response to how people played. Wikipedia's article on sandbox games describes Minecraft at its core as a survival game in which players collect resources to build tools and shelters, but with no limits on what they build, so that it has been compared to digital Lego bricks. Players' use of the game that way led the developers to add a dedicated creative mode that removed the survival elements so they could build without hazards or artificial limits. Wikipedia's Minecraft article dates it to Beta 1.8, released on 14 September 2011.

## The variations in between

Survival is not all-or-nothing. Minecraft has two settings that change its difficulty:

- **Peaceful**: in Minecraft, the hunger bar no longer needs refilling on peaceful difficulty, and monsters' appearance depends on the difficulty setting. Peaceful-style settings keep gathering and crafting but lower the pressure.
- **Hardcore**: Wikipedia describes Minecraft's hardcore mode as locking the difficulty to hard and adding permadeath; after dying, players must delete the world or continue as a spectator.

## A worked example: one hut, two modes

Imagine a simple hut with a 5 × 5 floor plan and walls three blocks high. Each layer of wall uses the 16 blocks around the edge of the square, so three layers take 48 blocks. A flat roof covering the 5 × 5 square takes 25 more. Leave a two-block doorway and the total is **71 blocks**.

In creative mode, those 71 blocks cost nothing: you pick them from the inventory and place them, flying to reach the roof. In survival, every one of them has to be gathered first, often with tools you must craft, while managing whatever the world throws at you. The same hut is a quick build in one mode and a gathering project in the other. Neither is better; they are different games.

## Which should you start with?

1. **To learn building**, start in creative. Nothing interrupts you, so you can practise shapes, materials and scale.
2. **To learn the game's systems**, start in a peaceful survival world. You learn gathering and crafting, which creative skips, without combat.
3. **For the full challenge**, play regular survival, where Wikipedia notes the open-ended goal is usually to last as long as possible.

If the game allows more than one save, you can keep one world of each.
