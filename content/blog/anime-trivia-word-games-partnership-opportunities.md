A quiz night works best with questions at several difficulty levels and formats that keep a room involved, and [Anime Trivia & Word Games](/apps/anime-trivia-word-games/) offers what its Google Play listing calls thousands of questions across multiple game modes. This page is for **fan events, clubs and publishers**, describing the app, ideas that fit, the independence rule, and how a sale would be handled.

## Eight ways to answer

The listing describes questions on **characters and villains, powers and abilities, organisations, story arcs, locations, lore and memorable scenes**, with content inspired by a wide range of anime genres. How players answer changes with the mode:

| Mode | What it asks |
| --- | --- |
| Multiple choice | Pick the right answer |
| Timed challenge | Answer against the clock |
| Survival | Keep going as rounds get harder until a slip |
| Fill in the blank | Supply a missing word |
| Matching | Pair related characters and concepts |
| Ordering | Put events in sequence |
| Word puzzles | Anime-themed word challenges |
| Party guessing | Group-friendly guessing games |

**Points, streaks, statistics and achievements** track progress, and **daily challenges** refresh each day. The app is free with ads and optional in-app purchases.

## Independence is the starting point

The app's page on this site states plainly that this is **an independent fan quiz game**, **not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to any anime studio, publisher or rights holder**. Every partnership keeps that clear. Event material would not imply official status, use studio logos or key art, or suggest endorsement. If a **rights holder** wanted an official quiz, that would be a separate, licensed collaboration negotiated on its own terms.

## Conventions and fan events

For an event that wants an activity that works for a crowd, ideas include:

- **a sponsored daily challenge** during a convention weekend;
- **quiz-night packs** built on the party-style and survival modes;
- **cross-promotion** between the event's programme and the app.

## Designing for a mixed crowd

The listing says **new fans can learn more about popular series**, while **longtime viewers can test how much they truly remember**. That matters at an event, where a room may hold both. A host can open with multiple choice and matching, which reward recognition, move to ordering and fill-in-the-blank for the middle of the night, and finish with survival, which gets harder each round.

## Clubs and societies

For university anime societies and local clubs that run quizzes, a partnership could provide **co-branded question rounds** for club nights or a simple guide to running a survival-mode tournament. Clubs need nothing from Reign Creative to play the free app; partnerships add structure and promotion.

## Quiz publishers

Publishers of quiz books or trivia games could explore **publishing and distribution**, **localisation** of questions and interface into other languages, **co-development** of new modes, **licensing**, and **white-label versions where technically feasible**. Localising trivia is more than translation: references and difficulty need checking for each audience, so publishers with editorial teams are well placed.

## A worked example: a convention survival bracket

A convention runs a survival tournament for 32 players. Players are drawn into eight groups of four; each group plays a survival round, and the top player from each goes through, leaving eight. Two more rounds of four-player groups produce two finalists, then a head-to-head final decides the winner. That is 8 + 2 + 1 = 11 rounds in total. A partnership could supply the bracket sheet and branded screens; the convention runs the room.

## Selling the trivia game

Reign Creative LLC is open to serious discussions about **acquiring this app individually, with the other anime and creative apps, or with the portfolio**. This page does not publish **prices, valuations, revenue or user numbers**, and it is not an offer.

## The Play Console transfer

A sale would be completed on Google Play through its **app transfer process**. Google's help notes that both developer accounts need to be registered and active, that each provides a registration transaction ID, and that apps with in-app products need an active payments profile on the receiving account. Users, statistics, ratings and reviews move with the app; **orders made before the transfer** and the seller's **earnings reports** do not. Ad integrations are updated afterwards.
