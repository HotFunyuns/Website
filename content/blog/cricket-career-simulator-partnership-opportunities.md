Cricket Sport Career Simulator is new territory for Reign Creative LLC: the app's page on this site describes it as the company's first cricket game and the first app in the catalogue built around the sport. That makes partners with cricket knowledge more valuable than usual. We would like to talk with cricket clubs and academies, and with distributors in South Asian and Commonwealth markets, about publishing, localisation, co-development, cross-promotion or acquisition.

## A player's career, not a franchise

According to the Google Play listing, you create one cricketer, improve their skills and progress through matches, with the player rather than a team at the centre. The store screenshots fill in the detail. Player creation covers look, role and style, overall rating and potential, strengths to develop, or a randomised player. Matches combine interactive decisions with a cricket field view, fast simulation, live match stats, and energy, morale and momentum.

Skills cover batting technique and scoring, pace and spin bowling, fielding and wicket-keeping, athleticism and cricket IQ. Around the matches sit a career command centre, coach and team choices, daily career goals, rewards and recognition, and rivals. The game is free with ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases. The listing describes no online multiplayer. The player guide is the [Cricket Sport Career Simulator guide](/blog/cricket-career-simulator-guide/); the [app page](/apps/cricket-career-simulator/) has store details.

## Confirm the format first

One limitation noted on the app's page matters more to cricket partners than any other: the listing does not say which formats — Test, one-day or T20 — the matches follow, and it recommends checking in the app if you want a specific one. A club, academy or distributor planning content around a particular format should raise this on the first call. The site's [guide to cricket formats](/blog/cricket-formats-explained/) explains why the distinction matters so much in the sport.

## Clubs and academies: roles from opener to spinner

The app page's audience list includes players who like developing a role, "from opening batter to spin bowler". For a club or academy, role is a natural theme for engagement with juniors; the site's explainer on [cricket player roles](/blog/cricket-player-roles/) covers the real positions. Partnership ideas:

- **A club-branded edition**, where technically feasible, with the club's identity in place of the game's.
- **Co-developed career scenarios**, written with coaches, for example around the jump from club to representative cricket.
- **Junior challenges** built on daily career goals and rivals.

We make no claim that the game develops real cricketers.

A worked example a coach might use with the live stats: batting strike rate is runs ÷ balls faced × 100, as the site's [cricket stats explainer](/blog/cricket-stats-explained/) shows, so a simulated innings of 45 runs from 30 balls has a strike rate of 45 ÷ 30 × 100 = 150. Comparing that figure across simulated innings is a simple discussion starter.

## Distributors in cricket-playing markets

A distributor brings regional store presence, marketing and language. Because the listing does not describe language options, localisation would be a scoped project covering menus, skill names, coaching text and store copy. The skill names alone — pace and spin bowling, wicket-keeping, cricket IQ — show how much of the game's text is cricket vocabulary, so translators who follow the sport would make the work easier to review. A distributor could also advise whether the game's player-first framing, which the app page pitches at "people looking for a cricket game built around a career rather than a team", should lead the store copy in its region. Publishing, distribution and bundling are all open for discussion, with territories and terms agreed in writing.

## Things to settle before any deal

- **Formats** used in matches (see above).
- **Branding**: the game carries no board, league or team marks; a board or league that wanted an official edition would need to license its own identity for it, and building that edition would be new work.
- **Scope of new work**: anything not in the listing, such as multiplayer, would be new development.

We do not publish prices, valuations, revenue or player numbers.

## The Play transfer, if it comes to that

Google's [help page on transferring apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) covers a sale. Both accounts must be active, the seller supplies the target's registration transaction ID with the first part of the order ID removed, and the target developer approves the request. The listing, users and reviews move across. With in-app products, the buyer needs a payments profile; with ads, ad SDK integrations must be updated. Google adds that registered package names and their keys related to Android developer verification are transferred too.
