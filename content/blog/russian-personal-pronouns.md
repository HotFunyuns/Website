Russian pronouns change form for every case, so "I", "me", "to me" and "with me" are four different words: *я*, *меня́*, *мне* and *мной*. That sounds like a lot until you see the table, where many cells repeat and two small rules explain most of the surprises. This reference gives the full table from Wikipedia's articles on Russian grammar and Russian declension, then the н- rule, the special preposition forms, the reflexive *себя* and formal *Вы*.

## The full table

| Case | I | you (sg.) | he / it | she | we | you (pl./formal) | they | -self |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | я | ты | он / оно́ | она́ | мы | вы | они́ | — |
| Accusative | меня́ | тебя́ | его́ | её | нас | вас | их | себя́ |
| Genitive | меня́ | тебя́ | его́ | её | нас | вас | их | себя́ |
| Dative | мне | тебе́ | ему́ | ей | нам | вам | им | себе́ |
| Instrumental | мной | тобо́й | им | ей | на́ми | ва́ми | и́ми | собо́й |
| Prepositional | мне | тебе́ | нём | ней | нас | вас | них | себе́ |

The masculine *он* and neuter *оно́* share every form after the nominative. Wikipedia also lists longer instrumental variants: *мно́ю*, *тобо́ю*, *е́ю* and *собо́ю*.

Some patterns are worth seeing at once:

- **Accusative = genitive** for every pronoun: *меня, тебя, его, её, нас, вас, их*.
- **Dative = prepositional** for *я*, *ты* and *себя*: *мне, тебе, себе*.
- **Нас and вас** cover accusative, genitive and prepositional.

If the cases themselves are new, [Russian cases explained](/blog/russian-cases-explained-for-beginners/) sets out what each one does.

## The н- after prepositions

Wikipedia's Russian grammar article states the rule: **when a preposition is used directly before a third-person pronoun, н- is added to the front**. So "him" is *его*, but "at him" is *у него́*, and "from her" is *с неё*.

| Without preposition | With preposition |
| --- | --- |
| *его* (him) | *у него*, *для него* |
| *ей* (to her) | *к ней* |
| *им* (by him / to them) | *с ним* (with him) |
| *их* (them) | *без них* |

Because the prepositional case is only ever used after a preposition, Wikipedia adds, the **third-person prepositional always starts with н-**: *о нём, о ней, о них*. That is why the table shows *нём, ней, них* in that row.

## Со мной, обо мне and other special forms

Before the first-person singular, some prepositions grow a vowel. Wikipedia lists:

- *со мной*, "with me" (normally *с*)
- *ко мне*, "to me" (normally *к*)
- *во мне*, "in me" (normally *в*)
- *обо мне*, "about me" (normally *о*)

It notes that these preposition forms are unstressed, so the stress stays on the pronoun.

Two more points come from the same source. The г in *его* and *него* is **pronounced в**, as in adjective endings: *ево*, *нево*. And English "it" has two translations: *оно́*, the neuter pronoun, and *э́то*, "this", used as a placeholder subject, as in *э́то хорошо́*, "it's good", and *кто э́то?*, "who is it?".

## Себя, the reflexive

*Себя́* is the reflexive "-self" pronoun, and a single column in the table serves every person: myself, yourself, herself, themselves. Wikipedia's Russian grammar table gives it no nominative form. Its forms mirror *ты*: *себя, себе, собой, себе*, against *тебя, тебе, тобой, тебе*. So "I bought it for myself", "she bought it for herself" and "they bought it for themselves" all use the one form *себе́*.

## Choosing the right form in five sentences

Work out each pronoun from the table and the rules above.

1. "My name is Pyotr." Wiktionary explains that *меня́ зову́т* means literally "they call me", so *me* is the accusative: **Меня́ зову́т Пётр.**
2. "What is your name?" Formal: **Как вас зову́т?** Informal: **Как тебя́ зову́т?** Both accusative.
3. "I'm going with him." *С* + instrumental *им*, plus the н- rule → **с ним**.
4. "We're talking about her." *О* + prepositional *ней* → **о ней**.
5. "Tell me." Dative of *я* → **скажи́ мне**.

Count the forms for *я* in the table: *я, меня, мне, мной*, which is 4 distinct words across 6 cases, because two pairs repeat. *Ты* has the same shape: *ты, тебя, тебе, тобой*, also 4.

## Formal Вы

Russian has a T–V distinction: the respectful singular "you" is the same as the plural, *вы*. Wikipedia notes it is **capitalised as Вы, Вас, Вам** in personal letters and official papers, where the addressee is definite, and in questionnaires, where the addressee is indefinite. Elsewhere it is written in lower case. Which situations call for *вы* in speech is covered in [Russian greetings](/blog/russian-greetings/).
