When a one-day or T20 international finishes with the scores level, the ICC's playing conditions do not settle for a tie. Under the October 2026 conditions, both formats send the teams to a **Super Over**: a one-over shootout. The procedure is set out in Appendix F of the ICC's T20I playing conditions, and the ODI conditions apply the same approach.

## The basics

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Length | Each team faces one over of six balls |
| Wickets | Losing two wickets ends that team's over |
| Winner | The team with more runs, regardless of wickets lost |
| Start | Normally five minutes after the match ends |
| Batting order | The team that batted second in the match bats first |
| Gap between the two overs | Five minutes |
| Players | Only players nominated for the match, including concussion replacements |

Umpires stay at the same ends as at the end of the match, the Super Over uses the match pitch unless the umpires decide otherwise, and penalty time being served in the match carries forward.

## Ball, end and field

The procedure fixes some details that matter on the night:

- **The ball.** The fielding captain chooses a ball from the box of spares, which includes the balls used in the match but no new ones. The side fielding second may use the same ball or choose another from the box.
- **The end.** The fielding side chooses which end to bowl from.
- **The field.** The fielding restrictions are those for the last over of an uninterrupted match, so up to five fielders may be outside the circle; [powerplays in cricket](/blog/powerplays-in-cricket/) explains the circle.
- **Reviews.** Each team gets one unsuccessful player review per innings in each Super Over, regardless of reviews used during the match; [how DRS works](/blog/how-drs-works-in-cricket/) covers what counts as unsuccessful.

## When the Super Over is tied too

If both teams score the same in the Super Over, another is played, and the procedure allows an unlimited number until there is a winner. For each repeat:

1. The team that batted second in the previous Super Over bats first.
2. Each team keeps the ball it used before.
3. The fielding side bowls from the opposite end to the one it used previously.
4. Any batter dismissed in an earlier Super Over cannot bat again.
5. Any bowler who bowled in the previous Super Over cannot bowl in the next.

Rules 4 and 5 make each repeat harder to plan: a captain who uses the best death bowler first may not have them for the next one.

## When there is no time

Time is not unlimited in practice. The procedure allocates extra time: the greater of the time left before the match's latest permitted finish, or 20 minutes. If delays use it up, the Super Overs are abandoned and the match is declared a **tie**. Where unavoidable constraints, such as floodlights having to go off at a set time, rule out several Super Overs, the match referee can cap the number before the first one starts.

The appendix gives a worked case: a match due to finish at 10.20 pm with 60 minutes of extra time ends tied at 10.35 pm. The Super Over starts at 10.40 pm with 45 minutes available. An interruption from 10.50 to 11.00 pm leaves 35 minutes; if a second Super Over is then interrupted at 11.15 pm and play cannot resume within those 35 minutes, the match is a tie.

## From bowl-outs to boundary count-back to repeats

Wikipedia traces the history. Super Overs were first used in T20 cricket in 2008, replacing the bowl-out. The 2019 Cricket World Cup final became the first ODI decided by one, and when England and New Zealand also tied the Super Over, England won on a boundary count-back. Wikipedia notes the ICC was criticised by many former cricketers and fans for that tie-breaker, and in October 2019 the ICC replaced it with repeated Super Overs.

## A worked example

Team A batted second in the match, so it bats first and scores 15 for 1 off six balls. Team B needs 16 to win. It reaches 15 for 2 with one ball left: two wickets end its over, so it is all out on 15. The Super Over is tied, and a second one follows: Team B bats first this time, without its two dismissed batters, and Team A, without the batter it lost, and both sides must use different bowlers from the first Super Over.
