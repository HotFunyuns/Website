**A throw-in restarts play when the whole ball crosses a touchline; a goal kick or a corner kick when it crosses a goal line without a goal — a goal kick if an attacker touched it last, a corner if a defender did.** In soccer (association football), those three restarts, plus the kick-off and the dropped ball, are set out in Laws 8, 15, 16 and 17 of the IFAB Laws of the Game. The 2026/27 edition, in force since 1 July 2026, added a five-second countdown to throw-ins and goal kicks, so a deliberate delay can now hand the restart to the other team.

## The five restarts side by side

| | Throw-in | Goal kick | Corner kick | Kick-off | Dropped ball |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Given when | Whole ball crosses a touchline | Whole ball crosses the goal line, last touched by an attacker, no goal | Whole ball crosses the goal line, last touched by a defender, no goal | Start of each half and of extra time; after a goal | Referee stops play and no other restart applies |
| Taken by | Opponents of the last player to touch it | Defending team | Attacking team | Coin toss decides the first; after a goal, the team that conceded | One player chosen by the Law (see below) |
| Taken from | Where the ball left the field | Anywhere in the goal area | The nearer corner area | The centre mark | Where the ball was, or where it struck an official |
| Ball in play when | It enters the field | Kicked and clearly moves | Kicked and clearly moves | Kicked and clearly moves | It touches the ground |
| Opponents must be | 2 m (2 yds) away | Outside the penalty area | 9.15 m (10 yds) from the corner arc | In their own half, 9.15 m away | At least 4 m (4.5 yds) away (everyone) |
| Score directly? | No | Yes, against the opponents only | Yes, against the opponents only | Yes, against the opponents only | No — two players must touch it |
| Exempt from offside? | Yes | Yes | Yes | — | No |

Two rules apply to every kicked restart: the ball must be stationary, and the taker may not touch it again until another player has. A second touch gives an indirect free kick — or a direct free kick if it is a handball, and a penalty kick if that handball is inside the taker's own penalty area, unless the taker was the goalkeeper.

## Throw-ins (Law 15)

At the moment of delivery, [Law 15](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-throw-in/) requires the thrower to:

- stand facing the field;
- have part of each foot on the touchline or on the ground outside it;
- throw the ball with both hands from behind and over the head, from the point where it left the field.

The ball is in play once it enters the field. If it hits the ground before entering, the same team retakes the throw; if the throw is taken incorrectly in any other way, the opponents take it instead. A thrower may deliberately throw the ball at an opponent to play it again, as long as it is not careless, reckless or excessive. An opponent who distracts or impedes the thrower, including by coming closer than 2 m (2 yds), is cautioned.

A goal cannot come straight from a throw-in: into the opponents' goal it is a goal kick, into the thrower's own goal a corner kick. And because Law 11 exempts throw-ins from offside, a player can receive a throw standing anywhere.

### The 2026/27 countdown

If the referee judges that a team is deliberately delaying its own throw-in, the [countdown protocol](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/throw-in-and-goal-kick-countdown-protocol/) applies: whistle, a signal for the throw to be taken, and a visible five-second count with a raised hand. If the ball is not in play at zero, the throw-in is awarded to the opponents from the same spot. The count can start before anyone has the ball — slow retrieval or an incorrect position is enough — and a player already in the act of throwing as it ends should not be penalised. The offender is cautioned only if they then excessively delay the opponents' throw.

## Goal kicks (Law 16)

Any player of the defending team may take a goal kick, from any point within the goal area, with the ball stationary. It is in play as soon as it is kicked and clearly moves, so a team-mate can receive it inside the penalty area. Opponents must stay outside the penalty area until the ball is in play; if some had no time to leave, play continues, but the kick is retaken if one of them touches or challenges for the ball first.

A goal kick can score directly, but only against the opponents. If it somehow goes straight into the kicker's own goal, the opponents get a corner kick.

The 2026/27 countdown applies here too, with a sharper sanction: a goal kick deliberately delayed beyond the five seconds becomes a **corner kick** to the opponents, on the side nearest where the goal kick was to be taken.

## Corner kicks (Law 17)

A corner kick is placed in the corner area nearest where the ball crossed the goal line, and taken by the attacking team with the flagpost left in place. It is in play when kicked and clearly moving — it does not need to leave the corner area — and opponents must keep 9.15 m (10 yds) from the corner arc until then. A goal can be scored directly, against the opponents only.

The corner has picked up two new triggers in two seasons:

1. **2025/26:** a goalkeeper who controls the ball in their hands inside their penalty area for more than eight seconds concedes a corner kick, placed in the corner area nearest the goalkeeper's position.
2. **2026/27:** a team that deliberately delays its own goal kick past the five-second countdown concedes a corner kick.

Both are listed in the current Law 17 introduction and in the [2026/27 change log](https://www.theifab.com/law-changes/latest/).

## Kick-offs and dropped balls (Law 8)

A kick-off starts each half, each half of extra time and play after a goal; after a goal, the team that conceded takes it. Every player except the taker must be in their own half, opponents 9.15 m from the ball, and the ball stationary on the centre mark. A goal may be scored directly from a kick-off, against the opponents only.

The dropped ball is the restart for any stoppage that does not call for another one — an injury, an outside agent, a ball that hits a match official and changes possession or starts a promising attack. Under [Law 8](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-start-and-restart-of-play/):

- if play stopped with the ball inside the penalty area, it is dropped for the defending goalkeeper;
- otherwise it is dropped for one player of the team that would have retained or gained possession — including, since 2026/27, the team that would have taken the restart had the ball gone out — or, if that cannot be judged, the team that last touched it;
- if the ball struck a match official or outside agent, it is dropped where that contact happened;
- everyone else stays at least 4 m (4.5 yds) away, and the ball is in play when it touches the ground.

A dropped ball cannot produce a goal until at least two players have touched it: straight into the opponents' goal is a goal kick, into the team's own goal a corner kick.

## Common restart mistakes

- **Throwing from the wrong spot.** The throw must be taken from where the ball left the field; a throw from elsewhere is not taken correctly, so it goes to the opponents.
- **Assuming a goal kick must leave the box.** It is live as soon as it is kicked and clearly moves.
- **Expecting offside from a corner.** There is none from the corner itself; it can apply once the ball has been played on.
- **Crowding the thrower.** Two metres is the minimum, and encroaching is a caution.
- **Treating the countdown as a caution.** The first punishment is losing the restart; the yellow card is only for excessive delay after that.
