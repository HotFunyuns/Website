"Play the shot, not the swing" is the tagline of Golf Career Simulator Pro Tour, and it is also the reason golf clubs, academies, golf media and publishers might find the game worth a conversation. Reign Creative LLC built a golf career game about decisions — which line, which club, how much risk — rather than meters and timing. We are open to brand partnerships, licensing, publishing, co-development and acquisition.

## Course management as the core mechanic

The Google Play listing says each shot is a choice made from the hole, the lie, the distance, the wind, the course conditions and your golfer's strengths. Aggressive and conservative lines are both viable; the right one depends on the golfer and the state of the tournament. Momentum, confidence, fatigue and pressure shift within an event, so a strong front nine guarantees nothing.

Around the shots is a tour schedule. Multi-round events with cuts, leaderboards, ties and playoffs decide finishes, and finishes decide rankings, earnings, sponsorship and next season's events. Your golfer develops seven skills — driving, accuracy, irons, short game, putting, composure and consistency — with training deciding the profile. The game is free with ads and offers optional in-app purchases. Every tour, event and golfer is fictional.

The player guide is the [Golf Career Simulator Pro Tour guide](/blog/golf-career-sim-guide/), and the app page is [/apps/golf-career-simulator/](/apps/golf-career-simulator/).

## Golf clubs and academies

Course management is about decisions rather than mechanics: when to lay up, how to play to a yardage. The site's explainers on [course management basics](/blog/golf-course-management-basics/) and [club distance gapping](/blog/club-distance-gapping/) show the real-world material. The game puts a version of those choices into a career, which could make it a member-engagement tool or a talking point between lessons.

Ideas we would discuss:

- **A club-branded edition**, with the club's name and colours in place of fictional branding, where technically feasible.
- **Co-developed scenarios**, such as signature holes designed with an academy, built as new content.
- **Member challenges**, using existing tournaments and leaderboards.

We make no claim the game improves anyone's real scores.

A worked example for an academy programme: one skill per week across the seven listed skills fills a seven-week block; running two sessions a week makes 7 × 2 = 14 sessions, after which members could enter a simulated tour event and compare finishes. Any real programme would be planned with the academy.

## Golf media and the tour season

For golf media, the game's tour structure is the storyline: cuts, ties and playoffs (which the site covers in [golf playoffs and tie-breaks](/blog/golf-playoffs-and-tie-breaks/)) create natural drama. A newsletter could follow a simulated golfer's season; a channel could run decision-based challenges. Sponsored events or branded content would be new work, agreed in writing, and would need to suit the game's audience.

## A publisher's view of a golf title

The app page's audience list includes "sports game players looking for something outside the major team sports", golf fans who prefer course management to swing meters, and players who want a long career arc. For a publisher, that is a distinct position in a sports slate. Publishing in new regions, distribution, localisation, licensing and acquisition are all open.

## Fictional tours and real brands

Because every tour, event and golfer is invented, the game carries no professional tour's marks and no player likenesses. A golf brand could appear only in ways agreed with us, and using a real tour or player would need that rights holder's licence. We do not publish prices, revenue or player numbers.

## Selling a golf game through Play Console

Google's [transfer help page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) governs the move. The app's users, statistics, ratings and reviews, content ratings and listing travel with it. Reports do not, so the seller exports what is needed first. Because the game has in-app products, the buyer needs an active payments profile; a different default currency means the app is unpublished until prices are verified and it is republished. Ad integrations need updating, Firebase projects would be unlinked and relinked if used, and test groups are rebuilt in the new account. The request goes to the target developer for review and approval, and Google says its support team then reviews and replies within two business days. What the sale covers outside Google Play — source code, course art, any handover support — would be written into the agreement rather than assumed.
