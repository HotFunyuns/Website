Anywhere on the course except a penalty area, you may declare your ball unplayable. Nobody else gets a say. For one penalty stroke you then choose one of three options: replay from where you made the previous stroke, drop back on a line from the hole through the ball, or drop within two club-lengths of the ball, no nearer the hole. In a bunker there is a fourth option, a drop on that line **outside** the bunker, which costs two penalty strokes in total.

That is Rule 19 of the 2023 Rules of Golf, the edition still in force on 2 October 2026; The R&A has said the next edition will be published on 1 January 2028.

## Who decides, and where you cannot

Rule 19.1 is short and absolute: the player is the only person who may decide to treat their ball as unplayable. There is no test of how bad the lie must be. A ball sitting cleanly in light rough may be declared unplayable if you think the options beat playing it, although you will rarely want to.

The one restriction is location. Unplayable relief is allowed anywhere on the course **except in a penalty area**. A ball wedged against a tree root inside red stakes has only the penalty-area options under Rule 17.

## The options side by side

| Option | Measured from | Where the ball goes | Must you know the ball's spot? | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stroke and distance (19.2a) | Where you made the previous stroke | Tee anywhere in the teeing area, or drop within one club-length of the previous spot | No | One stroke |
| Back-on-the-line (19.2b) | The spot of the ball | Drop on a line from the hole through that spot, any distance back | Yes | One stroke |
| Lateral relief (19.2c) | The spot of the ball, or the ground directly below it | Drop within two club-lengths, no nearer the hole | Yes | One stroke |
| Bunker extra option (19.3b) | The spot of the ball in the bunker | Back-on-the-line, outside the bunker | Yes | Two strokes in total |

The "must you know the spot" column is the detail most golfers miss. Stroke and distance can be taken even if the original ball has not been found and identified. The two dropping options need the ball's spot, so you must find it first.

Both dropping options let the ball come to rest in any area of the course, which cuts both ways. If a bunker lies within your two club-lengths, a ball dropped into it stays in it: where more than one area of the course falls inside the relief area, the ball must come to rest in the same area it first touched when dropped. Aim your drop at the ground you want.

## Three places where the details matter

### A ball lodged in a tree or bush

For lateral relief, the reference point for a ball above the ground is the spot **directly below it** on the ground. Measure two club-lengths from there, no nearer the hole. You still need to know where the ball is, which in practice means identifying your ball in the branches; if you cannot, stroke and distance is the only unplayable option left.

### A ball in a bunker

Rule 19.3 keeps the bunker in play for the one-stroke options. Back-on-the-line and lateral drops must come to rest **in the bunker**, and stroke and distance sends you back to your previous spot. The extra option, back-on-the-line relief outside the bunker, costs two strokes in total. It is the escape route from the lie every golfer dreads: a ball plugged under a steep face where any one-stroke drop would leave much the same shot. For players who use a wheeled mobility device, Rule 25 reduces that extra option to one penalty stroke.

### A ball on or just off the green

Rule 19.2 covers a ball on the putting green as well as one in the general area, so the same three options exist even on the green, although they are rarely worth using there. The fringe around a green is general area, not putting green, so a ball resting against something awkward just off the green has the same three options too.

## When an unplayable saves strokes

The penalty is a known cost: one stroke. Playing the ball as it lies has an uncertain one. So the useful comparison is not "penalty versus no penalty" but **one stroke and a clean next shot** against **the recovery swing and whatever it realistically leaves**.

Take an example with stated assumptions. Your ball is under a bush about 120 yards from the green, with the edge of the fairway a couple of yards to one side and trees on the other.

- **Play it:** you judge that the best realistic recovery is a short punch back out to the fairway, leaving roughly 130 yards from a clean lie. That has cost you a stroke.
- **Lateral relief:** for one penalty stroke, you drop within two club-lengths of the ball, no nearer the hole, which in this example reaches the edge of the fairway at about 120 yards.

Both routes spend one stroke to reach a clean lie, and the unplayable leaves the shorter shot. The recovery also carries a risk the penalty does not: if the punch catches a branch and stays in the bush, you have spent a stroke and gained nothing. The general principle is worth remembering: **a recovery that can only reach a clean lie costs the same stroke as the penalty, with extra risk attached.**

The calculation flips when playing it as it lies has a realistic chance of doing more than reaching a clean lie, such as advancing to the green. Then the penalty is buying less than the swing could.

Back-on-the-line has a hidden strength here. Because the Rule sets no limit on how far back you may go, you can choose to drop at a distance you play well rather than accept whatever yardage a lateral drop gives you.

## Unplayable or free relief?

Golfers sometimes look for free relief to escape a bad lie. The Rules close that route. Under Rule 16.1a(3) there is no free relief from an abnormal course condition, such as a sprinkler head beside the bush, when playing the ball as it lies is clearly unreasonable because of something else, such as the bush itself. In that position, the unplayable Rule is the way out.

The reverse also applies. When your ball is outside a penalty area and a no play zone interferes with your stance or swing, the Rules require you to take relief rather than play, and unplayable relief is one of the permitted routes.

## Mistakes that add a second penalty

Every one of these is playing from a wrong place, which costs the general penalty of two strokes in stroke play on top of the stroke you have already paid:

- **Dropping nearer the hole** than the ball's spot, or than the spot below it for a ball in a tree.
- **Measuring two club-lengths from the wrong point**, usually from where you would like to drop rather than from the ball.
- **Taking lateral relief outside a bunker.** One-stroke lateral and back-on-the-line drops must come to rest in the bunker; the only drop outside it is back on the line, for two strokes in total.
- **Playing a ball that rolled out of the relief area.** If a correctly dropped ball comes to rest outside the relief area, drop again; if it happens twice, place a ball where the second drop first touched the ground (Rule 14.3c).

And one that is simply not allowed: declaring a ball unplayable in a penalty area.
