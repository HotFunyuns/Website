Malay question words are short, and several of them are built from the same root. *Apa*, "what", turns up inside *siapa* "who", *berapa* "how many" and *mengapa* "why". Learn that family plus *mana*, *bila* and *bagaimana* and you can ask almost anything. This reference gives each word with an example sentence from Wiktionary, then shows how *-kah* and word order shape a question. Spellings are standard Malaysian Malay.

## The question words at a glance

| Malay | English | Example from Wiktionary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| apa | what | *Apa warna kegemaran kamu?* "What is your favourite colour?" |
| siapa | who, whose | *Siapa itu?* "Who is that?" |
| di mana | where (at) | *Di mana awak tinggal?* "Where do you live?" |
| ke mana | where to | *Awak hendak pergi ke mana?* "Where do you want to go?" |
| bila | when | *Bila kau pergi sekolah?* "When did you go to school?" |
| mengapa, kenapa | why | *Kenapa kau begitu degil?* "Why are you so stubborn?" |
| bagaimana | how | *Bagaimana cara memasak lauk ini?* "How do you cook this dish?" |
| berapa | how many, how much | *Berapa orang terlibat dalam persembahan?* "How many people are involved in the play?" |
| yang mana | which | *Baju yang mana dipakai Michael Jackson?* "Which shirt was worn by Michael Jackson?" |

## Apa and the words built from it

Wiktionary lists **apa** as an interrogative pronoun, with **apakah** as its formal interrogative form. It also lists the words formed from it, among them *siapa* "who", *berapa* "how much", *mengapa* "why" and *apabila* "when". Seeing the family helps: *berapa* is *ber-* plus *apa*, and *mengapa* is *meng-* plus *apa*, the same prefixes covered in our [Malay affixes guide](/blog/malay-affixes-guide/).

On its own, *apa* is also an interjection. Wiktionary notes that a bare *Apa?* for "pardon?" is often considered impolite, and that *Maaf?* is preferred.

## Siapa for people and names

**Siapa** asks "who" or "whose": *Ini baju siapa?*, "Whose shirt is this?". English speakers should note its second use. Malay asks for a name with *siapa*, not *apa*: *Siapa nama kamu?*, literally "who is your name?", means "What is your name?". Choose the "you" in that sentence with care; [Malay pronouns](/blog/malay-pronouns/) explains why *kamu* is familiar and *anda* polite.

## Mana: where, which, and the preposition it needs

**Mana** carries two meanings. As "where", Wiktionary says it is incomplete without a preposition:

- **di mana**, "where, at what place": *Adam di mana?*, "Where is Adam?"
- **ke mana**, "where to": *Awak hendak pergi ke mana?*
- **dari mana**, "where from".

As "which", *mana* is optionally used with *yang*: *Baju (yang) mana satu pilihan hati?*, "Which shirt is the one you choose?". Wiktionary also records **macam mana** as an everyday "how", a synonym of *bagaimana*. One caution from the same source: using *di mana* and *yang mana* as relative words, in the sense of English "where" and "which" in the middle of a sentence, is widespread but proscribed by Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka's official grammar.

## Bila, mengapa, kenapa and bagaimana

**Bila** is "when" as a question. Wiktionary notes that *bila* is also extremely common as a conjunction, but that Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka prescribes *apabila* for that use instead.

For "why", **mengapa** is formal and **kenapa** informal. Wiktionary lists *apa hal* and *apa pasal* as further synonyms of *mengapa*.

**Bagaimana** means "how", both "in what way" and "in what state": *Bagaimana keadaan pesakit itu?*, "How is the patient's condition?".

## Berapa for numbers, prices and the time

**Berapa** asks about amount, value, time and measurement, according to Wiktionary. Its examples show how much ground one word covers:

- *Sekarang pukul berapa?*, "What time is it?", literally "now, how many o'clock?"
- *Adam umur berapa?*, "How old is Adam?"

For the answers, see [Malay numbers](/blog/malay-numbers/).

## A worked example: three ways to ask one question

Take a simple statement and turn it into questions with the tools above:

1. **Statement**: *Awak lapar.* "You are hungry."
2. **Yes-no question with -kah**: *Adakah awak lapar?* "Are you hungry?" Wiktionary describes *-kah* as used on modal verbs and adverbs in questions, here on *ada*.
3. **Emphatic question word**: *Berapa ramaikah orang dalam keluarga awak?* "How many people are there in your family?" Here *-kah* attaches to the question phrase for emphasis. Wiktionary adds that *-kah* is usually only used in formal or literary speech or writing.

Word order is flexible too. Wiktionary's examples put *di mana* first in *Di mana awak tinggal?* and last in *Adam di mana?*. Wikipedia's Malay grammar article notes more generally that the focused element of a clause is usually placed at the beginning, followed by a slight pause.
