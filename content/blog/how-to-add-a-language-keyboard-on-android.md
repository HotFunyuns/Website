Reading a new script is one skill; typing it is another. Vietnamese is written with tone marks, Thai, Khmer and Lao use their own scripts, and Cantonese has to be entered as characters. On Android, Google's Gboard keyboard handles all of these once the right language is added. This guide follows Google's Gboard help pages; menu names can differ slightly between devices and Android versions, and Google notes that some steps work only on Android 7.0 and up.

## Before you start: Gboard and your Android version

Google's setup page says that on some Android devices Gboard is already the default keyboard, and on others you install it from Google Play. It recommends checking for updates so you have the most recent version, and warns that some steps might not work on Android 8 (Go edition). If your phone uses a different keyboard made by its manufacturer, the menus will look different; the steps below are for Gboard.

One reassurance first. Google's help states that you can change your keyboard language **without changing your device's language**, and that adding languages in Gboard does not affect your Android language settings. Your phone's menus stay in whatever language they already use.

## Adding a language from the keyboard itself

This is the quickest route, as Google describes it:

1. Open any app you can type in, such as Gmail or Keep.
2. Tap where you can enter text, so the keyboard appears.
3. At the top of the keyboard, tap **Settings**. (Google's setup page describes the same place as **Open features menu**, then **More**, then **Settings**.)
4. Tap **Languages**, then **Add keyboard**.
5. Pick the language you want.
6. Pick the layout you want to use.
7. Tap **Done**.

## Adding it through the Android Settings app

If you prefer the system menus, Google gives a second route: open the **Settings** app, tap **System**, then **Keyboard** and **On-screen keyboard**, then **Gboard** and **Languages**. Pick a language, turn on the layout you want, and tap **Done**.

## Which Southeast and East Asian languages are listed

Gboard's supported-language list on Google's setup page includes each of these languages, some in several forms:

| Language | How Gboard lists it |
| --- | --- |
| Vietnamese | Vietnamese |
| Thai | Thai |
| Khmer | Khmer (and separately Khmer Surin) |
| Lao | Lao; Lao, Transliteration |
| Malay | Malay (Malaysia), Latin; Malay (Malaysia), Arabic; plus Brunei and Singapore entries |
| Cantonese | Chinese, Cantonese, Traditional |
| Burmese | Burmese |

The Cantonese entry uses traditional characters, and Wikipedia's article on Jyutping lists Gboard among the tools that support Jyutping input on Android and iOS; [Jyutping explained](/blog/jyutping-explained/) covers how that romanisation works. For Malay, choose a Latin entry unless you specifically want to type Malay in Arabic script.

## A worked example: a two-language setup for a Vietnamese learner

Say you study Vietnamese and still write messages in English. Add **Vietnamese** as above, keep **English**, and stop there: two active languages. That matches the advice on Google's troubleshooting page, which suggests sticking to one or two active languages for smoother typing.

Now count the Malay options in the table: Malaysia Latin, Malaysia Arabic, Brunei Latin, Brunei Arabic and Singapore Latin make five entries for one language. If you later add Malay as well, picking exactly one of those five rather than several "just in case" keeps you at three active languages instead of seven, and Google's one-or-two advice is a reason to add that third only when you need it.

## Switching languages while you type

Google gives two methods:

- **Touch and hold the space bar** to switch languages.
- **Use the language switch key**: in the bottom left of the keyboard, touch and hold the comma, drag your finger to **Settings**, tap **Preferences**, and turn on **Show language switch key**. If that option is greyed out, Google's tip is to turn off **Show emoji switch key** first.

## When typing feels slow or keys go wrong

Google's "Fix problems with Gboard" page suggests, in order: restart the device; update Gboard from Google Play; clear Gboard's cache in **Settings > Apps > Gboard > Storage and cache**, and if that fails clear its data, which resets Gboard to its default state; or force-stop and reopen it. For lag and incorrect key presses it also suggests turning off **Personalization** and adjusting text correction, and repeats the advice to keep to one or two active languages.

If the problem is that text in a script **displays** as boxes or broken shapes, that is a font issue rather than a keyboard one; [Khmer fonts on Android](/blog/khmer-fonts-on-android/) explains why Khmer is especially prone to it.
