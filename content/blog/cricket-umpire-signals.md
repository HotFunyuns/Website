Umpires talk to the scorers, the players and the crowd with their arms. The code of signals is set out in Law 2.13 of the Laws of Cricket, in MCC's 2026 edition, and the ICC's international playing conditions add a few of their own. Knowing them lets you follow a match without commentary.

## Signals while the ball is in play

Law 2.13 lists four signals an umpire can make before the ball is dead:

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| One arm extended horizontally | No ball |
| Both arms extended horizontally | Wide |
| Index finger raised above the head | Out (if not out, the umpire simply calls "Not out") |
| Wrists crossed and re-crossed below the waist | Dead ball |

When the ball is dead, the bowler's end umpire repeats the no ball, wide and dead ball signals to the scorers. The out signal is not repeated.

## Signals to the scorers once the ball is dead

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Arm waved from side to side, finishing across the chest | Boundary 4 |
| Both arms raised above the head | Boundary 6 |
| Open hand raised above the head | Bye |
| Hand touching a raised knee | Leg bye |
| One arm bent upwards, fingertips touching the nearer shoulder | Short run |
| Repeated tapping of one shoulder with the opposite hand | Five penalty runs to the batting side |
| One hand placed on the opposite shoulder | Five penalty runs to the fielding side |
| Ball held above the head | New ball |
| Pointing to a raised wrist with the other hand | Start of the last hour |
| Both shoulders touched, each with the opposite hand | Revoke last signal |

Two pairs are easy to confuse. The **bye** and **leg bye** signals separate runs that pass the batter from runs off the body; [how cricket scoring works](/blog/how-cricket-scoring-works/) explains when each is allowed. The two **penalty-run** signals differ only in whether the hand taps the shoulder repeatedly (batting side) or rests on it (fielding side).

## Who signals, and the scorers' acknowledgement

The Laws assign the dead-ball scoring signals to the bowler's end umpire, with one exception: short run is signalled by the umpire at the end where it happened, though the bowler's end umpire gives the final signal and tells the scorers how many runs to record.

Two procedures keep the scorebook accurate:

- **Acknowledgement.** The umpire waits until a scorer has acknowledged each signal before play continues.
- **Order.** If several signals are needed, they are given in the order the events occurred. A no ball that is then hit for four is signalled no ball first, then four.

## A worked sequence

In a one-day international, the bowler oversteps and the batter hits the ball over the boundary on the full:

1. Under the ICC's ODI playing conditions, the third umpire reviews replays of the bowler's feet landing and advises the bowler's end umpire, who immediately calls and signals **no ball**, one arm out.
2. Once the ball is dead, the umpire repeats the no ball signal to the scorers, then signals **six**, both arms above the head, waiting for each to be acknowledged.
3. Under the ICC's playing conditions, the umpire then signals a **free hit** for the next ball, the arm raised and circled.

The scorers record seven runs: the one-run no ball penalty, charged to the bowler, and six credited to the batter.

## Conduct signals

The Laws also give two-part signals for serious offences under Law 42, Players' conduct. For Level 3 and Level 4 offences, the first part is the same, one arm out to the side and repeatedly raised and lowered; the second part distinguishes the level: both hands raised to shoulder height with fingers spread for Level 3, and an index finger held at shoulder height for Level 4.

## Extra signals in international cricket

The ICC's ODI playing conditions add:

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| After the no ball signal, one arm raised straight up and moved in a circle | Free hit |
| An arm rotated in a large circle | Powerplay over |
| The shape of a TV screen drawn with the hands | Referral to the third umpire, or a player review under way |

A player asks for a review with a "T" made with both forearms at head height. If a review overturns a decision, the umpire makes the revoke last signal before giving the new one; [how DRS works](/blog/how-drs-works-in-cricket/) covers the process, and [no ball and wide rules](/blog/no-ball-and-wide-rules/) explains the free hit.
