A franchise game gives you one team. [Basketball League Sandbox](/apps/basketball-league-sandbox/) gives you the league. Its Google Play listing describes a simulator for people who want to shape a whole competition: its rules, finances, schedule and future, with a sandbox for asking "what if?" on top. This guide explains how it works and links to the guides that explain the real-world systems it models.

## Commissioner Mode

In Commissioner Mode you run the office: league rules, team finances, schedule fairness and the league's future. The store screenshots show a League Office screen with season progress and the next game, beside labels for a season calendar, league history and league expansion. [What a sports commissioner does](/blog/what-does-a-sports-commissioner-do/) describes the real job.

## Sandbox Mode

Sandbox Mode is for experiments. The listing names **scenario presets**, a **trigger builder** and **what-if experiments**, and the start screen shows "chaos tools and editors". A sandbox is the place to test an idea, such as a rule change or a new structure, and watch the league respond, without the pressure of running a credible league. [League sandbox versus franchise GM](/blog/league-sandbox-vs-franchise-gm/) explains how the two styles of game differ.

## Watching the season

Seasons move forward through simulation, with a calendar of upcoming games, standings, power rankings, and player and team stats. When you want to see a game, **replays** come with:

- **shot maps**, showing where shots came from;
- **momentum charts**, showing which team controlled each stretch;
- adjustable playback speeds, and a jump to the **fourth quarter** or the **final two minutes**.

Shot maps and box scores reward a little statistical literacy; [basketball advanced stats explained](/blog/basketball-advanced-stats-explained/) covers the efficiency measures behind them.

## The offseason

Between seasons, an **18-step offseason** handles roster changes and player development. Players have contracts, health and relationships; teams have chemistry, coaching, rivalries and finances. [How the NBA offseason works](/blog/how-the-nba-offseason-works/) walks through the real calendar, and [how draft lotteries work](/blog/how-draft-lotteries-work/) explains one of its most watched steps.

## A bigger world

The world can extend past one competition. The listing describes **connected leagues**, transfers and loans, **promotion and relegation**, national teams and a **tournament creator**. History is preserved, from championship runs and upsets to draft classes and franchise achievements, and a timeline and newsroom track the stories. Leagues start from real-player rosters; once expansion exhausts the available pool, fictional players fill the spots.

## Thinking like a commissioner

The interesting decisions in a league simulator are the ones with trade-offs. Could a rule that speeds up play suit some rosters more than others? Would looser spending rules widen the gap between the richest and poorest teams in your league? Does a fairer schedule mean more travel for someone? Does promotion and relegation make the bottom of the table matter more, and at what cost to the clubs that go down? The game's value is that you can make one of those choices and then watch a season or two unfold to see who wins and who loses from it. Changing one thing at a time makes it easier to tell which change caused what you see.

## A sensible first league

1. **Start in Commissioner Mode** with the real-player rosters a new league starts from, and simulate a few weeks.
2. **Watch one replay with the shot map** to see how the engine plays.
3. **Run your first full offseason** before changing rules.
4. **Use Sandbox Mode for the wild ideas**, and keep a separate save for them.
5. **Read the newsroom and timeline** to follow the league's stories.

## Limits worth knowing

- There is no hands-on gameplay; games are simulated and watched as replays.
- Simulated outcomes are the game's own and are not predictions of any real season.
- The depth of options takes time to learn compared with a quick draft game.

The game's ratings and results are its own, and it is not affiliated with, endorsed by or licensed by any league, team, players' association or athlete.
