[Football League Commissioner](/apps/football-league-commissioner/) puts you in the league office rather than on the field or the sideline. Its Google Play listing describes an American football simulation built around running the whole league: customising rules, exploring expansion and relocation, and giving your football world its own identity, while simulation shows how those decisions play out. This guide explains how it works and links to the guides that explain the real jobs it borrows from.

## The commissioner's office

The store screenshots show what the job involves. The **Commissioner's Office** displays league health, with scores for owners, players and fans, a commissioner agenda and a Simulate Week button. An **operations menu** lists a schedule creator, special events, transaction approvals, league economics, and expansion, relocation and realignment. Other screens show owners and media deals, inbox proposals, votes and crises, and salary-cap settings.

The screens put owners, players and fans side by side as separate scores. [What a sports commissioner does](/blog/what-does-a-sports-commissioner-do/) describes the real role, and [how sports schedules are made](/blog/how-sports-schedules-are-made/) explains the scheduling puzzle behind the schedule creator.

## Shaping the league

Commissioner decisions fall into a few kinds:

- **Rules.** Customise how the game is played and see the effect in simulated results.
- **Structure.** Expansion adds teams, relocation moves them, and realignment redraws divisions. Real expansions need a way to stock new rosters, which [expansion drafts explained](/blog/expansion-drafts-explained/) covers.
- **Economics.** Salary-cap settings and media deals shape how teams spend; [how the NFL salary cap works](/blog/how-does-the-nfl-salary-cap-work/) explains the real system.
- **Approvals.** Transaction approvals keep teams' moves under the league's oversight.

## Simulating a game, a week, a season or the playoffs

You decide how much to watch. The listing describes simulating **a single game, a week, a season or the playoffs**, then checking standings, schedules and stats. **Observer mode** lets you follow favourite players and teams, explore league storylines and find games to watch.

## The Yearbook, records and multiple saves

History is kept as you go. A **Yearbook**, timelines, records and awards turn results into a league with a memory, and **multiple saves** let you keep separate football worlds: a realistic one and a wild experiment, for example.

## Sandbox and Unlimited Draft

Two modes sit beside the commissioner's job:

1. **Sandbox mode** is for experiments: the listing calls it sandbox freedom to experiment with the competition.
2. **Unlimited Draft** is a separate roster-building mode, in which you choose players for a team and weigh the balance of your selections.

## Testing one decision across several seasons

The listing pairs the commissioner's choices with two ways of reading their effect: simulation, which "reveals how it develops", and history, which "helps you understand what changed along the way". That suggests a simple method. Note the owner, player and fan scores on the Commissioner's Office screen, make one change (a rule, an expansion, a salary-cap setting), simulate a season or two, then compare the scores, the standings and the Yearbook with where you started. Because multiple saves are supported, you can run the same league with and without the change and compare the two histories. The results are the simulation's own, so they show how this game's league responds, not how a real league would.

## A first season as commissioner

1. **Simulate a week before changing anything** to see how the office screens respond.
2. **Note the league-health scores** before making big changes, so you have a baseline.
3. **Change one thing at a time** so you can see its effect.
4. **Use observer mode** to follow a few teams through the season.
5. **Save before experiments**, or try them in a second save.

## What the simulation does not do

- There is no on-field gameplay; results come from simulation.
- Simulated results are the game's own outcomes, not predictions of any real season.
- Ads support the free version; Google Play lists no in-app purchases.

According to the app's own screenshots, the league uses real players from across football's eras, with ratings and results that are the simulation's own estimates; the game is not affiliated with, endorsed by or licensed by any league, team, players' association or athlete.
