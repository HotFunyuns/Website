[Jellyfish Arena Survivor io](/apps/jellyfish-arena-survivor-io/) offers io-style competition, tap, grow, try to reach the top, against AI rivals, so a run never waits for a lobby. This page is for **casual publishers** and **cross-promotion partners**, covering the game, ideas that fit, an important honesty rule, and the sale route.

## Small to big in one run

The listing describes an **eat-and-grow arena game** with the shape of an io title and the movement of a snake game. Players start small, collect **glowing food** to grow longer and stronger, and stay clear of anything bigger. The rules, the app's page on this site says, **explain themselves in about ten seconds**, which keeps runs repeatable. Steering is **one-finger, with momentum**, so cutting off a larger rival or escaping one depends on the line taken.

Each run fills the arena with **up to 100 AI-controlled rivals**, rising to **up to 200 with Premium**. A **live leaderboard** tracks the ten largest, with a **crown** for first place, and a **minimap** shows food, danger and the current leader. **Pearls** earned from runs unlock **jellyfish skins**. The game is free with ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases.

## The honesty rule

The most important line in the listing for any partner: **the rivals are AI-controlled, not real players**. The game plays like a multiplayer arena but is a **solo experience**. That is a strength, runs start instantly and work without other people, but it means marketing must never call the game online multiplayer, show fake player counts or imply real-time competition with strangers.

## Casual publishers

A publisher building a casual catalogue might value three things the app's page on this site describes: a run that **starts the moment you tap**, a **five-minute** play shape with a leaderboard, and arena competition **without real-time multiplayer**, so there is no lobby or matchmaking for a publisher to run. The audience that page names, io players who want instant runs, eat-and-grow and snake fans, and people after a short game with a leaderboard, is the profile to compare against a publisher's own.

## The crown as an event hook

The live leaderboard and its crown give promotions a simple, honest goal: "hold the crown". Because the field is AI-controlled, an event can be framed as a personal challenge, such as reaching first place in a themed arena, rather than a contest against other people. That keeps the marketing truthful and avoids prize mechanics, which would bring a different set of rules into play.

## A worked example: a skin collaboration

A brand proposes three themed skins for a summer campaign. Players earn pearls in normal runs and spend them on the skins, so the collaboration adds no new paywall. If a player earns enough pearls for one skin across a handful of runs, three skins give a reason to come back several times over the summer. The skins carry a collaboration label, the brand approves the artwork, and the agreement covers how long they stay available. Nothing about the AI rivals changes, and no marketing suggests players compete against real people.

## Moving the game on Google Play

Google Play's **app transfer process** handles a sale. Its help explains that both developer accounts must be active and identified by registration transaction IDs. Because the game sells a Premium upgrade, the new owner needs an **active payments profile**; and if that owner's account uses a different default currency, an app with in-app purchases is unpublished after the transfer until the new owner has verified the new prices and published it again. Ratings, reviews and users go with the game, earnings reports stay with the seller, and **ad SDK integrations are updated** afterwards.
