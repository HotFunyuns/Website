[Video Player All Formats PRO](/apps/regal-video-player/) is a focused player for local video files; the app's page on this site notes it was listed on Google Play as Regal Video Player until its recent rename, and the store description still uses that name. This page explains, for **device makers and media-tool companies**, what it plays, where integration and distribution ideas fit, and how licensing or acquisition talks would proceed.

## What the player handles

The listing covers files already on a device: downloaded films, saved clips, lessons, interviews and personal recordings.

| Area | Listed support |
| --- | --- |
| Containers | MP4, MKV, MOV, M4V, WebM, 3GP, FLV, TS, M2TS |
| Video codecs | H.264, H.265, VP8, VP9, AV1 |
| Audio codecs | AAC, MP3, FLAC, Ogg |
| Speed | 0.1x to 3.0x in 0.1x steps |
| Continuity | Resume position, recent videos, playback history, favourites |
| Controls | Gestures for seek, skip, volume and brightness; mute, rotate, screen lock |
| Extras | Picture-in-Picture on supported devices, sleep timer, repeat |

One sentence in the listing matters most to a hardware partner: **playback depends on the video container, audio and video codec, Android version, device hardware and available system decoders**. The player relies on the platform's decoders, so AV1 or H.265 support on a given model depends on that model.

The free version covers local playback with ads; an optional **Premium** purchase removes ads and unlocks extra themes, and the listing says monthly, yearly and lifetime options may be available through Google Play.

## Device makers

For a phone or tablet maker, the question is whether a dedicated player improves the out-of-box experience. Arrangements Reign Creative LLC would discuss:

- **preload distribution**, after testing on the target hardware;
- **a white-label build where technically feasible**, carrying the device brand;
- **localisation** of the interface for the markets a device ships to.

Because codec support follows the device's decoders, a sensible first step is a joint test on the target hardware with a set of sample files in each container.

## Media-tool companies

For companies building recording, editing or teaching tools, the player could sit at the end of a workflow. **Integrations supported by the app's existing capabilities** are one route, for example a hand-off that opens a user's exported file in the player and lets resume, speed control and Picture-in-Picture do the rest, if the app's current capabilities support it; anything more would be framed as new work. **Cross-promotion** between complementary apps is another, as are **co-development** of features both sides want, **licensing** and **sponsorship**.

## Where the product's limits sit

Partners should describe the player as the listing and the app's page on this site do:

- **It plays local files; it is not a streaming service.**
- **Picture-in-Picture depends on the device and Android version.**
- **No account is needed** for normal local playback, and recent videos, playback history, saved positions and favourites are **stored locally** on the device.

## A worked example: a lecture-capture partner

An education company records 90-minute lectures and exports MP4 files to students' tablets. A student watching at 1.5x finishes a lecture in 90 ÷ 1.5 = 60 minutes; at 1.2x, in 75 minutes. Resume keeps their place between sessions, and the sleep timer stops playback at the end of the file. A partnership might add an "open in player" hand-off and co-branded instructions; the education company keeps control of its content.

## Moving the app on Google Play

An ownership change happens through Google Play's **formal transfer process**. Because this app offers an optional Premium purchase through Google Play, Google's help notes that the buyer's account needs an **active payments profile**, and that **orders created before the transfer stay with the original account**, including for refunds. The new owner may request a **new upload key** if there is concern about reusing the existing one. Google's help also says that users, statistics, ratings, reviews and **subscriptions** related to the app are transferred with it, which matters for a paid tier like this one; promotions are not, though previously issued promo codes should still work.
