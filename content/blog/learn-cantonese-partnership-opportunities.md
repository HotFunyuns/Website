Learn Cantonese for Beginners begins from a clear premise, stated on its page on this site: Cantonese is treated as its own spoken language rather than a version of Mandarin, and that changes what the course teaches and in what order. Reign Creative LLC built the course around that idea and would like to discuss it with heritage-language schools, community organisations and other educational partners. This page sets out what the app teaches, where a partnership could fit, and how a conversation proceeds.

## Built on Cantonese terms

The Google Play listing describes a path of **12 units**, from greetings and introductions out to family, food, shopping, numbers, money, dates and time, transport, work, school, health, emergencies and social situations. Traditional Chinese characters and **Jyutping** appear together from the first lesson. Pronunciation has its own foundation — initials, finals, syllables, then tone contrasts — and grammar is taught on Cantonese terms: word order, pronouns, classifiers, possession, negation, aspect markers, questions, comparisons and sentence-final particles. The listing notes that characters come with notes on spoken Cantonese, written Cantonese and Standard Written Chinese, a distinction the site explores in [spoken versus written Cantonese](/blog/spoken-vs-written-cantonese/).

Where a device supports Cantonese speech, the app uses it and says clearly when it cannot. It does not teach Mandarin or simplified characters. The full learner's view is in the [Learn Cantonese beginners guide](/blog/learn-cantonese-beginners-guide/); the app page is [/apps/learn-cantonese/](/apps/learn-cantonese/).

## Why heritage-language schools are a natural fit

The listing names heritage learners reconnecting with a family language as one of its audiences, alongside complete beginners and travellers to Hong Kong, Macau or Guangdong. For a weekend or after-school programme whose learners hear Cantonese at home but cannot yet read it, a course that pairs characters with Jyutping and starts with the tone system — the subject of the site's [Cantonese tones explainer](/blog/cantonese-tones-explained/) — could sit beside a teacher's lessons as homework or revision. We would describe it as a support for teaching, not a substitute.

## Community organisations and the English direction

The app also runs in reverse. Cantonese speakers can study English, and the two directions keep separate progress and review queues so switching does not mix lessons. For a community organisation serving Cantonese-speaking adults, that second course may be the more relevant one. Partnership ideas include co-promoting the English direction, or co-developing community-specific content as new work.

## Design choices a teacher will notice

Several features matter in a classroom or programme setting:

- **No hearts or lives.** Nothing blocks a learner after a mistake.
- **Review built from history.** Daily Review, Speed Run Review, Mistake Rescue, Listening Blitz, Sentence Builder, Match Blitz and Dictation Mode all draw on covered material, and a missed-questions list collects persistent errors.
- **Progress signals.** XP, daily goals, streaks and achievements.

On monetisation, the app is free to download, ad-supported, offers optional in-app purchases, and some locked lessons can be opened with a rewarded ad. A school partnership would need to consider how that works for its students.

## What a school partnership could look like

A worked example: a school year of 36 teaching weeks could take the 12 units at a steady 36 ÷ 12 = 3 weeks per unit, leaving the review modes for homework between lessons. A term-length pilot of 12 weeks would cover four units at that pace. The actual shape would depend on the school.

Other models we are open to discussing: localisation into additional support languages with a partner who knows them, a branded edition for a programme where technically feasible, licensing of course content, sponsorship that suits learners, and acquisition of the app alone or with others.

## Limits we state up front

The app's page says the course builds a beginner foundation and that no app can promise fluency on its own. We do not claim native-speaker review or certification, and we do not publish user numbers, revenue or prices.

## Starting a Cantonese programme conversation

Use the [partnerships page](/partnerships/) or email ReignCreativeSupport@gmail.com. Tell us about your school or organisation and what you have in mind in a few sentences. Please do not include confidential, financial or personal information — including anything about students — in a first message. If there is a fit, a call follows. Every discussion is nonbinding; any arrangement depends on due diligence and a signed agreement.

## If the Cantonese course changed hands

A sale follows Google Play's [transfer process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). The listing, users, ratings and reviews move to the new account; earnings reports do not. Because the app has in-app products, the buyer's account needs an active payments profile, and ad SDKs must be updated to credit the new owner — relevant here, given the rewarded-ad unlocks.
