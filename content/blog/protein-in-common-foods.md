This list starts with staple foods that are not usually thought of as protein sources, such as rice, bread, pasta, potatoes and vegetables, and then covers beans, dairy, eggs, meat and fish. Every figure below is worked out from the US Department of Agriculture's **SR Legacy** database in FoodData Central, per the serving shown, so you can see what your usual meals already contribute.

## How the numbers were worked out

SR Legacy lists nutrients per 100 g of food, along with gram weights for common portions such as "1 cup" or "1 slice". Protein per serving is the per-100 g value multiplied by the portion weight. **Protein per 100 calories** divides protein by energy, a quick way to tell protein-dense foods from foods that simply contain some. All figures are per the serving shown, not per 100 g, except the per-100 g comparisons in the text. Branded products and home recipes will differ, so treat these as reference values.

## Grains and starchy foods

| Food | Serving | Protein | Calories | Protein per 100 kcal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White rice, long-grain, cooked | 1 cup (158 g) | 4.3 g | 205 | 2.1 g |
| Brown rice, long-grain, cooked | 1 cup (202 g) | 5.5 g | 248 | 2.2 g |
| Oats, dry | ½ cup (40.5 g) | 5.3 g | 153 | 3.5 g |
| Whole-wheat bread | 1 slice (32 g) | 4.0 g | 81 | 4.9 g |
| White bread | 1 slice (29 g) | 2.6 g | 77 | 3.3 g |
| Pasta, cooked (spaghetti, not packed) | 1 cup (124 g) | 7.2 g | 196 | 3.7 g |
| Quinoa, cooked | 1 cup (185 g) | 8.1 g | 222 | 3.7 g |
| Potato, baked with skin | 1 medium (173 g) | 4.3 g | 161 | 2.7 g |

Whole-wheat bread has about half as much protein again per slice as white bread. A cup of pasta has about 1.7 times the protein of a cup of white rice: pasta carries more than twice as much protein per gram (5.8 g against 2.7 g per 100 g), even though USDA's cup of pasta weighs less.

## Vegetables

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Green peas, cooked from frozen | ½ cup (80 g) | 4.1 g | 62 | 6.6 g |
| Sweetcorn, cooked from frozen | 1 cup (165 g) | 4.2 g | 134 | 3.1 g |
| Broccoli, raw | 1 cup chopped (91 g) | 2.6 g | 31 | 8.3 g |

Spinach shows why both columns matter: it is protein-dense per calorie but so light that a cup gives under a gram.

## A worked example: what staples add up to

Take a day built only from staples: two slices of whole-wheat bread (8.0 g), a cup of brown rice (5.5 g), a cup of pasta (7.2 g), a cup of raw broccoli (2.6 g), half a cup of peas (4.1 g) and a cup of 2% milk (8.1 g). That is about **35 g of protein** before any meat, fish, eggs, beans or tofu. For comparison, the FDA's Daily Value for protein, the reference used on food labels, is **50 g** a day. It is a labelling reference, not a personal target; [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day/) explains how reference intakes are set, and a health professional can advise on your own needs.

## Using these numbers

Two habits make a reference table useful. First, weigh or measure a serving once, because a "cup" of rice at home may not be USDA's 158 g. Second, log the staples too: as the worked example shows, they add up.
