## Choosing a machine

The listing says each of the 50 machines has its own visual style, symbols, reel animations and atmosphere. Themes range from **dragons, dinosaurs and outer space** to **the ocean, western adventures, animals, flowers, storms and treasure**. ## Volatility, in practice

Machines come in **low, medium and high volatility**. The listing describes low volatility as more frequent smaller virtual wins, medium as balanced play and high as the chance at larger virtual payouts; the app's page here puts it as high volatility paying larger virtual wins less often. With virtual coins, volatility shapes the feel of a session rather than anything of value: steady small wins, or long quiet stretches broken by big ones. [Slot volatility explained](/blog/slot-volatility-explained/) covers the mathematics.

## Bet size and session length

You choose a **virtual coin bet** before you spin (the listing says bets adjust across a wide range), and bet size decides how long a balance lasts. **A worked example:** with 10,000 virtual coins, bets of 100 give you 100 spins before any wins come back; bets of 250 give you 40. Wins extend a session, but on high-volatility machines they arrive less often, so the balance swings more, and larger bets make those swings bigger. If you want long, relaxed sessions, pair smaller bets with low volatility.

## A first-session plan

1. **Pick a theme you like.** With 50 machines, start with the setting that appeals most; you can try others later.
2. **Start on low volatility with a small bet.** You will see wins more often, which makes it easier to learn what the symbols and animations mean.
3. **Try a high-volatility machine next**, with the same small bet, and notice how different the rhythm feels.
4. **Watch the meters** as you spin, then collect the day's free coins before you stop.

That order teaches you how the machines differ without burning through your virtual balance on the first spin.
