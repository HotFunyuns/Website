Android offers two buttons that sound alike and do very different things: **Clear cache** and **Clear storage**, sometimes labelled Clear data. One is a harmless clean-up; the other, in Google's words, permanently deletes all app data. This guide explains the difference, when to use each and the steps, using Google's Android and Pixel help.

## Cache versus storage

| | Clear cache | Clear storage (Clear data) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it deletes | Temporary files | All of the app's data |
| Accounts and settings | Kept | Removed |
| Saved content kept only on the device | Kept | Removed |
| Typical side effect | The app may be slower the next time it opens | Everything the app saved is erased |
| Undoable | Nothing to undo | No: the data is gone |

Google's help puts it plainly. Clearing cache **deletes temporary data**, and some apps may be slow the next time you open them. Clearing storage **permanently deletes all app data**, and Google suggests trying to delete the data within the app first.

## What the cache is for

Google's Pixel help describes the cache as temporary files, and its Android help warns that some apps may be slow the next time you open them after it is cleared. Google's help also makes the important point that clearing cache is a **short-term solution**: **over time, cached data will build up again**.

## When to clear the cache

Clear an app's cache when:

- the app is **behaving oddly**: showing stale content, failing to load images or crashing on one screen;
- you need a **quick bit of space** and the app's cache is large;
- an **update** has left the app misbehaving.

It is safe to try first, because it does not remove your data.

## When to clear storage

Clear storage only when clearing the cache has not helped and you accept starting again. Expect to sign in again and to lose anything the app kept only on the phone. Back up or export anything you want to keep first.

## Check before you clear storage

Pause before clearing storage if the app holds anything that exists only on your phone:

- **notes, drafts or journals** that are not synced to an account;
- **game progress** that is not linked to a cloud save;
- **offline downloads**, such as maps, music or videos saved inside the app, which you would have to download again;
- **app-specific settings** you spent time choosing, such as playback preferences or themes.

If in doubt, open the app's own settings and look for an export, backup or sync option first. Clearing storage cannot be undone, so a two-minute check is worth it.

## The steps

Google's Pixel help gives this path for Pixel phones. Its general Android help says settings can vary by phone and points you to your device manufacturer, so the menu names on another brand may differ:

1. Open the **Settings** app.
2. Tap **Apps**, then **See all apps**.
3. Tap the app.
4. Tap **Storage & cache**.
5. Tap **Clear cache**, or **Clear storage** (or **Clear data**) and confirm.

## A worked example: what each button costs a video player

Suppose a local video player starts misbehaving. Clearing its **cache** removes only temporary files, so the cost is, at most, a slower next start. Now suppose you clear its **storage** instead. [Video Player All Formats PRO](/apps/regal-video-player/), for example, keeps recent videos, playback history, saved positions and favourites **locally on your device**, so clearing its storage would erase where you stopped in each video and your favourites list. The app plays files already stored on your device, so the videos themselves are a separate matter from the app's data. Cache first, storage last.

## If neither fixes it

Google's troubleshooting steps for an installed app that is not working come in this order:

1. **Restart the phone**; Google suggests restarting after each step.
2. **Update Android and the app**; [updating apps on Android](/blog/how-to-update-apps-on-android/) covers how.
3. **Force stop** the app.
4. **Clear the cache**, then, if needed, **clear storage**.
5. **Uninstall and reinstall**.

Google notes that if problems continue after force-stopping or clearing data, you may need to **contact the app's developer**.
