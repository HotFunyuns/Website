Forms are where PDFs meet deadlines: a school permission slip, a job application, a benefits claim, a tenancy form. On a phone they can be quick or maddening, depending on what kind of PDF form you have been sent. This guide explains the two kinds and how to fill each one in on Android.

## Two kinds of PDF form

Before you start, find out which kind you have. Adobe's help for Acrobat notes that interactive forms have highlighted fields, such as text fields, checkboxes or radio buttons. So tap on a blank line: if a cursor appears or a box highlights, the form is **interactive**. If nothing happens, it is probably **flat**.

| | Interactive (fillable) form | Flat form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Fields built into the PDF | A page image or plain page, often a scan |
| How to fill it | Tap a field and type | Add your own text boxes and marks |
| Checkboxes | Tap to tick | Place a mark or an X |
| Where you may meet it | Forms built to be completed on screen | Scanned paper or "print and fill" forms |

Wikipedia explains the history behind interactive forms. **AcroForms**, introduced in PDF 1.2 and part of all later versions, use objects such as text boxes and radio buttons and can support submitting, resetting and importing form data. A second kind, Adobe's **XML Forms Architecture (XFA)**, arrived with PDF 1.5; Wikipedia notes XFA forms are not compatible with AcroForms and that XFA was **deprecated in PDF 2.0**. Wikipedia adds that **most PDF processors do not handle XFA content**, so if a form will not display properly in your app, ask the sender whether a standard version exists.

## Filling an interactive form

1. **Open the form** in a PDF app.
2. **Tap the first field** and type; some forms let you jump to the next field.
3. **Use the right control**: tap checkboxes and radio buttons, choose from drop-down lists.
4. **Watch the formats**: dates and phone numbers may need a particular pattern.
5. **Save a new copy** when finished.

Adobe's help for Acrobat describes the same core actions: adding text, checkmarks and signatures. Field controls and menus differ between apps and Android versions, so expect the labels to vary.

## Filling a flat or scanned form

Flat forms need more care, because you are placing text on a picture:

1. **Zoom in** on the first line.
2. **Add a text box** with the app's text tool and type your entry.
3. **Match the size**: choose a font size that fits the line without overlapping the printed text.
4. **Tick boxes** by placing an X or a checkmark centred in each one.
5. **Repeat line by line**, then zoom out to check the overall look.

OCR will not help here: it reads printed text but does not create fields; [OCR explained](/blog/ocr-explained/) covers what it does.

## A worked example: a two-page application

Page one asks for your name, address, date of birth and three yes/no questions; page two has a declaration and a signature line. On an interactive form, that is seven fields to tap plus a signature. On a flat scan, it is seven text boxes, three ticks and a signature, each positioned by hand. In both cases, finish **all** the fields first, save, then sign last; [signing a PDF on Android](/blog/how-to-sign-a-pdf-on-android/) covers that step.

## Forms with a Submit button

Some interactive forms include a **Submit** button. Wikipedia explains that an AcroForm "submit" action transmits the names and values of the form's fields to a specified web address. That can be convenient, but whether a phone app supports it depends on the app, and you may not see where the data is going. If the instructions also allow it, the safer route is to save the completed form and send it the way the organisation asks, such as by email or through its upload page, keeping your own copy.

## Checking before you send

- **Zoom to full size** and read every entry for spelling and alignment.
- **Check overflow**: long entries can be cut off in small fields; shorten or use abbreviations the form allows.
- **Confirm required fields** are not empty.
- **Name the file** clearly, for example "2026-10 application form completed.pdf".

If you need to add comments rather than answers, such as a note to the person reviewing the form, [annotating a PDF](/blog/how-to-annotate-a-pdf/) explains the difference between comments and content.
