[Easy Recipes & Meal Planner](/apps/easy-recipes-meal-planner/) connects four things: what people cook, when they plan to cook it, what they already own, and what it contains nutritionally. That chain from pantry to plate is what makes it interesting to **grocery businesses, meal-kit companies and recipe publishers**, and this page explains how a partnership could work.

## Four tools that feed each other

The app's page on this site describes a **recipe collection**, a **weekly planner**, an **ingredient inventory** and **nutrition information**, designed so that "the planner knows what you own, and cooking a planned recipe can update what you own".

- **Recipes** have clear ingredients and step-by-step directions, browsable by meal type, cuisine and dietary preference.
- **Servings scale properly**: every recipe starts at a one-person serving, and ingredient quantities and available nutrition, meaning calories, protein, carbohydrates and fat, adjust with the serving size.
- **The pantry tracker** lists what is in the kitchen, surfaces recipes using those ingredients and shows what is missing before a meal is chosen.
- **The planner** has breakfast, lunch, dinner and snack slots for the week.
- **An allergy log** records reactions with notes and timestamps, and recipes are flagged when an ingredient matches something recorded.

It is free with ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases.

## Where grocery businesses fit

The "what is missing" step is the moment a plan turns into shopping. For a grocer, that suggests partnerships around **cross-promotion**, **sponsorship** of seasonal recipe collections, or **integrations supported by the app's existing capabilities**. The proposal that would make most sense is one that keeps the user's list honest: it should still show what they need, not what a sponsor would like them to buy.

## Where meal-kit companies fit

A meal-kit company's portioned recipes with ingredient lists would be close to the app's own format. Licensing a set of a company's recipes, or **co-developing** a collection, could give users a way to plan with them alongside their pantry. Because the app scales from one serving, kits designed for two or four could map onto its serving controls.

## Where recipe publishers fit

For a publisher with a library of tested recipes, arrangements to discuss include **licensing**, **publishing and distribution**, **localisation** into other markets and cuisines, and **white-label versions where technically feasible**.

## Accuracy and allergy rules

Food partnerships carry real responsibility, and the listing and the app's page on this site set the limits:

- **Nutrition appears only for supported recipes.** Where data is missing, the app does not estimate it, and partner recipes would follow the same rule.
- **Allergy flags are prompts, not guarantees.** They depend on what users log and on each recipe's ingredients, so users should always confirm ingredients and labels.
- **No health claims.** The app plans and informs; it is not medical or dietary advice and does not prescribe a diet.

## A worked example: one recipe, scaled

A licensed recipe lists 75 g of dry pasta per serving. A user plans it for a household of three, so the app scales the quantity to 3 × 75 = 225 g, and the available nutrition scales with it. If pasta is already on their pantry list, the app does not show it as missing; if it is not, pasta shows up as something to buy. The partner's recipe is used and the shopping step reflects what the user recorded.

## Acquiring the meal planner

Reign Creative LLC is open to serious discussions about **acquiring this app individually, alongside the other food and health apps, or as part of the portfolio**. This page publishes **no prices, valuations, revenue or user numbers**, and it does not constitute an offer.

The mechanics of a sale run through **Google Play's developer-account transfer**. Google's help says the new owner's account needs an **active payments profile** because the app has in-app products, and that if the new account uses a different default currency, an app with in-app purchases is unpublished after the transfer until the new owner has verified the new prices and published it again. **Promotions do not carry over**, test groups must be rebuilt, and the original account keeps its **orders and earnings reports**.
