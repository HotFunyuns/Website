Learn Shanghainese Wu Chinese was built to teach Shanghainese as a language in its own right, in the words of its page on this site, rather than as a Mandarin accent. That focus is also why Reign Creative LLC is keen to work with heritage programmes and cultural institutions, whose own work with Shanghainese speakers and learners could help a course like this reach the people it was built for. Below is what the app covers, how organisations might use it, and how a partnership or sale would proceed.

## A course for Shanghai Wu itself

The Google Play listing describes Shanghainese — the Shanghai dialect, Shanghai Wu, a variety of Wu Chinese — as distinct from both Mandarin and Cantonese, and says the course teaches its pronunciation, vocabulary and sentence patterns directly rather than routing them through Mandarin. Twelve units run through greetings, family, food, numbers, time, home, transport, shopping, school, work, health, social life and real conversations.

Chinese characters and Shanghainese romanisation appear together, and the course covers **connected speech and tone sandhi**, the way tones change when syllables run together. The site's explainer on [Shanghainese tones and tone sandhi](/blog/shanghainese-tones-and-tone-sandhi/) goes into the linguistics. Practice includes vocabulary and phrase lessons, character drills, pronunciation guidance, grammar and sentence building, travel language and review of missed items, with daily goals, XP, levels, streaks and achievements.

The app is free to download, supported by ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases. It also runs in reverse, offering a full English course explained through Shanghainese. The learner's guide is [Learn Shanghainese: a beginner's guide](/blog/learn-shanghainese-beginners-guide/); the app page is [/apps/learn-shanghainese/](/apps/learn-shanghainese/).

## Families keeping the language across generations

One of the audiences the app's page names is "families trying to keep Shanghainese across generations", alongside heritage learners reconnecting with family in Shanghai. A heritage programme could use the app as shared homework between sessions, or as a common reference so parents and children are working from the same twelve units. The site's piece on [heritage learners of Shanghainese](/blog/heritage-language-learners-shanghainese/) discusses that learner group.

A worked example of a family programme: meeting once a fortnight and covering one unit per meeting, the twelve units would take 12 × 2 = 24 weeks, about six months, with the review activities filling the weeks between. A programme could then move on to the themed packs.

## Cultural institutions and the themed packs

Beyond the core path, themed packs extend into idioms, informal phrases, travel, business, relationships, pronunciation, grammar, listening, culture and etiquette. For a cultural centre or institution, those packs are the natural point of collaboration:

| Partner type | Possible collaboration |
| --- | --- |
| Cultural centre | Co-developed culture or etiquette content, as new work |
| Heritage school | Using the core units alongside classes |
| Community association | Promoting the English direction to Shanghainese speakers |
| Publisher | Distribution or localisation into further support languages |

Every row describes a conversation to have, not an existing arrangement.

## The romanisation caveat

The app's page is candid that Shanghainese has no single standard romanisation, so spellings in the app may differ from other resources. An institution using its own teaching materials should know this before a pilot; the site's guide to [Shanghainese romanisation choices](/blog/shanghainese-romanisation-choices/) sets out the options. We do not claim native-speaker certification for the course, and the listing describes it as teaching Shanghainese, a Wu variety, not Mandarin or Cantonese.

## Initial contact from an institution

An institution can reach us through the form on the [partnerships page](/partnerships/), which opens a pre-filled email, or by writing to ReignCreativeSupport@gmail.com directly. A paragraph naming the organisation, its learners in general terms and the idea is plenty. Please leave confidential, financial or personal information — learner names, contact lists, budgets — out of that opening email. Where both sides see potential, we suggest a call; deeper information is exchanged only later, with qualified parties, and under a non-disclosure agreement where that is sensible. Discussions are nonbinding: nothing becomes binding unless due diligence is completed and an agreement is signed. This site does not carry figures on downloads, income or pricing for any app.

## If Learn Shanghainese changed owners

Other routes we are open to include licensing, a branded edition where technically feasible, sponsorship that suits learners, and acquisition of this app on its own or with others. A sale would use Google Play's [transfer process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247): both accounts registered and active, a request approved by the receiving developer, and Google's support team replying within two business days. Users, ratings, reviews and the store listing move; payout reports stay. The buyer's account needs a payments profile for the app's in-app products, and ad SDK integrations must be updated.
