**Most confusion about anime terminology comes from mixing up four different kinds of term.** Demographic labels, format words, production vocabulary and source-material descriptions all get used in the same sentence, and they answer completely different questions.

Sorting them by kind makes the whole vocabulary easier to hold.

## Demographic labels

These describe an **intended readership**, originally of the magazine a manga was serialised in. They are not genres, and they are not art styles.

| Term | Intended readership |
| --- | --- |
| Shōnen | Younger male |
| Shōjo | Younger female |
| Seinen | Adult male |
| Josei | Adult female |
| Kodomomuke | Children |

Two things follow that are worth knowing:

**Editorial conventions cluster around them.** A shōnen magazine's editorial expectations produce recognisable tendencies in pacing and subject. That is real, and it is a tendency rather than a rule.

**The label does not describe the audience.** Plenty of adults read shōnen and plenty of men read shōjo. The label records where a work was published, not who consumes it.

[Anime genres explained](/blog/anime-genres-explained/) covers the genre categories, which are a separate axis, and [anime art styles explained](/blog/anime-art-styles-explained/) covers why these labels are not style descriptions.

## Format terms

These describe **how something was released**.

- **TV series** — broadcast, episodic.
- **Film** — theatrical release.
- **OVA (Original Video Animation)** — released directly to home video rather than broadcast or theatrical.
- **ONA (Original Net Animation)** — released directly online. The modern equivalent of an OVA.
- **Special** — a short additional episode, often bundled with a home release.

The OVA and ONA distinction matters historically: OVAs allowed content and budgets that broadcast television did not, which shaped what got made in that format.

## Production and scheduling vocabulary

- **Cour** — a broadcast quarter in the Japanese television calendar, roughly three months. This is the reason series lengths cluster: a show is built to fit one cour or two.
- **Studio** — the animation production company. Studios have recognisable tendencies, though staff move between them and a studio name is a weaker predictor than it is often treated as.
- **Key animation** — the drawings that define the extremes of a motion, as distinct from the in-between frames.
- **Sakuga** — used in fan discussion to refer to sequences of notably high animation quality.
- **Seiyū** — voice actor.
- **Sub / dub** — subtitled in the original language versus dubbed into another.

The cour concept is the most practically useful of these, because it explains a structural feature of the medium: episode counts are not arbitrary, they are calendar-shaped.

## Source-material terms

Knowing what something was adapted from tells you a great deal about what it will be like.

- **Manga adaptation** — ongoing source material, which frequently means the anime catches up with the manga and either stops or diverges.
- **Light novel adaptation** — prose source, often with distinctive pacing and a heavy reliance on internal narration in the original.
- **Visual novel adaptation** — branching source material compressed into a single linear path, which is why these adaptations often feel like they are omitting something.
- **Anime original** — no prior source. Free to end properly, and with no reference version to be compared against.
- **Original net animation** — see format terms; frequently also anime original.

The most common structural complaint about an adaptation usually traces back to this category.

## Fandom shorthand

Terms that appear constantly in discussion:

- **Isekai** — a story premise in which a character is transported to another world.
- **Slice of life** — low-stakes, everyday-focused storytelling.
- **Filler** — episodes not derived from the source material, historically used to avoid overtaking an ongoing manga.
- **Canon** — material considered part of the official story.

The [Anime News Network lexicon](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/encyclopedia/lexicon.php) is a substantially broader reference than this list and a reasonable place to look up anything not covered here.

## A caveat on all of it

**Usage varies and drifts.** Terms are used loosely, communities develop their own conventions, and some words have shifted meaning over time. A glossary is a starting point, not an authority, and encountering a term used differently from a definition is normal rather than a sign that someone is wrong.
