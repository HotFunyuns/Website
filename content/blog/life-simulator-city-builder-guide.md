[Life Simulator: City Builder](/apps/life-simulator-city-builder/) is a town builder with people in it. You place homes, roads, facilities and production spaces, welcome residents as they move in, and follow their stories through quests, chapters and events, while farming and orders keep the town supplied. This guide covers the first neighbourhood and the habits that keep residents happy, based on the game's Google Play listing and what our app page records from its store screenshots.

## What kind of builder it is

Wikipedia describes a city-building game as one in which the player acts as the overall planner of a town, looking down on it from above and responsible for its growth. It traces the genre to *SimCity* in 1989, where the indicators of success were a positive budget balance and citizen satisfaction. Life Simulator keeps the planner's view but changes the mood. It is designed to be **relaxed rather than punishing**: you can follow the guided goals or ignore them for a while, return to unfinished projects whenever you like, and plan for convenience, for looks or simply for a neighbourhood you enjoy.

## Your first neighbourhood

The first of the four "how it works" steps on our app page is "start small": place the first homes and roads and welcome your first residents. The listing agrees that "your city starts small". As general city-builder advice, a compact first neighbourhood is easier to keep connected than a sprawling one, and it leaves room to expand later.

Plan the first block around three things:

1. **Homes** for the first residents.
2. **Roads** that connect them, laid out so you can extend them later rather than rebuild.
3. **Essential services**, which the screenshots show as part of town life, close enough to the homes they serve.

Leave space next to the first block for production and farming; the listing says farming supplies are used to support construction and production. [City builder tips for beginners](/blog/city-builder-tips-for-beginners/) covers road layouts and services in more depth.

## Farming and orders: how the town pays for itself

The second step on our app page is "farm and fill orders". Farming is part of the daily rhythm: grow crops, organise supplies and use them for construction and production. Orders give those supplies a destination, and completing them funds **construction and upgrades**. One routine to try: tend the farm, fill whatever orders you can, then spend on the next building or upgrade.

## Keeping residents happy

Residents are what make this a life sim rather than a pure builder. Each has **daily activities**, **personal goals** and a part in **community milestones**, and the story follows them through the places you build. The screenshots show resident **wellbeing** alongside the budget and essential services, so it pays to check on people as well as buildings. The listing says residents "connect the story to the places you create", so their goals are one way to decide what to build next.

## The story, at your own pace

Quests, chapters, episodes and special events give the town new direction, but you set the pace. Our app page's third step is "follow the story": work through quests and events, or set your own goals. Because you can always return to an unfinished project, a short session can simply mean placing one building or finishing one order. Achievements, which the screenshots also show, mark progress over the longer run.

## Sandbox: building for its own sake

The fourth step on our app page is "design freely". **Sandbox play** is there for pure design, with construction that can be instant when enabled, while normal costs in cash and materials still apply. With multiple town saves, you can keep one town for the story and another for experiments. [What cozy games are](/blog/what-are-cozy-games/) looks at the wider style that relaxed, open-ended builders like this one sit in.

## Limits worth knowing

Our app page is upfront: it is a relaxed builder, so players looking for traffic simulation or deep economics will find it light. The listing does not describe online or shared towns.
