Low-carb eating has its own market of products, recipes and content, and [Ketora: Keto Diet Carb Tracker](/apps/keto-diet-tracker/) is built around the number its Google Play listing puts first: net carbs. This page explains, for **low-carb food brands and recipe publishers**, what the app does, which kinds of brand and licensing arrangement fit it, and the firm rules on health claims.

## The features a brand would sit beside

Ketora, which the app's page on this site says was listed on Google Play as Keto Diet Tracker: Low Carb before its rename, is built around **net carbs**. When a food is logged, the app subtracts **fibre and sugar alcohols** from total carbohydrate and shows net and total side by side, with a **progress ring** for what is left of the daily limit, whether that is 20 g or 50 g.

Around that core:

| Feature | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Barcode scanner | Fills in nutrition from a label |
| Food diary | Shows the whole day, with saved meals and copied entries |
| Targets | Daily fat, protein, carb and calorie targets from a few questions; strict, lazy or flexible low-carb modes |
| Recipes | Keto recipes with net carbs per serving |
| Meal planner | Turns the week into a grocery list |
| Fasting timer | 16:8, 18:6, 20:4, OMAD or custom windows |
| Progress | Weight, measurements, water and self-taken ketone readings, with weekly insights |

The listing also describes an **AI coach** that suggests low-carb swaps and recipes from a user's logs. The app is free with ads, and the listing describes an optional **Ketora Premium** for extras such as advanced ketosis charts and data export.

## Brand ideas that fit the product

**Sponsored recipe collections.** A brand whose product suits keto cooking could co-develop recipes that appear with net carbs per serving. Sponsorship would be labelled.

**Accurate product data.** The barcode scanner fills in nutrition from a label. A brand could raise how its products' nutrition information appears when scanned; whether that is possible depends on the app's existing food-data capabilities, which would be part of the conversation.

**Grocery-list moments.** The meal planner's shopping list is where a recipe becomes a purchase. Cross-promotion there would need care to stay useful rather than intrusive.

## Publisher and licensing ideas

Recipe publishers already have the content; Ketora has the tracking. Reign Creative LLC is open to discussing **licensing**, **co-development** of recipe packs, **publishing and distribution**, **localisation** for other markets, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, and **integrations supported by existing capabilities**.

## The claims rule

Any partnership here keeps its marketing modest. The listing describes Ketora as **informational, not medical advice**, and suggests **speaking to a doctor before starting any new diet**, especially when pregnant, nursing or managing a medical condition. The app's page on this site also notes two technical limits: the app **records ketone readings people take themselves but does not measure ketones**, and **net carbs are only as accurate as the food data logged**.

Partner copy therefore talks about counting, planning and convenience. It does not promise weight loss, ketosis or health benefits, and it does not present a brand's product as a treatment.

## A worked example: a sponsored week

A recipe publisher proposes a "seven dinners under 10 g net carbs" collection. Each recipe lists net carbs per serving, worked out the way the app does: total carbohydrate minus fibre and sugar alcohols. If a dish has 14 g total carbohydrate, 5 g fibre and no sugar alcohols, its net figure is 14 − 5 = 9 g, inside the target. Users add the week to the planner and get one grocery list. The collection is clearly labelled as sponsored, and every number traces back to ingredient data.

## Selling the app

Reign Creative LLC is open to serious discussions about **acquiring Ketora on its own or with other apps in the portfolio**. This page does not include **prices, valuations, revenue or user figures**, and it is not an offer.

Google Play handles a sale as an **app transfer between developer accounts**. According to Google's Play Console help, the receiving account must be registered and active; the app's **users, ratings and store listing** move with it, while **reports such as earnings and payouts** stay with the original account. Integrated services like Firebase or Google Analytics need their account settings updated, and the **ad SDK integrations in the app are updated** so ad traffic is credited to the correct account.
