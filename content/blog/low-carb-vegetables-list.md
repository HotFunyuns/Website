Vegetables are not all equal in carbohydrate. This list gives total carbohydrate, fibre and **net carbs** for common vegetables, per typical serving and per 100 g, worked out from the US Department of Agriculture's **SR Legacy** data in FoodData Central.

## How net carbs are counted here

Net carbs, as this list counts them, are **total carbohydrate minus dietary fibre**. The FDA's Nutrition Facts label lists Total Carbohydrate and Dietary Fiber as separate lines, but it has no "net carbs" line, so the subtraction is yours to do. [Net carbs explained](/blog/net-carbs-explained-keto-tracking/) covers the definition and its caveats, including sugar alcohols, which these whole vegetables do not contain.

All values are for raw vegetables, using the SR Legacy entries (for example "Broccoli, raw" and "Lettuce, cos or romaine, raw") and the portion weights USDA lists; each table gives both the per-serving and the per-100 g figure. Cooked vegetables have their own SR Legacy entries with different values per cup, so look those up if you weigh food after cooking.

## The lowest: leafy greens and watery vegetables

| Vegetable | Serving | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Net per 100 g |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kale | 1 cup (21 g) | 0.9 g | 0.9 g | 0.1 g | 0.3 g |
| Spinach | 1 cup (30 g) | 1.1 g | 0.7 g | 0.4 g | 1.4 g |
| Romaine lettuce | 1 cup shredded (47 g) | 1.5 g | 1.0 g | 0.6 g | 1.2 g |
| Celery | 1 cup chopped (101 g) | 3.0 g | 1.6 g | 1.4 g | 1.4 g |
| Cucumber, with peel | ½ cup slices (52 g) | 1.9 g | 0.3 g | 1.6 g | 3.1 g |
| Mushrooms, white | 1 cup slices (70 g) | 2.3 g | 0.7 g | 1.6 g | 2.3 g |

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Eggplant | 1 cup cubes (82 g) | 4.8 g | 2.5 g | 2.4 g | 2.9 g |
| Zucchini | 1 cup chopped (124 g) | 3.9 g | 1.2 g | 2.6 g | 2.1 g |
| Cabbage | 1 cup chopped (89 g) | 5.2 g | 2.2 g | 2.9 g | 3.3 g |
| Cauliflower | 1 cup chopped (107 g) | 5.3 g | 2.1 g | 3.2 g | 3.0 g |
| Tomato | 1 medium (123 g) | 4.8 g | 1.5 g | 3.3 g | 2.7 g |
| Broccoli | 1 cup chopped (91 g) | 6.0 g | 2.4 g | 3.7 g | 4.0 g |
| Green beans | 1 cup (100 g) | 7.0 g | 2.7 g | 4.3 g | 4.3 g |

## Higher per cup: red pepper, carrot and onion

| Vegetable | Serving | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Net per 100 g |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red bell pepper | 1 cup chopped (149 g) | 9.0 g | 3.1 g | 5.9 g | 3.9 g |
| Carrots | 1 cup chopped (128 g) | 12.3 g | 3.6 g | 8.7 g | 6.8 g |
| Onion | 1 cup chopped (160 g) | 14.9 g | 2.7 g | 12.2 g | 7.6 g |

Avocado, botanically a fruit but eaten like a vegetable, is unusual: half an avocado, about 100 g, has 8.6 g of carbohydrate but 6.7 g of fibre, leaving about **1.8 g** net, though it is high in fat and calories.

## Why both columns matter

Servings hide weight. A cup of raw kale weighs 21 g and a cup of chopped broccoli 91 g, so kale looks far lower per cup. Per 100 g, kale (0.3 g) is still lowest, but broccoli (4.0 g) and Brussels sprouts (5.1 g) are closer to green beans (4.3 g) than the cups suggest. Red and green bell peppers show the other effect: the same 149 g cup, but the red pepper carries more sugar (4.2 g against 2.4 g per 100 g) and about 1.5 g more net carbs.

## A worked example: a low-carb dinner plate

A plate of a cup of cauliflower (3.2 g), a cup of broccoli (3.7 g) and a cup of shredded romaine (0.6 g) comes to about **7.5 g of net carbs** (7.4 g before rounding). Swap the cauliflower for a cup of chopped carrot (8.7 g) and the plate rises to about 13 g (12.9 g before rounding). Against a low daily limit, such as the 20 g or 50 g examples on the Ketora app page (examples, not recommendations; any limit of your own is best set with a health professional), that one swap moves a meaningful share of the day. [Keto macros explained](/blog/keto-macros-explained/) covers how targets are set, and [low-carb fruits](/blog/low-carb-fruits-list/) gives the same figures for fruit.
