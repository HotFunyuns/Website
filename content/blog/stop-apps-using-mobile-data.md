A data plan can disappear in a weekend: a video that kept streaming, an app that synced photos over the mobile network, a pile of updates downloaded away from Wi-Fi. Android has good tools to find the culprit and stop it. This guide uses Google's Pixel help, so the paths are for Pixel phones; Google itself notes that if the steps do not work on a custom Android version you should check with your device manufacturer, and menu names may differ on other brands.

## Step 1: find out what is using data

Google defines data usage as how much data your phone uploads or downloads using mobile data. To check it on a Pixel:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Network & internet**, then **SIMs**. The total appears at the top.
3. Tap **App data usage** for graphs and details, and choose a time period with the arrow.
4. Look **below the graph** for how much each app has used.

Start here: the list shows which apps to look at first, so you can deal with them directly rather than relying only on a blanket setting.

## Step 2: turn on Data Saver

**Data Saver** is the broad fix. Google explains that it lets **most apps and services get background data only via Wi-Fi**, while **currently active** apps and services can still use mobile data. Turn it on in **Settings, Network & internet, Data Saver**. When it is on, a Data Saver icon appears in the status bar and a notice shows at the top of Settings. Google also suggests adding Data Saver to Quick Settings for one-tap access.

## Step 3: make exceptions for apps that must keep working

Google notes that some apps and services will not work as expected unless they can run in the background even when you are not using them. Its help describes **Unrestricted data** exceptions: open **Settings, Network & internet, Data Saver, Unrestricted data**, and turn on each app or service that should keep using mobile data in the background. Google Play's own help notes that the Play Store may be affected when Data Saver is on and Wi-Fi is unavailable, and that you can let it keep getting background data the same way.

## Step 4: set a warning or a limit

For a hard ceiling, Google describes **Data warning & limit** under **Settings, Network & internet, SIMs**:

- a **warning** sends a notification when usage reaches the number you set;
- a **limit** turns mobile data off automatically when you reach it, with a notification. To turn data back on, switch the limit off.

Google warns that some features will not work while mobile data is off.

## A worked example: a 10 GB plan

Suppose, as a hypothetical, your plan gives you 10 GB a month. Set a **warning at 8 GB** and a **limit at 9.5 GB**, leaving a small buffer, because Google notes the phone measures the usage shown in Settings and some carriers measure it differently. Check App data usage after the first week: if one app has used 3 GB, that single app is on course for about 12 GB over a four-week month, more than the whole plan. Restrict that app, or use it only on Wi-Fi, before reaching for anything else.

## Step 5: build Wi-Fi-only habits

Several big data users have their own settings:

- **App updates**: Google Play's help explains that **Auto-update apps** can be set to update **over Wi-Fi only**, under the Play Store's profile menu, **Settings, Network preferences**.
- **Auto-sync**: Google notes that turning off auto-sync uses less data, though you then need to sync apps manually.
- **Roaming**: Google notes some carriers charge extra for data roaming, which can be turned on or off under **SIMs**.
- **Video**: if App data usage shows a streaming app near the top, download or copy videos to the phone over Wi-Fi where the service allows it, then watch them offline.

## Check again after a week

New apps and new habits change the picture. A week after changing settings, open **App data usage** again and compare the list with what you saw before. If the total is falling and no single app dominates, the settings are working. If one app is still climbing, look inside that app's own settings for options such as Wi-Fi-only downloads or lower streaming quality before restricting it further.
