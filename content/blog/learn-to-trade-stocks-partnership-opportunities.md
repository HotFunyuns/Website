Teaching people how markets work without putting their savings at risk is the core idea behind [Learn to Trade Stocks Beginner](/apps/learn-to-trade-stocks/). That makes it relevant to **financial-literacy programmes, schools and learning platforms**. This overview explains what the simulator does, the safeguards any partnership would need, the arrangements Reign Creative LLC will discuss, and how a transfer would work if the app were sold.

This page is about education. It is **not investment advice**, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any security.

## A simulator, plainly described

The listing calls the app a **trading simulator** for education and simulated trading. Learners buy and sell with a **virtual balance**, build a practice portfolio and connect each decision to its outcome. The listing is explicit that there are **no real-money brokerage transactions, no personalised financial advice and no guaranteed profits**.

Lessons start with **ownership**, since a stock represents ownership in a company, and move through **order types, portfolios, risk, options and technical indicators**. The store screenshots describe a chart-reading academy covering candlestick and chart patterns, moving averages and momentum indicators. Practice includes a **guided first trade**, **market, limit and stop orders**, tracked positions and profit and loss, **chart replay** with play, step and speed controls, simulated scenarios, daily challenges and a **trading journal**.

The app is free with ads, and Google Play lists **no in-app purchases**.

## Safeguards a partner would inherit

The limits stated in the listing and on the app's page on this site become the rules for any partner material:

- **Simulation is not prediction.** Simulated results cannot predict what happens in the real market.
- **Virtual money does not reproduce real risk**, including the emotions that come with it.
- **Options carry their own risks**, which the listing names: leverage, time decay and substantial risk.
- **Indicators summarise; they do not confirm.** Technical indicators cannot confirm a future outcome or remove uncertainty.
- **Rules change.** Trading restrictions and settlement can change, so learners should check current rules with their broker and official sources such as **Investor.gov and FINRA**.

A programme that respected these limits would present the app as a safe place to learn vocabulary and mechanics, never as a route to returns.

## Programmes, schools and platforms

For **financial-literacy programmes**, a simulator could serve sessions on investing, where participants practise reading a quote or placing a limit order without consequences. Sponsorship or co-branded session guides are possible.

**Schools** teaching personal finance could pair lessons with simulator tasks: place three orders of different types, record the reason for each in the journal, and review the outcome a week later.

**Learning platforms** might discuss **licensing**, **integrations supported by the app's existing capabilities**, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, **localisation**, or **co-development** of new lesson units.

## A worked example: a four-session module

A community programme runs four evening sessions. Session one covers what a share is and how a quote reads. Session two introduces market and limit orders, with each participant placing one of each in the simulator. Session three uses chart replay to show how prices moved over a period, stressing that the past does not predict the future. Session four reviews journals: each participant explains one decision and what they would check before risking real money. Across the module, no one handles real funds, and the closing message points to official investor-protection resources.

## Changing ownership on Google Play

Google treats a sale as a **developer-account transfer**, completed in Play Console. The receiving account has to be registered and active, and Google's help notes that a new account carries a **US$25 registration fee**. Users, download statistics, ratings and reviews and the store listing move with the app; **payout and earnings reports do not**, and Google suggests downloading them beforehand. Services linked to the app need their settings updated, and ad SDK integrations are updated so ad traffic is credited to the correct account. Google says its team **replies within 2 business days** of a request the receiving developer has approved.
