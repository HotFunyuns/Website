Soccer is the only sport in this genre where you can play well and get nothing for it.

A basketball or baseball roster that is slightly better than its opponent wins slightly more often. A soccer side that is slightly better than its opponent draws a lot. That third result is why a perfect campaign in this sport reads 38-0-0 rather than 38-0, and why it demands something that "do not lose" does not: you have to convert. A side built to be difficult to beat will produce a long unbeaten run and nowhere near a perfect record.

Everything about drafting an XI follows from that.

## Three outcomes change the shape of a good squad

In a two-outcome sport, defensive solidity and attacking quality are close to interchangeable ways of buying wins. Add draws and they stop being symmetric.

Solidity converts losses into draws. Attacking quality converts draws into wins. Both are worth having, but they buy different things, and a squad that has spent everything on one of them hits a ceiling that more of the same will not raise. This is the specific reason a balanced XI outperforms a lopsided one in [38-0 Football Draft XI Soccer](/apps/soccer-draft-gm-xi/), and it is a genuinely different constraint from the ones the other drafting sports impose.

The practical version: before you start picking, decide roughly where your side sits on that axis, and notice when a pick would push you further in a direction you have already invested in.

## Coverage is not fit

Every draft game asks you to fill each position. This one asks something more specific, because position fit and playing style feed the result alongside the ratings.

The distinction is worth labouring:

- **Coverage** means there is a body in every slot. It is a completeness check, and it is easy.
- **Fit** means each slot holds someone suited to it. It is a judgement, and the simulation reads it.

An XI with full coverage and poor fit looks fine on the team sheet and underperforms in the campaign, which is the most confusing possible failure mode if you do not know fit is being measured. You did everything the interface asked. The result disagreed anyway.

Style adds a second layer on top of fit. Two players can both suit a position and want incompatible things from the players around them. A squad assembled purely by rating tends to accumulate this quietly — several players who each individually make sense and collectively want to play different games.

## Drafting the XI

There is no single correct order, but there is a defensible one, and it comes from thinking about which mistakes are expensive.

### Settle the spine first

The positions that run through the middle of the side — the goalkeeper, the centre of defence, the centre of midfield, the focal point in attack — touch the most situations. A compromise in the spine is a compromise that appears constantly.

Draft these while the board is at its widest. It costs you nothing to solve your most load-bearing positions early, and deferring them is how you end up choosing between two players who neither fit nor appeal.

### Then decide what the wide positions are for

Wide areas are where a squad expresses what it is. This is the point at which the earlier decision about solidity versus conversion becomes a concrete pick rather than an intention: you are either buying more of what you already have or you are balancing it.

If you have built a resilient spine, this is where you buy the goals that turn draws into wins. If your spine is attacking, this is where you make sure the side can defend a lead.

### Finish by checking the squad, not the players

Before you lock the XI, read it as a group rather than a list. Two questions do most of the work:

1. **Is there a position where I settled?** That slot will show up in the campaign.
2. **Do these players want the same things?** A side of individually excellent players with incompatible styles is the classic high-rating, low-result draft.

## The formats change the question

The game runs limited drafts, budget challenges and computer battles alongside the open format, plus tournaments, quizzes, player guessing, season modes and daily challenges.

Budget challenges are the most instructive of these, because a budget converts drafting from ranking into allocation. Without one, the best available player who fits is the pick. With one, a good player at a low price can beat a better player at a high one, and the actual skill is knowing when you are paying for a name rather than a contribution. It is the same lesson the [football draft guide](/blog/football-draft-gm-guide/) covers from the other direction, where budgets expose entire units you underfunded.

Limited formats test something else again: whether you can commit without searching. When you cannot keep looking, each pick has to be made against an internal standard of what "good" means in this run, and building that standard from the first few cards is the skill. Drafters who are excellent when they can search and poor when they cannot are relying on the search to do their judging.

Competitive formats add an opponent's board to the things you are tracking. A pick can now be worth making early purely because losing that player to the other side would cost you twice.

## What the campaign simulation is doing

When the XI is locked and the simulation runs, it plays out a 38-game campaign and reports wins, draws and losses.

That result is a model's output. It is computed from the squad you built — ratings, position fit, playing style, balance — according to the game's own rules. Three clarifications matter:

**It does not describe anything real.** The players are fictional and generated inside the game. The result has no relationship to any real match, club, competition or player, and it is not usable as an indication of any real outcome.

**It is a score for your squad building.** Read it the way you would read the score at the end of a puzzle. The question worth asking is not whether you were unlucky but which decision the result is disagreeing with.

**The perfect record is meant to be hard.** A campaign of 38 wins with no draws and no defeats is the target the game is built around, not the expected reward for a competent draft. Getting close is the normal good outcome.

Because completed runs can be saved and measured against your next attempt, the genuinely useful comparison is your own previous squad rather than any absolute standard. That is the loop the game is designed for: draft, read the result, change one thing, draft again.

## The longer save

Club management extends a single draft into something that runs: formations, squad depth, transfers, contracts, fitness and season progress.

Squad depth is the concept that most changes how you think. A one-off draft is an XI, and an XI is a snapshot. A club is a group that has to keep functioning, and depth is what stops a single problem becoming a structural one. The trade is familiar to anyone who has played a management game — resources spent on depth are resources not spent on the first eleven, and the correct balance depends on how long a horizon you are playing for.

Transfers deserve a specific note, because they are the only tool that fixes a shape problem. If a draft left you strong in one area and thin in another, no amount of good selection repairs it; you have to convert surplus into coverage. That is the same function trades serve in the other sports, covered in the [basketball draft and GM guide](/blog/basketball-draft-gm-guide/).

## Why the quizzes are not filler

Tournaments, quizzes, player guessing and daily challenges sit alongside the drafting, and it is easy to write them off as padding. The knowledge formats are testing the same faculty the draft board needs: reading a set of attributes and forming a fast, accurate judgement about what they will do.

If your drafts keep returning campaigns you did not expect, the gap is usually in that reading rather than in your strategy. Practising it directly is cheaper than practising it 38 games at a time.

## A pre-draft checklist

1. **Decide early whether you are buying solidity or conversion**, and notice when a pick doubles down.
2. **Draft the spine while the board is wide.**
3. **Check fit, not just coverage.** A filled slot and a suited slot are different things.
4. **Read the XI as a group before you lock it.** Incompatible styles are the quiet killer.
5. **After the campaign, change exactly one thing and run it again.** One variable at a time is the only way to learn what the model rewards.
