A batter's innings can end in many ways, and only some of them involve the bowler beating the bat. The Laws of Cricket, in MCC's 2026 edition, set out nine methods of dismissal in Laws 32 to 40, and Law 25 adds a tenth, retired out. Here is each one, how often it happens, and the rules about extras and credit that trip up new fans.

## The ten dismissals

| Law | Dismissal | In brief | Bowler's wicket? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 32 | Bowled | A legal delivery breaks the striker's wicket, even off bat or body | Yes |
| 33 | Caught | The ball comes off the bat and a fielder holds it before it touches the ground | Yes |
| 34 | Hit the ball twice | The striker wilfully hits the ball a second time, other than to guard the wicket | No |
| 35 | Hit wicket | The striker breaks their own wicket with bat or body while receiving the ball or setting off for a first run | Yes |
| 36 | Leg before wicket | The ball would have hit the wicket but for the striker's body | Yes |
| 37 | Obstructing the field | A batter wilfully obstructs or distracts the fielders, including striking the ball with a hand not holding the bat | No |
| 38 | Run out | A batter is out of their ground when a fielder fairly breaks the wicket | No |
| 39 | Stumped | The striker leaves the crease without attempting a run and the wicket-keeper breaks the wicket | Yes |
| 40 | Timed out | The incoming batter is not ready within 3 minutes | No |
| 25 | Retired out | A batter retires for a reason other than illness, injury or another unavoidable cause, and does not resume; resuming needs the opposing captain's consent | No |

Wikipedia notes that before the 2017 Code, "handled the ball" was a separate dismissal; it is now covered by obstructing the field. For run out, obstructing the field, hit the ball twice and timed out, the Laws state outright that the bowler does not get credit for the wicket; Wikipedia's summary table of dismissals shows bowled, caught, hit wicket, LBW and stumped credited to the bowler.

## How common each one is

Wikipedia cites an analysis of 63,584 Test match dismissals between 1877 and 2012:

| Dismissal | Share of Test dismissals |
| --- | --- |
| Caught | 56.9% (40.6% by fielders, 16.3% by the wicket-keeper) |
| Bowled | 21.4% |
| Leg before wicket | 14.3% |
| Run out | 3.5% |
| Stumped | 2.0% |

Those five methods made up 98.2% of all dismissals; the rest were retirements, hit wicket, hit the ball twice, handled the ball or obstructing the field, and timed out.

## Out off a no ball or a wide

Extras change what is possible:

- **No ball.** Law 21 says that when no ball is called, neither batter can be out except **hit the ball twice, obstructing the field or run out**.
- **Wide.** Law 22 allows only **hit wicket, obstructing the field, run out or stumped**.

So a batter can be stumped off a wide, because the keeper may gather a ball the batter has reached for, but never bowled, caught or LBW off one. [No ball and wide rules](/blog/no-ball-and-wide-rules/) covers when each is called.

## Fine print that decides close calls

**Bowled takes precedence.** If the wicket is broken by a legal delivery, the striker is out bowled even if another method could also apply.

**Caught must be a fair catch.** Neither the ball nor any fielder touching it may be grounded beyond the boundary before the catch is completed. The 2026 edition also ends the "bunny hop" catch: MCC's summary of the changes explains that a fielder who jumps from outside the boundary may touch the ball while airborne only once, and must then be wholly grounded inside the boundary for the rest of the delivery.

**Hit wicket has new clarifications.** MCC's 2026 summary explains that receiving the ball lasts until the batter regains balance, so a striker who stumbles onto the stumps after a shot is out. But the striker is not out if a fielder knocks them into the wicket, or if a detached piece of equipment, such as a bat that slips from the hands, touches another player before hitting the stumps.

**Stumped versus run out.** If the striker has not attempted a run and the keeper breaks the wicket alone, it is stumped; if the striker was attempting a run, or another fielder was involved, it is run out.
