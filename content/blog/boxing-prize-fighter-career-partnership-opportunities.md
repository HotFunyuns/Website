Boxing Prize Fighter Career is small, sharp and pixelated: a boxing game in which you throw the punches yourself and pay for it if you forget to breathe. Reign Creative LLC owns it, and its mix of hands-on bouts and career structure makes it worth a conversation with boxing gyms, boxing and fitness brands, and publishers of compact sports games. We are open to sponsorship, cross-promotion, licensing, publishing, co-development and acquisition.

## Pixel art, real punch selection

According to the Google Play listing, you create and customise a fighter, then take them into a career where results and preparation decide how far they go. In the ring you throw jabs, crosses, hooks and uppercuts, mix head and body shots, link combinations and block the reply. The site's guide to [boxing punches](/blog/boxing-punches-explained/) covers the real versions.

Three words sum up what the store page says the game rewards: **stamina, distance and defense**. Swing wildly in round one and the energy bar punishes you later; chase the knockout carelessly and you leave yourself open to the counter. Between career bouts, sparring and training are safe places to fix a flaw, because a bad session there costs nothing on the record. The screenshots show more configuration than the description mentions — weight classes, difficulty settings, custom round counts and timers, opponent profiles and a side-by-side fighter comparison — which matters to anyone planning events, because it suggests bouts can be set up to suit a format.

Variety comes from opponents and challenges: Quick Play with changeable opponents, rotating daily and weekly challenges, and a Boss Ladder of tougher fights. The game is free, ad-supported, and Google Play lists in-app purchases. The listing describes single-player play against the game's own opponents. The player-facing [Boxing Prize Fighter Career guide](/blog/boxing-prize-fighter-career-guide/) and the [app page](/apps/boxing-prize-fighter-career/) have more.

## What a boxing gym might do with it

Range, energy and defence are the ideas the game's listing puts first, and a gym could relate them to its own classes. That overlap is the whole basis of the pitch: a light-hearted way for a gym to stay in touch with members between sessions, with no suggestion that tapping a phone screen replaces pad work. Ideas we would discuss:

- **A gym leaderboard** built around the daily and weekly challenges or the Boss Ladder.
- **A co-branded edition** with the gym's name and colours, where technically feasible.
- **Corner talk content**, linking the game's training and sparring to topics like [what a boxing corner does](/blog/what-does-a-boxing-corner-do/).

A worked example for a four-week gym challenge: if every daily challenge and one weekly challenge were counted, members would have 7 × 4 = 28 daily attempts plus 4 weekly ones, 32 in all, to post scores against. The real rotation would be confirmed before any event.

## Fitness brands: what we can and cannot say

Fitness and boxing equipment brands are a plausible audience match, but the partnership would be about promotion and audience rather than health. The game is played on a phone; we will not claim it gets anyone fit or teaches technique. Sponsored challenges or branded content would be new work, agreed in writing, and would need to suit the game and its players.

## Publishers: a compact fighting game with a ladder

For a publisher, the appeal is a short-session sports game with a clear identity. The app page's audience list includes boxing fans who want to throw the punches rather than simulate them, and anyone who likes pixel-art sports games with short bouts. Publishing in new regions, distribution, localisation and co-development are all open. Reign Creative's [40-0 MMA & Boxing Fight Draft](/apps/mma-boxing-fight-draft/) covers the drafting and management side of combat sports, so a publisher could look at the pair.

## Boxing only, and other limits

The app's page names its limits: boxing only, with no MMA rules or kicks; pixel art rather than realistic 3D; and no online multiplayer mentioned. Real boxers, promotions or sanctioning bodies are not in the game, and using any of them would need their agreement. We do not publish prices or financial figures.

## Handing Boxing Prize Fighter Career to a buyer

A sale uses Google Play's [app transfer process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). The target account must be registered and active and, for an app with in-app products, hold an active payments profile; ad SDKs such as AdMob need updating afterwards. Users, ratings, reviews and the listing go across; promotions do not, though promo codes already issued should still work. The receiving developer approves the request, and Google's support team then replies within two business days.
