"How long is a cricket match?" has three very different answers. A T20 international is scheduled for just over three hours, a one-day international for about seven and a half, and a Test for up to five days. The ICC's men's playing conditions, effective October 2026, fix the scheduled hours and the minimum over rates behind each, and they explain why the clock matters so much.

## The scheduled length of each format

| Format | Scheduled playing time | Interval between innings | Typical total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test | Five days, six hours of play a day | Lunch (40 minutes) and tea (20 minutes); 10 minutes between innings | Up to five days |
| Four-day Test | Four days, six and a half hours a day | Lunch (40 minutes) and tea (20 minutes); 10 minutes between innings | Up to four days |
| ODI | Two sessions of 3.5 hours | 30 minutes | About 7.5 hours |
| T20I | Two sessions of 1 hour 25 minutes | 15 minutes, reducible to 10 | About 3 hours 5 minutes |

## How the over rates fit the sessions

The session lengths line up with the minimum over rates in the playing conditions:

| Format | Minimum over rate | Overs | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test | 15 overs an hour | 90 a day | 6 hours |
| ODI | 14.28 overs an hour | 50 an innings | About 3.5 hours (50 ÷ 14.28 = 3.50) |
| T20I | 14.11 overs an hour | 20 an innings | About 85 minutes (20 ÷ 14.11 × 60 = 85.0) |

The four-day Test conditions ask for at least 98 overs in six and a half hours, slightly faster than 15 an hour.

The rates include allowances. The T20I conditions, for example, give time back for injuries treated on the field, third-umpire referrals and reviews, time-wasting by the batting side and a drinks break of 2 minutes 30 seconds in each innings, plus an extra minute for each of the sixth to ninth wickets. The current over rate is displayed on the scoreboard, updated at least every four overs.

## A Test day in detail

On every day except the last, play continues until at least **90 overs** have been bowled or the scheduled finish is reached, whichever is later, with up to **30 minutes** of overtime. Overs still not bowled are not made up on later days.

Lost time is recovered in two ways: on the day, by extending play by up to an hour, and on later days, by adding up to 30 minutes a day. The last day has its own pattern: at least 75 overs before the final hour, and at least 15 in it. [How a Test match ends in a draw](/blog/how-a-test-match-ends-in-a-draw/) explains why that last hour matters.

## Why matches finish early

Scheduled time is a maximum, not a promise. Matches end as soon as a result is reached:

- In a limited-overs match, the side batting first can be bowled out early, and a chasing side can pass its target with overs to spare.
- In a Test, a side that wins by an innings needs only three innings, and the [follow-on](/blog/follow-on-rule-cricket/) can speed that up.

**A worked example.** In a T20I, the team batting first is all out after 16 overs, and the chasing team wins in 14.3 overs. Only 30.3 of the 40 scheduled overs are bowled; at the minimum rate of 14.11 an hour, the missing 9.3 overs are worth about 40 minutes, so the match finishes that much early.

## Why matches run late

- **Slow over rates.** Play continues until the overs are bowled; the ICC's sanctions for slow rates include, in the World Test Championship, a deduction of one competition point per over short.
- **Rain.** In limited-overs matches, overs are cut at the over rate when time is lost, and targets are reset by [the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method](/blog/duckworth-lewis-stern-method/); shortened innings also shrink the [powerplays](/blog/powerplays-in-cricket/).
- **Reserve days.** ODI and T20I series may agree a reserve day for an incomplete match. The playing conditions say every effort must first be made to finish on the scheduled day, reducing overs if necessary; the match moves to the reserve day only if the minimum overs needed for a result cannot be bowled, and a match that had already started resumes as though the last ball had just been played.
- **Super Overs.** A tied ODI or T20I is followed by a [Super Over](/blog/super-over-rules/), normally starting five minutes after the match ends, with extra time set aside to complete it.
