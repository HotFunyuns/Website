Every Thai consonant letter carries two pieces of information: a sound, and a **class**. The class is one of the inputs that decide a syllable's tone, which is one reason two letters that sound identical today are not interchangeable. This reference lists the letters in each class, explains the pairs and the leading ห, and suggests the fastest way to memorise them.

## Why Thai has consonant classes

In Thai the system is called **ไตรยางศ์** (*trai-yang*), which Thai Wikipedia derives from Sanskrit words meaning "three levels". It says the earliest evidence of the classification is in the *Chindamani*, a book from the Ayutthaya period.

English Wikipedia explains where the classes came from. The names reflect how the sounds were pronounced in **Old Thai**: "middle" letters were voiceless unaspirated stops, "high" letters voiceless aspirated stops or voiceless fricatives, and "low" letters voiced sounds. Later sound changes blurred those differences, but the tone behaviour stayed with the letters, which is why the classes still matter.

## The three lists

| Class | Count | Letters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mid (กลาง) | 9 | ก จ ฎ ฏ ด ต บ ป อ |
| High (สูง) | 11 | ข ฃ ฉ ฐ ถ ผ ฝ ศ ษ ส ห |
| Low (ต่ำ) | 24 | ค ฅ ฆ ง ช ซ ฌ ญ ฑ ฒ ณ ท ธ น พ ฟ ภ ม ย ร ล ว ฬ ฮ |

That makes 44. Two of them, ฃ (*kho khuat*) and ฅ (*kho khon*), are marked obsolete in Wikipedia's alphabet table.

## Each letter has a name

To aid learning, each consonant is traditionally paired with a Thai word that features its sound. Wikipedia's example is ข, called *kho khai*: *kho* is the sound, and *khai* (ไข่) means "egg". A few more from its table:

| Letter | Name | Meaning of the word | Class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ก | ko kai | chicken | Mid |
| ข | kho khai | egg | High |
| ค | kho khwai | water buffalo | Low |
| ส | so suea | tiger | High |
| ม | mo ma | horse | Low |

Learning a letter's name and its class together saves relearning later.

## Paired low letters

Thai Wikipedia divides the low class in two. **Fourteen paired low letters** share their sound with a high-class letter, forming **seven pairs**:

| High | Low |
| --- | --- |
| ข ฃ | ค ฅ ฆ |
| ฉ | ช ฌ |
| ฐ ถ | ฑ ฒ ท ธ |
| ผ | พ ภ |
| ฝ | ฟ |
| ศ ษ ส | ซ |
| ห | ฮ |

Between them, a pair can spell **all five tones** of one syllable. Thai Wikipedia's example runs คา, ข่า, ข้า or ค่า, ค้า, ขา: mid, low, falling, high and rising, using low-class ค and high-class ข.

## Single low letters and the leading ห

The other **ten low letters**, ง ญ ณ น ม ย ร ล ว ฬ, have no high-class partner. To reach the tones a partner would provide, Thai writes a **silent ห** in front. Wikipedia calls this **ho nam**, "leading ho": the ห is not pronounced, and the syllable follows **high-class** tone rules. Thai Wikipedia shows the full set for นา: นา, หน่า, หน้า or น่า, น้า, หนา. It also notes that no current word begins with หณ or หฬ.

A rarer leader exists too. In **four words only**, Wikipedia says, a silent mid-class อ leads ย into mid-class rules: อย่า, อยาก, อย่าง and อยู่.

## How class sets the tone

Class is one of four inputs. The others are vowel length, whether the syllable is **dead** (a short vowel at the end, or a vowel plus a stop) or **live** (a long vowel, or a vowel plus a sonorant), and any tone mark. With no tone mark, Wikipedia's tone table gives these base tones:

| Syllable | Low class | Mid class | High class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Live | mid | mid | rising |
| Dead, short vowel | high | low | low |
| Dead, long vowel | falling | low | low |

Only mid-class letters take the third and fourth tone marks, *mai tri* and *mai chattawa*. [Thai tones explained](/blog/thai-tones-explained/) works through the rules with marks.

## A worked example: reading three syllables

1. **กา**: ก is mid class, the vowel is long and the syllable is live, so the tone is **mid**.
2. **ขา**: ข is high class, live, no mark, so the tone is **rising**.
3. **หนา**: น is single low, but the silent ห leads it into high-class rules, so the tone is **rising**, like ขา.

## The fastest way to memorise them

Count the lists: mid and high together are only **20 letters**, and every other consonant is low. Learn the 9 mid and 11 high letters until you know them without thinking, and treat anything else as low. Then learn the ten single low letters, because they are the ones that take a leading ห.
