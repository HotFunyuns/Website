Some basketball career games let you choose what your player does from a menu. [Basketball Career Pro League](/apps/basketball-career-pro-league/) puts the ball in your hands: you create a player, then take them onto the court with on-screen controls. This guide explains how it works, based on its Google Play listing and store screenshots, and points to the basketball guides that help you play it well.

## What kind of game it is

| | Basketball Career Pro League |
| --- | --- |
| Genre | Hands-on basketball career game |
| You control | Your player on the court, with Auto Play and simulation as options |
| Between games | Training in shooting, passing and defense |
| Tracking | A compact scoreboard during play, box scores after, league standings |
| Price | Free on Google Play; ads; no in-app purchases listed |

## Creating your player

You start by creating a player with a role to grow into, and the listing ties that role to training: focus on shooting, improve passing or make defense your calling card. [Basketball positions explained](/blog/basketball-positions-explained/) describes what each role is expected to do. Guided tutorial steps then introduce the controls before games start to count.

## Playing the games

On court, the listing describes moving your player, shooting, passing, sprinting into position, going for steals and fighting for rebounds. The store screenshots add the menus around it: player career, quick play, team play, a season and GM mode, team development, and missions and daily rewards, plus 82-game team seasons and the option to play any position.

Three ways to get through a season:

1. **Play directly**, controlling every possession you want to.
2. **Auto Play**, to watch AI-controlled basketball.
3. **Simulation**, to move the season forward faster.

Selectable camera views, including a wider **Broadcast** view, change how much of the court you can see. A wider view helps you read movement away from the ball, which matters for passing and help defense.

## Training between games

Training is where the career is built. The listing names three skills, **shooting, passing and defense**, and describes the loop that ties them to the games: play, read the box score, then train whatever the numbers say is missing. The store screenshots describe a path from youth prospect through high school and college to the pro game.

## Choosing when to play, watch or simulate

The three options suit different goals. Playing a game yourself is how you learn the controls and how your training shows up, so it is worth doing for the games that test your role: a close game, a strong opponent, a stretch where you are working on one skill. Auto Play lets you watch AI-controlled basketball and, as the listing puts it, watch your player work with the team. Simulation keeps a long season moving when you want the next milestone more than the next game. Mixing the three is the point of the design, rather than a shortcut.

## Reading your box score

The box score after each game is the game's feedback, and the listing suggests comparing your scoring, shooting and passing contributions and letting defense matter alongside offense. Read it for the role you picked rather than for points alone: if your player is a passer, the assist column is the one to compare from game to game. [How to read a basketball box score](/blog/how-to-read-a-basketball-box-score/) explains every column, and two guides help you use it:

- [Basketball shot selection](/blog/basketball-shot-selection/) explains why some shots are worth more than others, using points per shot.
- [Double-doubles and triple-doubles](/blog/double-double-triple-double/) explains the milestone stat lines that all-round players chase.

## A sensible first season

1. **Pick a role and stick to it** for a season, so you can see training pay off.
2. **Learn the controls in quick play** before career games.
3. **Use the Broadcast camera** when you are learning to pass.
4. **Read the box score after every game**, and train the weakest skill for your role.
5. **Simulate the games you do not need to play**, and play the ones that matter.
