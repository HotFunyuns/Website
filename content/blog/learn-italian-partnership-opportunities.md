The app's Google Play listing names beginners, travellers, students and heritage learners among the people it is for. That puts three kinds of organisation close to [Learn Italian for Beginners](/apps/learn-italian/): **language schools**, **travel companies** and **cultural institutes**. This page explains what the app offers each of them, what Reign Creative LLC is willing to discuss, and what would happen if a deal involved the app changing hands.

## A defined course, not a word list

The Google Play listing describes **12 structured units and 192 core lessons**, covering greetings, family, food, numbers, daily routines, shopping, travel, work, health, relationships, past events, future plans and everyday communication. Each lesson mixes **vocabulary exercises, multiple choice, sentence building, matching, dictation, translation, listening, pronunciation and grammar challenges**. Grammar covers verbs, articles, sentence structure, agreement, questions and common tenses, revisited in later units.

Around the core path, a **Review area** brings back difficult words and past mistakes through flashcards, listening challenges, dictation, matching and speed practice. Further material covers **idioms, slang, business and workplace Italian, culture and etiquette, and roleplay conversations**, and **Italian speakers can study English** with separate lessons and progress. The app is free with ads; a **Premium** purchase removes ads and unlocks the Premium learning features.

## For language schools

A structured path is easier for a teacher to plan around than an open-ended app. A school could:

- **assign units as homework** that mirrors the classroom syllabus;
- **use the Review area** for revision before assessments;
- **point advanced beginners** to the business Italian and roleplay material;
- **serve Italian-speaking students** learning English with the reverse course.

Where a school wants its own branding or extra units, the conversation turns to **licensing**, **co-development** or a **white-label version where technically feasible**.

## For travel companies

The listing's travel material covers **restaurants, airports, train stations, shops and hotels**. A tour operator, cruise line or study-abroad provider might consider:

- a **pre-departure pathway**, pointing customers to the travel lessons in the weeks before a trip;
- **sponsorship** or **cross-promotion** inside a booking journey;
- **co-branded material** built around a specific destination's vocabulary.

The app is free to download, so a traveller can start without a purchase; the partnership is about how it is presented.

## For cultural institutes and publishers

Cultural institutes run courses, events and exhibitions; publishers produce textbooks and readers. The app's **culture and etiquette** lessons and audio for words, phrases and conversations could sit alongside either. Reign Creative LLC is open to discussing **publishing and distribution**, **localisation**, **integration** with capabilities the app already has, and **educational and institutional partnerships**.

## What the app's own pages do not promise

Partners should describe the app the way its page on this site does: a **structured beginner foundation** in vocabulary, grammar, listening and pronunciation, **not a substitute for conversation practice** with fluent speakers. Some lessons belong to the optional Premium purchase. Co-branded copy would follow those limits.

## Acquisition and the Google Play transfer

Reign Creative LLC is also open to serious discussions about **acquiring this app alone, alongside other language apps, or with the portfolio**. This page publishes no prices, valuations, revenue or user numbers, and it is not an offer.

Selling an Android app is not a matter of handing over files. Google requires the move to go through its own **developer-account transfer**, and its Play Console help flags several points that matter for this particular app. Because Learn Italian for Beginners sells a Premium upgrade, the buyer's account would need an **active payments profile**, and Google notes that if the buyer's account uses a different default currency, an app with in-app purchases is unpublished after the transfer until the buyer has verified the new prices and published it again. The listing, its reviews and its users travel with the app; the seller's **earnings reports do not**, and **orders placed before the move remain with the seller's account**. Apps that show ads also need their **ad integrations updated** after the move. Once the buyer approves the request, Google's team says it replies **within two business days**.
