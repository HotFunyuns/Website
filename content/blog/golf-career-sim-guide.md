Almost every golf video game asks the same question: can you stop a moving bar in the right place? It is a fine question, and it has almost nothing to do with what makes golf difficult.

[Golf Career Simulator Pro Tour](/apps/golf-career-simulator/) asks a different one. Given this hole, this lie, this distance, this wind and this position on the leaderboard, what are you going to try? The execution is handled by your golfer's skills. The decision is yours, and it is where the game lives.

## What replaces the swing meter

Each shot presents a situation and a set of ways to play it. The result is weighted by your golfer's skills, the conditions, and the risk in the line you chose.

That changes the texture of the game in three ways:

- **You cannot rescue a bad decision with good hands.** There is no execution skill to fall back on. If you take on a line your golfer is not equipped for, the odds were bad before you started.
- **Consistency becomes a strategy rather than a personality trait.** Choosing the percentage play repeatedly is not passive; it is a scoring method.
- **Pressure is a real variable.** Momentum, confidence and fatigue move within a round, and the model accounts for them, so the same choice on the first tee and the seventy-first hole are not equivalent.

## The seven skills, honestly ranked

Your golfer develops across driving, accuracy, irons, short game, putting, composure and consistency. They are not equally valuable per point spent.

| Skill | What it buys | Early-career value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Putting | Turns greens in regulation into birdies and misses into pars | Highest |
| Short game | Rescues the holes where the approach missed | Very high |
| Accuracy | Keeps you playing from places you have shots from | High |
| Irons | Better approach outcomes, more birdie looks | High |
| Composure | Reduces the penalty from pressure and momentum swings | Moderate, rising with event size |
| Consistency | Narrows the spread of your outcomes | Moderate, compounding |
| Driving | Distance, which mostly changes which club you approach with | Lowest early |

The reason driving ranks last is not that distance is worthless. It is that in a scoring model, the strokes are lost around and on the green, and a skill that reduces losses is worth more than one that increases upside. A player who never three-putts and gets up and down half the time scores better than a longer hitter who does neither.

**Consistency deserves a note of its own.** It narrows the distribution of your results rather than shifting its centre. That is a strange thing to buy early — it does not make good rounds better — but it makes disasters rarer, and disasters are what miss cuts.

## Risk is priced by the state of the tournament

Aggressive and conservative lines are both correct, and the variable that decides which is not the hole. It is what a bad outcome costs *right now*.

Three cases:

**Round one, par five, decent lie.** A bad outcome costs a stroke and some confidence. There are 63 holes to recover. Take the line.

**Round two, cut line in sight, water short of the green.** A bad outcome costs the week. Play to the fat part of the green and take your four.

**Final round, one back, par five, last chance.** A bad outcome costs a placing you were unlikely to hold anyway. The conservative play has a low ceiling that is not high enough. Take the line.

The general rule: **risk is cheap when there is time to recover and when the safe play cannot win. It is expensive in the middle of a tournament you are surviving rather than winning.**

## Cuts, and why the first two rounds are about survival

Multi-round events include cuts, leaderboards, ties and playoffs. A cut removes most of the field after a set number of rounds, and once you are out, nothing else about the week happens.

Two consequences that change how you should play:

1. **Round one and two are variance-management rounds.** You cannot win the event there, and you can absolutely lose it. Minimise the chance of a card-wrecking hole.
2. **A missed cut costs more than a poor finish.** No ranking points, no earnings, and a lost week of tour progression. Ranking and earnings decide which events you can enter next season, so missed cuts compound.

This is the strategic heart of the game, and it is the part that most closely mirrors real professional golf. The rules of the actual sport — stroke play scoring, penalty areas, relief procedures — are published by the [USGA](https://www.usga.org/rules-hub.html) and the R&A; the game borrows the *shape* of stroke play rather than any specific rule set.

## The tour season is the actual campaign

Finishes move rankings, earnings and reputation. Those decide which events are open to you next season, which decides how good the fields you are competing in are, which decides how hard it is to finish well.

That loop is why a mediocre season is not a neutral outcome — it narrows next season's schedule. It is also why a single strong finish early can change the shape of a whole year.

## Who this is for

Golf Career Simulator Pro Tour suits people who like risk-versus-reward decisions, long-arc progression and a sport that is not one of the big team games. It does not suit someone who wants to feel a swing — there is no swing to feel.
