[Cricket Sport Career Simulator](/apps/cricket-career-simulator/), Reign Creative's first cricket game, puts you in one player's boots rather than in charge of a team or a franchise. Its Google Play listing describes the premise in three steps: create your cricketer, improve their skills, and progress through matches. This guide explains how each part works, based on the listing and the store screenshots summarised on the app's page on this site, and points to the cricket guides that help you play it better.

The listing describes cricket as a sport of batting, bowling, runs and wickets, and says a lasting career takes more than a single memorable performance.

## Creating your cricketer

The store screenshots show the "Create your cricketer" screen:

1. **Appearance**, so the player is recognisably yours.
2. **Role and style**, the decision that shapes everything after it. [Cricket player roles](/blog/cricket-player-roles/) explains what openers, middle-order batters, bowlers, all-rounders and keepers each do.
3. **Overall rating and potential**, where your player starts and how far they might go.
4. **Strengths to develop**, choosing what to build first.
5. **Randomise**, if you would rather start from a surprise.

## The skill groups

Development is split into groups, according to the store screenshots:

| Skill group | What it relates to on the field |
| --- | --- |
| Batting technique and scoring | Defence, shot-making and run rate; see [cricket batting shots](/blog/cricket-batting-shots/) |
| Pace bowling | Speed, swing and seam |
| Spin bowling | Turn, drift and variations; see [pace versus spin bowling](/blog/pace-vs-spin-bowling/) |
| Fielding and wicket-keeping | Catching, ground fielding and keeping |
| Athleticism | Fitness, speed and movement in the field and between the wickets |
| Cricket IQ | Reading the game and making decisions |

Because role and skills are separate choices, you can lean into a specialist or spread your development to build an all-rounder.

## How matches play

The screenshots describe matches as a mix of:

- **Interactive match decisions**, where you make the key calls;
- **a cricket field view**, showing the action from above;
- **fast match simulation**, for the parts of the match you do not play out;
- **live match stats**, so you can follow your player's numbers as they happen;
- **energy, morale and momentum**, which affect how a performance goes.

Knowing the Laws makes those decisions clearer. [Cricket rules for beginners](/blog/cricket-rules-for-beginners/) covers the basics, and [cricket stats explained](/blog/cricket-stats-explained/) shows how averages and strike rates are calculated.

## Building a career

Around the matches, the screenshots show a **career command centre**, **coach and team choices**, **daily career goals**, **rewards and recognition**, and **rivals** to face as you rise through the ranks.

## A sensible first career

1. **Pick a role you understand.** Role and style are chosen at creation, so it helps to read what each role does in a real team first.
2. **Develop the skills that match the role.** A batter's job draws on batting technique and scoring; a spinner's on spin bowling.
3. **Watch energy and morale.** The screenshots tie performance to condition and momentum, so a player's condition is part of every match.
4. **Use daily goals** to give each session a target.
5. **Follow the live match stats** to see how your player's numbers develop.

## Limits worth knowing

- The listing does not say which formats, Test, one-day or T20, the matches follow, so check in the app if you want a specific format.
- It is a career simulator: you make decisions rather than playing every delivery with a bat-swing control.
- No online multiplayer is described in the listing.
