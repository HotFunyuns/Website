Italian prepositions are short words with wide meanings, and five of them refuse to stand next to a definite article without merging into it. Wikipedia's Italian grammar article describes a closed class of basic prepositions: *di, a, da, in, su, con, per, tra* and *fra*. Learn what each one covers and how the merged forms are built, and a large share of everyday sentences opens up. This explainer takes the meanings first, then the contractions, then the exceptions.

## What the nine basic prepositions mean

Wikipedia's tables gloss them like this, and Wiktionary's entries fill in the main uses.

| Preposition | Core meanings | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| di | of, 's, from (origin), than | *la coda del cane*, the dog's tail |
| a | to, at | *vive a Roma*, she lives in Rome |
| da | from, since, by, at someone's place | *andare dal dottore*, to go to the doctor's |
| in | in, to, into, by (transport) | *in autobus*, by bus |
| su | on, about, out of | *sulla tavola*, on the table |
| con | with | *con il* or, optionally, *col* |
| per | for, through | *per strada*, a fixed phrase with no article |
| tra / fra | between, among | *tra fratelli*, among brothers |

Some uses reach beyond the English glosses. Wiktionary gives *di* for comparison, *Jack è più alto di sua moglie*, "Jack is taller than his wife", and for "some" with the article: *Vuoi dell'acqua?*, "Would you like some water?". It gives *su* for proportions: *un europeo su cinque*, "one European in five". And *in* covers shops and transport: *Sono andato in panetteria*, "I went to the bakery", and *Vado a scuola in autobus*, "I go to school by bus".

## Di or da: two ways to say "from"

The two look alike and both can translate "from". Wiktionary's entries separate them.

**Di** is mostly about belonging and description: possession (*L'ira di Apollo*), authorship (*la Divina Commedia di Dante*), topic (*libri di matematica*), material (*una collana d'oro*), and origin with *essere*: *Lei è di Monreale*, "She's from Monreale".

**Da** is about starting points and functions. Wiktionary lists:

- **from** a place or source: *dalla terra alla luna*
- **since**: *da quando?*, "since when?"
- **at or to someone's place**: *da Giovanni*, "at Giovanni's"
- **by** an agent: *saccheggiato dai ladri*, "looted by thieves"
- **purpose**: *una macchina da scrivere*, a typewriter, literally "a machine for writing"
- **necessity** before an infinitive: *un bel libro da leggere*, "a nice book to read"

## The 35 contracted forms

Wikipedia states that in modern Italian **di, a, da, in and su must combine** with a definite article placed after them. The result is one word.

| | il | lo | la | l' | i | gli | le |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **di** | del | dello | della | dell' | dei | degli | delle |
| **a** | al | allo | alla | all' | ai | agli | alle |
| **da** | dal | dallo | dalla | dall' | dai | dagli | dalle |
| **in** | nel | nello | nella | nell' | nei | negli | nelle |
| **su** | sul | sullo | sulla | sull' | sui | sugli | sulle |

Read across the rows and the pattern is regular. *Di* becomes *de-* and *in* becomes *ne-*; *a*, *da* and *su* keep their shape. *Il* shrinks to *-l* (*al*, *del*, *nel*), *i* stays *-i* (*ai*, *dei*, *nei*), *gli* stays *-gli* (*agli*, *negli*), and *lo*, *la*, *l'* and *le* double their l (*allo*, *della*, *nell'*, *sulle*).

To know which article to fuse, you need the noun's gender and its first letter, which is the job of [Italian articles and gender](/blog/italian-articles-and-gender/). Five prepositions times seven articles gives 5 × 7 = **35** forms, but if you know the seven articles you are really learning five prefixes.

## Taking four phrases apart

Run the table backwards on phrases quoted in Wiktionary's entries and each contraction resolves into a preposition and an article.

1. *la coda **del** cane*, "the dog's tail": *del* = *di* + *il*, because *cane* is masculine and starts with a consonant.
2. ***dalla** terra **alla** luna*, "from the Earth to the moon": *dalla* = *da* + *la* and *alla* = *a* + *la*, both feminine.
3. *Il libro è **sulla** tavola*, "the book is on the table": *sulla* = *su* + *la*.
4. *Vuoi **dell'**acqua?*, "would you like some water?": *dell'* = *di* + *l'*, because *acqua* starts with a vowel.

Four phrases, five contractions, and every one of them sits in the 35-cell table above.

## When there is no contraction

Several cases break the pattern.

- **Con and per** have optional contractions (*col, coi, pel, pei* and so on). Wikipedia notes that apart from *col* and *coi*, which are occasionally used, these are archaic and very rare. Write *con il* and *per la*.
- **Tra and fra** are interchangeable, Wikipedia says, often chosen by sound: *tra fratelli*, but *fra i tralicci* to avoid *tra tra-*.
- **No article at all.** Wikipedia notes that, under the influence of Latin, which had no articles, some fixed phrases drop the article: *a capo*, *da capo*, *di colpo*, *in bicicletta*, *per strada*.
- **Su di before pronouns.** *Su* becomes *su di* before a pronoun: *su di te*, "on you".
- **Su di before u.** Some speakers also say *su di* before a word starting with u, as in *su di un cavallo*; Wikipedia notes grammarians regard this as incorrect.
