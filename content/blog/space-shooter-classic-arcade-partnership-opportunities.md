[Space Galaxy Attack Arcade](/apps/space-shooter-classic-arcade/) is a wave-based space shooter built around dodging as much as firing. This page is for **arcade publishers** and **cross-promotion partners**, and it covers the game, the deal shapes that fit, and the route to a licence or sale.

## Movement first, firepower second

The app's page on this site describes hostile fleets filling the screen with projectiles, where **the safe route changes every few seconds**, and the listing asks players to "choose the safest path through enemy attacks". Staying alive comes down to positioning; firing is only part of the job.

Players manage four abilities:

| Ability | Role in a run |
| --- | --- |
| FIRE | Main weapons |
| ALT | A secondary attack |
| Ultimate | A charged attack for bosses or bad waves |
| Shield | Protection when the screen closes in |

Rewards earned during a run go into **stronger weapons, unlocked upgrades, improved systems and chosen bonuses**. Enemy models **move and attack differently**, bosses must be learned rather than out-gunned, and **Endless Mode** removes the finish line. The game is free with ads and optional in-app purchases.

## Why it suits arcade publishers

Three features matter to a publisher. **The controls take seconds to learn**, according to the app's page on this site. **Runs fit into a short break**, which suits casual sessions. And **progression carries between runs**, so players have a reason to return after a failed attempt. A publisher could discuss **publishing and distribution**, **localisation** of the interface text, **licensing** and **white-label versions where technically feasible**.

## How cross-promotion could work

One shape for cross-promotion pairs two games whose audiences overlap without competing head-on: an arcade shooter and a puzzle game, for example, or two titles from different genres with similar session lengths. In practice it might mean:

- a promotional slot in each game pointing to the other;
- a shared themed event, such as a ship skin inspired by a partner's art;
- joint seasonal campaigns.

The app's page on this site names its audience: **arcade shooter players who enjoy dodging**, **people who want runs that fit a short break**, players who like **building a stronger ship** between attempts, and **bullet hell fans who want readable patterns**. A promotion partner should be honest about whether its players look like that.

## The sibling game

Reign Creative also publishes **Space Galaxy Attack Hardcore**, a separate vertical shooter with a sector campaign and a Hardcore difficulty mode that raises enemy durability, projectile speed and formation complexity. The two could be offered together, promoted to each other's players, or discussed separately. A partner interested in both should say so early, because it changes the shape of a deal.

## Limits a partner should state

The app's page on this site notes that the game is **run-based**: upgrades carry over, but **each run starts from the first wave**. Ads support the free version, and in-app purchases are optional. Partners describing the game would say "arcade runs with lasting upgrades", not "a story campaign", and would not suggest that purchases are needed to progress.

## A worked example: a two-week cross-promotion

A puzzle game and Space Galaxy Attack Arcade agree a 14-day cross-promotion. Each shows the other's promotion at most once per day after a completed session, so a daily player sees it no more than 14 times across the campaign. Both games label the slot as a promotion. At the end, each side reviews only the aggregate results its own analytics allow, and nothing about individual players is exchanged. The terms, including frequency caps and disclosure, would be written down before launch.

## Google Play transfer basics

A sale would move the game through Google Play's **developer-account transfer**. Google's help lists the conditions and consequences: active accounts on both sides, registration transaction IDs, and a payments profile on the receiving account for a game with in-app products. Users, statistics, ratings and reviews move with the game; **orders created before the transfer, promotions and test groups** do not. For a game that shows ads, **ad SDK integrations must be updated** so ad traffic is credited to the correct account.
