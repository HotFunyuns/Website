To keep score, draw a grid with one row for each batter in lineup order and one column for each inning. In each box, write how that plate appearance ended using the fielders' position numbers — **6-3** for a ground ball to the shortstop thrown to first, **7** for a fly ball caught in left field, **K** for a strikeout — and mark every base the runner reaches in the corner of his box. Circle the runs, total runs and hits at the end of each inning, and the game score builds itself.

That is the method in [MLB's scorekeeping glossary](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/miscellaneous/scorekeeping), which is honest about one thing beginners should hear early: "Different fans have different methods of keeping a scorecard, and many use their own notations." There is no single official notation for fans. What is official is the record the league's scorer produces, and the last part of this guide shows how your card connects to it.

## Before the first pitch

Set the card up in three steps:

1. **Write the lineup in batting order**, one batter per row, with each fielder's position number beside his name. The designated hitter is usually listed as DH, since he never fields.
2. **Leave room under each name for substitutes.** The official score report marks substitutes with letters and notes such as which inning they entered, and you will need the space by the late innings.
3. **Write your key.** Decide your symbols now and keep them for the whole game.

The position numbers are fixed: 1 pitcher, 2 catcher, 3 first base, 4 second base, 5 third base, 6 shortstop, 7 left field, 8 center field, 9 right field.

## The marks you will use most

| Event | MLB's suggested mark | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single | S or - | Written in the first-base corner |
| Double | D or = | Written in the second-base corner |
| Home run | HR | |
| Walk / intentional walk | BB / IBB | |
| Hit by pitch | HP | |
| Strikeout | K | MLB's list gives a called third strike a separate line of its own |
| Sacrifice bunt / sacrifice fly | SH or SAC / SF | |
| Fielder's choice | FC | |
| Error | E | Many scorers add the fielder's number, as in E6 |
| Wild pitch / passed ball | WP / PB | |
| Stolen base | SB | |
| Double play | GIDP | |
| Forceout | FO | |
| Unassisted | U | As in 3U |
| Foul fly / line drive | F / L | |

Two conventions you will meet that are not in that list, and that vary between scorers:

- **A backwards K for a called third strike.** It is one common way to fill MLB's separate "called out on strikes" line.
- **F before a fly out, as in F8.** MLB's simple method writes a caught fly to center as just "8" and uses F for a foul fly, so under that key F2 is a foul pop caught by the catcher. Other scorers write F8 for any fly out and L8 for a line drive. Either works; mixing them in one game does not.

Each box also works as a little diamond. In MLB's method the lower-right corner is first base, the upper-right second, the upper-left third and the bottom home, and a runner's progress is drawn corner by corner as he advances.

## Scoring one half-inning, step by step

Here is an invented top of the first, five batters long, scored the way MLB's glossary describes.

1. **Batter 1 grounds to the shortstop, who throws him out.** Write 6-3. Many scorers also write a small 1 in the box to show it was the first out.
2. **Batter 2 walks.** BB in the first-base corner of his box.
3. **Batter 3 singles to right, and the runner goes from first to third.** Draw the single in batter 3's first-base corner. In batter 2's box, mark the advance in the second- and third-base corners with a 3, for the hitter who moved him.
4. **Batter 4 hits a fly ball to center, caught, and the runner tags up and scores.** Batter 4's box gets SF8 and the second out. At the bottom of batter 2's box, draw a circle with a 4 inside it, MLB's way of showing who drove the run in.
5. **Batter 5 strikes out.** K, third out. Batter 3 is left on first.

Inning total: one run, one hit. That small sequence already touches most of the scoring rules worth knowing:

- **The sacrifice fly is not an at-bat.** Rule 9.02(a)(1) excludes sacrifice bunts and sacrifice flies from times at bat, along with walks, hit batters and awards for interference, obstruction or a shift violation. Rule 9.04 still credits a run batted in for a run that scores on a sacrifice fly, so batter 4 gets an RBI and no at-bat.
- **The groundout gives credit to two fielders.** Under Rule 9.10 the shortstop earns an assist for the throw, and under Rule 9.09 the first baseman earns the putout.
- **The strikeout is the catcher's putout.** Rule 9.09(b) credits the catcher with an automatic putout when a batter is out on strikes.

## The calls that belong to the official scorer

Some boxes you cannot fill in until someone else decides. Rule 9.01 of the [2026 Official Baseball Rules](https://mktg.mlbstatic.com/mlb/official-information/2026-official-baseball-rules.pdf) has the Office of the Commissioner appoint an official scorer for each Major League game, with "sole authority" over judgment calls such as whether a batter reached on a hit or an error. The scorer relays each decision to the league's data staff and the press box, and to the public address announcer on request. If you are unsure, leave the box in pencil until the ruling comes.

The standard behind most of those calls is **ordinary effort**, defined as "the effort that a fielder of average skill at a position in that league or classification of leagues should exhibit on a play." Three consequences are worth knowing:

- **An error does not need a touch.** Under Rule 9.12, a ground ball through a fielder's legs or a fly ball that drops untouched can be an error if ordinary effort would have made the play.
- **Slow is not an error.** The same rule says that fielding a ball cleanly but throwing too late is not a misplay.
- **A strikeout can put a man on base.** Rule 9.15 scores a strikeout even when the batter reaches because the third strike was not caught. Write K, then how he reached — many scorers use K-WP or K-PB.

Innings pitched follow the same out-by-out logic: the comment to Rule 9.02(c)(1) counts each putout as one third of an inning, so a reliever who records two outs is credited with two thirds of an inning.

## From scorecard to box score

A box score is built from the records in the official score report, and Rule 9.02 lists what that report must contain. The first four batting items are times at bat, runs, hits and runs batted in, which is why box scores open with AB, R, H and RBI. The report goes on to doubles, triples, home runs, stolen bases, walks, strikeouts and more; for each fielder, putouts, assists and errors; and for each pitcher, innings pitched, hits, runs, earned runs, walks, strikeouts, wild pitches and balks.

Everything on that list can be counted from a well-kept card:

| Box score column | Where it comes from on your card |
| --- | --- |
| AB | Plate appearances, minus walks, hit batters, sacrifices and awards for interference, obstruction or a shift violation |
| R | Circled runs in the batter's own box |
| H | Singles, doubles, triples and home runs |
| RBI | The batter's number written inside other runners' circles |
| PO / A | The last number of each out (putout) and the numbers before it (assists) |
| E | Every E you wrote, by fielder |

### Proving the box score

Rule 9.03(c) gives a check that every scorer should run at the end of a game. In the rule's own terms, a box score "balances" when the two sides of this equation match:

```
AB + BB + HBP + SH + SF + (first base awarded for interference,
                           obstruction or a shift violation)
   = R + LOB + the opposing team's putouts
```

Here AB is at-bats, BB walks, HBP hit batters, SH sacrifice bunts, SF sacrifice flies, R runs and LOB runners left on base. The logic is that every batter who comes to the plate and finishes his turn ends up in exactly one of three places: he scores, he is stranded, or he is put out. Try it on the half-inning above. The left side is 3 at-bats + 1 walk + 1 sacrifice fly = 5. The right side is 1 run + 1 runner left on base + 3 putouts = 5. It balances. Over a full nine-inning defensive game, the opposing team's putouts will total 27, three for each inning.

If your totals do not match, one plate appearance has been miscounted, and the proof tells you to go looking before the details fade.

## Reading the line under a player's name

Box scores and broadcasts often compress a hitter into a slash line. MLB's [slash line entry](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/miscellaneous/slash-line) defines it as batting average, on-base percentage and slugging percentage in that order, separated by slashes, with on-base plus slugging (OPS) the sum of the last two. If those terms are new, [baseball stats explained for beginners](/blog/baseball-stats-explained-for-beginners/) works through each formula with an example. Your card already holds the inputs — at-bats, hits by type, walks, hit batters and sacrifice flies — and the [batting stats calculator](/tools/baseball/batting-average-calculator/) turns them into a slash line.
