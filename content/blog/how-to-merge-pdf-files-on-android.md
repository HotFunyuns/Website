Merging is a PDF job that tends to turn up on a phone at awkward moments: an application that wants one file, a set of receipts for an expense claim, a scanned contract that came out as five separate files. The job itself takes seconds. Getting a clean result takes a little preparation. This guide covers both, on Android.

## What merging does

Merging joins several PDFs into **one sequential document**. Adobe's help for its desktop Acrobat describes the same idea: preview, arrange, move or delete pages, then create a single PDF. Nothing about the pages themselves needs to change; they are placed one after another in the order you choose. That makes merging safe to repeat, but it also means any problem in a source file, such as a sideways scan, is carried into the result.

## Before you merge

A minute of preparation saves re-doing the whole file:

1. **Collect the files in one place**, such as your Downloads folder, so you are not hunting for them mid-task.
2. **Fix pages in the source files**: rotate sideways scans and remove blank or duplicate pages.
3. **Decide the order** on paper or in your head: cover letter, form, then supporting documents is one sensible sequence.
4. **Check sizes**: a merged file is roughly the sum of its parts.

## Merging on Android, step by step

In [PDF All in One Tool No Limit](/apps/pdf-all-in-one-tools/), the merge tool joins several files into one document. Screens and button labels can change between app versions and look different on different Android devices, so read these as the stages rather than exact taps:

1. Open the app and choose the **merge** tool.
2. **Add the files** you want to combine.
3. **Arrange them** in the order they should appear.
4. **Combine** and save the result as a new file, keeping the originals until you have checked it.
5. Use the **page tools** to rearrange, rotate, remove or extract individual pages if something is still out of place.

The same order works in any PDF app with a merge feature: add, arrange, combine, check.

## A worked example: will it fit in an email?

You need to send three files: a 9 MB scanned form, an 8 MB set of scanned receipts and a 10 MB PDF of photos. Merged, they come to about **27 MB**. Google's Gmail help says personal accounts have a **25 MB** attachment limit, and that Gmail turns larger attachments into Google Drive links instead. To send the merged file as a normal attachment, compress it first, or compress the two scanned files before merging. [How to compress a PDF](/blog/how-to-compress-a-pdf/) explains the trade-offs.

## Check the result

Before sending, scroll through the merged PDF once:

- **Order**: are the sections where you intended?
- **Orientation**: are any pages sideways or upside down?
- **Completeness**: is the page count the sum of the originals?
- **Duplicates or blanks**: did a blank page sneak in from a scan?

Then **rename** the file to something descriptive, such as "2026-10 expense claim receipts.pdf". The app's PDF manager lets you review, organise and rename files before sharing.

## Mixed sources, mixed page sizes

Files from different places may not match. A form from a US website may be letter size, a document from Europe A4, and a phone scan whatever shape the camera captured. Where a merge keeps each page's own size, the combined file may change shape from page to page. That is not an error, but if the recipient expects a uniform document, scan or export everything at one size before merging. The same applies to orientation: a landscape spreadsheet page among portrait pages is fine, as long as it is the right way up.

## Keep a clean record

A few habits make merged files easier to live with later:

- **Keep the originals** in a folder until the merged version has been accepted, so you can rebuild it if something is wrong.
- **Use dated, descriptive names**, starting with the year and month, so files sort in order.
- **Save a new version** rather than overwriting when you correct a merged file, for example "v2", and delete the old one once the new one is sent.

## Check what you are sharing

Merging makes it easy to send more than you meant to. Before sharing, make sure the file does not contain pages from another document that were in the same folder, an earlier draft or a page with personal details the recipient does not need. Removing a page takes seconds with the page tools; recalling an email does not.
