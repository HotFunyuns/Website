Collectible creatures appear in toys, cartoons and games alike, and [Dragon Pet Evolve Hatch Eggs](/apps/dragon-pet-evolve/) is built around them: eggs, evolutions, an album to complete and seasonal arrivals. This page explains, for **character and toy brands** and **casual-game publishers**, how the game works, where a collaboration would slot in, and the rules on chance-based rewards.

## From egg to battle team

Seen from a partner's side, the game has four loops that a collaboration can plug into. **Collection**: eggs hatch into dragons belonging to one of seven elements, and an album records every species a player has found. **Care**: short daily attention, which the listing says speeds growth. **Growth**: staged evolutions and a breeding cave that produces hybrid eggs. **Play**: an island to decorate and expand, idle production while the player is away, and turn-based fights on adventure maps and in an arena where element match-ups matter. According to the listing, the essentials run without Wi-Fi. The game is free with ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases.

## The seasonal event slot

The listing mentions that **seasonal events add limited dragons, eggs and decorations**. For a brand, that is the obvious entry point: a collaboration released as an event, with its own creature, egg design and island decorations, and a defined start and end.

Reign Creative LLC is also open to **licensing**, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, **sponsorship** and **cross-promotion**.

## Chance, and what promotions can say

One limit matters more than any other for a brand. The app's page on this site says **rare and legendary eggs depend on chance, so a particular dragon is not guaranteed**. A promotion therefore cannot promise that every player gets a featured rare dragon from a random egg. If a brand wants everyone to receive its creature, it would be designed as an event reward rather than a random drop, and that design choice would be agreed up front.

The listing also says some rare dragons evolve into **secret forms "only dedicated trainers will see"**, so it is a long-term collection game. Marketing would not suggest that everything is quick to unlock. For a family-facing brand in particular, clear wording about what is guaranteed, what is earned through play and what depends on luck is part of the deal, not an afterthought, and it belongs in the agreement alongside the artwork and the dates.

## A worked example: a two-week toy launch event

A toy company launches a storm-element dragon figure. The matching in-game event runs for 14 days. Every player who completes five care tasks during the event receives the storm dragon egg as a guaranteed reward, not a random drop. Island decorations themed on the toy's packaging are available through event play, and the album gains one new entry. The listing's offline claim covers core features, so whether event content also works offline would be confirmed during development. Rights, artwork approvals and the event window would all be in a signed agreement.

## Transfer steps on Google Play

A sale is carried out through Google Play's **app transfer to a different developer account**. The help page sets out the conditions: active accounts on both sides, registration transaction IDs, and an active payments profile for a game with in-app products. Google explains that users, ratings, reviews and the store listing move with the game, that **bulk export and earnings reports do not**, and that **ad SDK integrations must be updated** in the app so ad traffic is credited to the correct account. If the game uses Google Play game services, Google's help says to contact its support team for help transferring that project.
