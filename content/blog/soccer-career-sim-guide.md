Most football career games get judged on one question: does getting into the team feel earned? [Football Career Soccer XI Sim](/apps/soccer-career-sim-xi/) answers it with a mechanic rather than a rating — Coach Trust — and once you understand how that interacts with everything else, the rest of the career becomes legible.

This guide covers the six positions, how trust and playing time compound, and how to think about the transfer decisions that most players get wrong.

## Six positions, and what each one actually asks

You can play as a goalkeeper, defender, midfielder, winger, forward or striker, developing attributes including finishing, passing, dribbling, pace, strength, defending, vision, stamina and goalkeeping.

| Position | Attributes that carry it | The decision you make most | Hardest part |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goalkeeper | Goalkeeping, positioning | Hold, claim or spread | Statistics depend heavily on the team in front of you |
| Defender | Defending, strength, positioning | Press, hold the line, or step out | Success is invisible; failure is not |
| Midfielder | Passing, vision, stamina | Recycle or progress | Every option is available, so choosing badly is easy |
| Winger | Pace, dribbling | Take the man on or release early | High variance; a bad run erases a good one |
| Forward / Striker | Finishing, movement | Shoot, hold, or run in behind | Chances are scarce, so each one is expensive |

A first career is easiest as a winger or forward, because the feedback between a decision and a result is immediate. A first career is *most instructive* in midfield, because that is where the game asks the widest range of questions.

Understanding what the shape around you is trying to achieve helps considerably. [Soccer formations explained](/blog/soccer-formations-explained/) covers what each system buys and what it gives up, which is a useful lens on why your manager keeps asking you to do a specific job.

## Coach Trust is the gate on everything

The progression ladder runs prospect → rotation player → starter → key player → superstar → club icon, and Coach Trust is what moves you along it.

Three things follow:

**Consistency beats brilliance.** A run of solid, low-error performances raises trust more reliably than one outstanding match surrounded by anonymous ones. The model is measuring dependability, which is also what selection in the real sport measures.

**Trust is slower to rebuild than to lose.** A poor spell costs more trust than an equally good spell earns back. Plan around that: it means the right time to take risks is when you are established, not when you are fighting for a place.

**Playing time is the development engine.** Minutes produce situations, situations produce experience and attribute points, and those produce better performances. A player who gets into the XI a season earlier is not one season ahead — they are compounding.

## The transfer decision most players get wrong

Perform well and clubs come for you. The interface makes the offers look comparable, and they are not. Four things vary independently:

1. **Contract value.** The number everyone looks at first.
2. **Playing time.** The number that actually decides your development.
3. **Team quality.** Affects trophies, and also how easily you shine.
4. **Your role.** Whether you arrive as the answer or as competition.

The failure mode is joining a stronger club as a rotation player at 22. The wage is better and the season is worse: fewer minutes, slower development, and a trust score starting from zero at a club with better alternatives to you.

**A rule that holds up well:** before you are an established starter, optimise for minutes. Once you are, optimise for the level you are playing at. Loyalty is a legitimate strategy too — staying and becoming a club icon is an achievable arc, not a consolation prize.

## Match decisions and why the same choice is not always right

During matches you choose whether to pass, shoot, dribble, cross, press, tackle, make a run or attempt the riskier option. The outcome is weighted by your attributes, position, energy, morale, the match situation and the difficulty setting.

Energy and morale are the two people forget. Late in a match with low energy, the dribble that worked in the twentieth minute is a different proposition — and the model treats it that way. Similarly, a risky ball at 0-0 in the eightieth minute and the same ball at 3-0 in the fiftieth carry different costs.

The transferable habit: **before choosing, ask what the failure costs right now.** That question resolves most in-game decisions on its own.

## The career around the football

Transfers, contract negotiations, free agency, rivalries, teammate and coach relationships, injuries and comebacks, press conferences, social media reaction, endorsements, fans, international selection and career milestones all react to how you are playing. Signature moves, attribute points and training decide what you can attempt on the ball.

None of it is decoration. Reputation opens doors; relationships change the offers that appear; injuries reshape a season. A career played entirely through match screens leaves value on the table.

## What is being modelled

The real sport is governed by the [Laws of the Game](https://www.theifab.com/laws-of-the-game-documents/), published by IFAB, which set out among other things that a match is played by two teams of not more than eleven players ([Law 3](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-players/)). This game borrows the structure of the sport — eleven players, a season, promotion through levels of the game — rather than any real competition's rules or identities.

Every club, competition and player is fictional. Results are outputs of a model under its own assumptions and are not forecasts of anything real. If you want the reasoning behind why two identical seasons produce different outcomes, [how sports simulation engines work](/blog/how-sports-simulation-engines-work/) covers it, and [how soccer league tables work](/blog/how-soccer-league-tables-work/) explains the points and tiebreaker arithmetic your simulated seasons are scored with.
