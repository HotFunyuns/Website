The Taiping Rebellion was a civil war in late imperial China, fought from 1850 to 1864 between the Qing dynasty and a millenarian state, the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom, whose leader believed himself the younger brother of Jesus Christ. Columbia University's Asia for Educators says no other event devastated China as much in the nineteenth century. Wikipedia calls it the deadliest civil war in history.

How the death toll is estimated — and why the range is so wide — is the subject of [China's deadliest rebellions](/blog/chinas-deadliest-rebellions/). This page follows the war itself: where it came from, how it spread, and why it collapsed.

## Pressure in the south before 1850

Wikipedia describes a Qing state under strain: famines, natural disasters, heavy taxes and rising rents, and the defeat of the First Opium War, with an opium-driven trade imbalance damaging the economy (the background is in [the Opium Wars and unequal treaties](/blog/opium-wars-and-unequal-treaties/)). Banditry, secret societies and self-defence units multiplied.

There was a demographic squeeze too. Wikipedia states that China's population nearly doubled between 1766 and 1833 while the amount of cultivated land stayed the same. Run the arithmetic on that: if people go from 1 to roughly 2 and land stays at 1, the land per person falls from 1 to about 0.5 — close to halving in under seventy years. Anti-Manchu feeling, Wikipedia adds, was strongest among the Hakka, a Han subgroup in the south.

## A failed candidate's visions

The movement grew from one man's crisis. The two sources date it differently, which is worth noticing. Asia for Educators says Hong Xiuquan failed the imperial examinations for the third time in 1847 and was delirious for thirty days. Wikipedia places the third failure in 1837, followed by visions of a celestial family, and a fourth and final failure in 1843 — after which he read Christian pamphlets given to him years earlier and concluded that his heavenly father was God and the elder brother he had seen was Jesus. In 1847 he studied the Bible in Guangzhou with the American Baptist missionary Issachar Jacox Roberts, who refused to baptise him. When [two sources disagree on a date](/blog/when-two-sources-disagree-on-a-date/), the honest course is to report both.

His follower Feng Yunshan founded the God Worshipping Society in 1844. Asia for Educators lists the programme that grew from it: common property, land reform, equal position of women, abstinence from opium, tobacco and alcohol, calendar and literary reform, and a new political-military organisation of society.

## From Jintian to the Heavenly Capital, 1851–1853

Wikipedia dates the outbreak to persecution of the God Worshippers in Guangxi. In early January 1851 a rebel army of 10,000 routed Qing forces at Jintian, and on 11 January 1851 Hong declared himself Heavenly King of the Heavenly Kingdom of Peace — *Taiping Tianguo*. The Taiping marched north into Hunan, took Wuchang in December 1852 and Anqing in February 1853, and on 19 March 1853 captured Nanjing, which Hong renamed Tianjing, the heavenly capital.

The violence was extreme on both sides. Wikipedia records that the Taiping, who regarded the Manchus as demons, killed the Manchu men of Nanjing and burned the Manchu women to death, and that the Qing in turn carried out massacres, most notably of Nanjing's civilians at the end.

## The Tianjing Incident and the turn of the war, 1853–1860

Nanjing became the Kingdom's high point and its trap. A Northern Expedition towards Beijing failed completely. Hong withdrew from administration and ruled by proclamation, and his rivalry with Yang Xiuqing — who claimed to speak as God — ended in the 1856 Tianjing Incident, when Yang and his followers were slaughtered on Hong's orders. The general Shi Dakai left for Sichuan with an army, fearing for his life.

The landowning class sided with the government. The decisive Qing instrument was not the regular army but the Xiang Army raised in Hunan by Zeng Guofan, which Wikipedia says recruited from villages and paid and drilled its men well. It retook Wuchang for the final time in December 1856 and Jiujiang in May 1858. The Taiping recovered for a time: in 1860 they broke the siege of Nanjing and took Hangzhou, Changzhou and Suzhou in the wealthy lower Yangtze.

## Shanghai, Anqing and the end, 1861–1864

Wikipedia describes an attempt on Shanghai from June 1861, repulsed after fifteen months by Qing troops with European officers under Frederick Townsend Ward — the force later known as the Ever Victorious Army, commanded by Charles George Gordon. Zeng's army captured Anqing in 1861 and besieged Nanjing from May 1862.

Hong died in June 1864 after a twenty-day illness from eating wild plants; Wikipedia notes the intent may have been suicide. Nanjing fell in July 1864 after street fighting, and his fifteen-year-old son was caught and executed. The last loyalist commander Wikipedia names, Wang Haiyang, was defeated on 29 January 1866 — so the end date depends on whether you count Nanjing or the final holdouts.

The cost to the dynasty was lasting. Wikipedia credits the war with prompting the Self-Strengthening Movement and with accelerating the rise of provincial power that fed the later Warlord Era; Asia for Educators notes that some parts of central China had not fully recovered even by the 1950s.
