[World War Gunship Attack](/apps/world-war-gunship-attack/) is an arcade shooter built around one aircraft and a lot of incoming fire. You fly a gunship into battle zones, engage enemy aircraft and armoured targets, dodge what they throw back and strike before they overwhelm you. This guide explains how to get better at it, using the game's store listing and screenshots.

## What kind of shooter it is

The listing describes controls that are easy to understand, with the practice going into **movement, aiming, attack timing and enemy patterns**. Missions bring new enemy formations and tougher objectives as you progress, and they build to **elite enemies and bosses**.

| Specials | Ultimate abilities for when the battlefield gets crowded |

## Reading enemy patterns

Because the controls are simple, knowledge is what separates a good run from a bad one. The listing frames mastery as learning movement, aiming, attack timing and enemy patterns. A useful habit on a new mission is to play the first attempt defensively: dodge first, shoot second, and watch where enemies enter, where they fire from and which targets are armoured. The second attempt can then be planned.

## Switching attacks

Combat gives you more than one button. You **switch between attack options** and use **alternative attacks** at the right moment. The listing does not say which attack suits which target, so finding out is part of the practice: missions mix enemy formations, armoured targets and elite enemies, and trying each attack option against each of them shows you what works. Practise switching early in a mission, when the screen is quiet, so it is automatic when it matters.

## Saving the ultimate for bosses

**Ultimate abilities** are powerful special attacks for when the screen gets crowded, and the listing tells you to **time your attacks carefully** to deal major damage to elite enemies and bosses. The common mistake is firing the ultimate at the first big wave and arriving at the boss without it. A simple rule: use the ultimate when you are about to be overwhelmed, or when the boss arrives, whichever comes first.

## Hangar upgrades between sorties

Between missions, the **hangar** turns progress into power: upgrades for **weapons, armour and performance**. Spend with a purpose, the way you would in any upgrade game:

1. If enemies **survive too long**, upgrade weapons.
2. If you **die to chip damage** before the boss, upgrade armour.
3. If you **cannot dodge** the patterns you know, look at performance.

**A worked example, with made-up numbers.** Suppose a boss takes 100 seconds to destroy at your current firepower. A 25% weapons upgrade cuts that to 100 ÷ 1.25 = 80 seconds, so you spend a fifth less time dodging its attacks, and take roughly a fifth fewer hits if it fires at a steady rate. A 25% armour upgrade instead lets you absorb a quarter more hits over the same 100 seconds. The two are close for one boss, but the weapons upgrade also shortens every other fight, which can make firepower the better first buy when you are surviving but slowly.

The same reasoning, worked through with numbers for a tower defense game, is in [the idle tower defense upgrade guide](/blog/tower-defense-upgrade-priority/).

## Sectors, aircraft and difficulty

The screenshots show a **mission select across connected sectors**, a **choice of aircraft**, and three difficulty settings: **Normal, Hardcore and Prestige**. Normal is the place to learn patterns; the harder settings are for when you know a sector well and your aircraft is upgraded. Moving up a difficulty is a good test of whether you have learned a mission or merely survived it.

## Not a simulator, not a history lesson

The listing presents it as an arcade shooter with accessible controls, and [the app's page here](/apps/world-war-gunship-attack/) is explicit: it is **not a flight simulator or a historical recreation**, and it uses a world-war setting and styling for entertainment. The aircraft type behind the name has a real history, though, from World War II escort experiments to Vietnam-era side-firing aircraft; [what a gunship is](/blog/what-is-a-gunship-aircraft/) explains it.
