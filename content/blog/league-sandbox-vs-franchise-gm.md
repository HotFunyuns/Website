"Basketball GM game" can mean two different jobs. One is running a team: drafting, signing and trading players to build a winner. The other is running the league those teams play in. We make one of each for Android: [Basketball Game Franchise GM](/apps/pro-basketball-draft-gm-mode/) and [Basketball League Sandbox](/apps/basketball-league-sandbox/). This guide compares them using their store listings and their app pages on this site, so you can choose the job you want.

One clarification first. **Basketball GM**, the free browser game from ZenGM, is a different game from a different developer. ZenGM describes it as a free online single-player basketball management game in which you are the general manager of a team, and says it started in 2008. It is not one of our apps.

## Two different jobs

The two games mirror two real roles. Wikipedia describes a sports **general manager** as the team executive responsible for acquiring players' rights, negotiating their contracts and moving on players the team no longer wants, says the GM may also be responsible for hiring and firing the head coach, and notes GMs usually select players in the draft with the coaching staff and scouts. A **commissioner** sits above the teams and runs the competition itself; [what a sports commissioner does](/blog/what-does-a-sports-commissioner-do/) explains that office.

Franchise GM gives you the first job. League Sandbox gives you the second, in Commissioner Mode, and adds a third role: an experimenter who can ask "what if?" of the whole league.

## The two games side by side

| | Basketball Game Franchise GM | Basketball League Sandbox |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your role | General manager of one franchise | Commissioner of the league, or experimenter in Sandbox Mode |
| Where it starts | A roster draft from the player pool | A league created from real-player rosters |
| Drafting | Eight formats: Unlimited, Quick, Blind, Franchise, Salary Cap, VS Computer, Battle Mode and a Daily Challenge | Unlimited Draft, offered as a separate way to build a team |
| The season | 82 games, with rotations, contracts and trades to manage | Simulated seasons with a calendar, standings, power rankings and stats |
| Watching games | Simulated from your roster; no on-court play | Replays with shot maps, momentum charts and adjustable speeds |
| Between seasons | Review what worked and start the next draft | An 18-step offseason of roster changes and player development |
| Beyond one league | Not described on its app page | Connected leagues, transfers, loans, promotion and relegation, national teams and a tournament creator |
| Players | A pool built from real-world players' careers, with the game's own ratings | Real-player rosters to start, with fictional players added once expansion uses up the pool |

## Franchise GM: can you build a winner?

Franchise GM is a team-building puzzle. Its app page says it starts at the draft board, where you weigh value against fit and assemble a lineup that works as a unit, and that balance and depth carry more weight than any single marquee pick. The season then judges the roster: 82 games, with rotations, contracts and trades in your hands as you push for a playoff place.

The eight formats change the rules of each run. The app page says each asks for a different plan, and warns that a roster that dominates one format can fall apart in the next. Finished drafts can be compared and shared. [How a basketball draft simulator works](/blog/basketball-draft-gm-guide/) covers drafting strategy in depth.

## League Sandbox: what happens to a league if...?

League Sandbox works at a different scale. In Commissioner Mode, its listing explains, you shape league rules, explore team finances and schedule fairness, and see how rule changes play out. Sandbox Mode is for experiments, with scenario presets and a trigger builder.

Seasons are simulated and can be watched as replays, including a jump to the fourth quarter or the final two minutes, and an **18-step offseason** carries the league from one year to the next. The real NBA calendar is also a long sequence of steps, which [how the NBA offseason works](/blog/how-the-nba-offseason-works/) lays out. League Sandbox also keeps the league's history, with championship runs, upsets, draft classes and franchise achievements, a timeline and a newsroom. [The Basketball League Sandbox guide](/blog/basketball-league-sandbox-guide/) walks through the modes.

## One question, asked two ways

Take one question: what makes a dynasty? Both listings use the word. In Franchise GM, you answer it from the inside: its app page talks of turning a single draft into a long-term plan, with the depth and roster construction needed to stay strong across seasons, and you are the one doing the building. In League Sandbox, you answer it from the outside: its listing invites you to see which teams become dynasties, while you set the rules they are built under. The first tests your judgment as a general manager. The second lets you study how a league produces its winners. [Salary cap basics for GM games](/blog/salary-cap-basics-for-gm-games/) explains one of the constraints that real team-builders work under.

## How much time each asks for

The app pages point in different directions. Franchise GM's says you do not need deep basketball knowledge, because the draft is readable from your first run. League Sandbox's is candid that its depth of options takes time to learn compared with a quick draft game, and says it is aimed at people who want a league history that builds over many seasons.

## What they share

Both are simulations rather than action games: neither has on-court play. Both are free to download on Google Play, supported by ads, with in-app purchases. Both app pages say their ratings and results are the games' own, and that they are not affiliated with, endorsed by or licensed by any league, team, players' association or athlete.
