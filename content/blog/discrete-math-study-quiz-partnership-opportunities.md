The Google Play listing describes [Discrete Math: Study & Quiz](/apps/discrete-math-study-quiz/) as a focused study companion designed for college students, computer science learners and anyone returning to the subject. This page is for **university departments, tutoring services and edtech platforms** considering whether working with the app makes sense, and for anyone interested in licensing or acquiring it.

## Coverage, unit by unit

The app's page on this site, drawing on the store screenshots, describes the course as a sequence of units:

1. Logic and reasoning
2. Quantifiers and rules of inference
3. Proof techniques
4. Sets, functions and countability
5. Number theory and modular arithmetic
6. Induction and algorithms
7. Combinatorics
8. Probability
9. Recurrences
10. Graph theory

Each unit breaks into topics and lessons with **knowledge checks and a mastery measure**. Practice comes as **quick practice, topic, timed and adaptive quizzes**, review of missed questions, a **custom homework mode** and **full-length practice exams**. A **tools section** works through mechanical steps: a truth-table generator, a logical-equivalence checker, a statement simplifier, a converse-inverse-contrapositive helper, quantifier negation and an argument-validity checker. A progress screen tracks accuracy, streaks, weekly goals and focus time.

The app is **free with ads**, and Google Play lists **no in-app purchases**.

## University departments

A department could explore:

- **a revision pathway** mapped to its own syllabus order;
- **co-developed question sets** for topics where students struggle, reviewed by its staff;
- **an educational partnership** that gives students a clear, free practice resource.

One condition is non-negotiable and comes from the app's page on this site: the app is **not affiliated with any university**, and **a course's own definitions and notation take priority** where they differ. Any partnership would keep that clear.

## Tutoring services

The homework mode, which builds a set around what a student is studying that week, and the timed and adaptive quizzes suit between-session work. A tutoring company might discuss **cross-promotion**, **sponsorship**, or **co-development** of tutor-facing material. The limits stated on the app's page matter here too: the app **does not grade proofs written in a student's own words** the way an instructor would, so it complements a tutor rather than replacing one.

## Edtech platforms

For platforms, the questions are about content and integration. Reign Creative LLC is open to discussing **licensing**, **integrations supported by the app's existing capabilities**, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, **publishing and distribution**, and **localisation**. A useful first message says which units interest you, how your learners would reach them, and what technical form you have in mind.

## A worked example: an exam-week bundle

Suppose a tutoring service supports 40 students through a final exam. Two weeks out, each student takes one full practice exam and records the units where they lost marks. Their tutor then builds a homework set in the app for the two weakest units, and the student completes one timed quiz a day. If each daily quiz takes 15 minutes, a student spends 14 × 15 = 210 minutes, three and a half hours, on targeted practice over the fortnight, while tutor time goes on explaining proofs. A partnership could formalise the routine into a branded guide, with the tutoring service supplying the explanations and Reign Creative supplying the practice structure.
