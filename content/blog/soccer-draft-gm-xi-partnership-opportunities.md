For a buyer, a licensee or a brand partner, Pro Football GM Club Soccer reduces to a short description: a soccer draft game with a club-management layer, built and owned by Reign Creative LLC. This overview is written for football media, fantasy communities and publishers who want to evaluate it, and it is organised the way a diligence conversation might run — product, open questions, routes, then mechanics.

## The product in an acquirer's terms

Taken from its Google Play listing: each run offers a fresh set of player choices, and you draft across the pitch to complete an XI. Position fit and playing style matter as much as ratings. The simulation then plays the campaign and reports wins, draws and losses. The headline goal is a perfect season — every match won, which the app's page on this site points out is a different thing from merely going unbeaten, because a side built only to avoid defeat draws too often.

Breadth comes from formats: limited drafts, budget challenges, computer battles, tournaments, quizzes, player guessing and season modes, plus daily challenges. For longer saves there is club management, covering formations, squad depth, transfers, contracts, fitness and season progress. Runs can be saved and compared.

Monetisation, as listed: free download, ads, optional in-app purchases. There are no hands-on matches. The app's page on this site is [/apps/soccer-draft-gm-xi/](/apps/soccer-draft-gm-xi/), and the player-facing [soccer draft XI guide](/blog/soccer-draft-xi-guide/) walks through a run from first pick to final table.

The audience the app page describes is telling for a buyer: supporters "who argue about lineups more than results", drafters who want a squad puzzle rather than match controls, and players who want quick runs and a long save in the same game. That mix of short and long sessions is one of the things an acquirer or publisher would be inheriting.

## Questions a diligence team would raise

**Whose data are the players?** The pool is drawn from real footballers' careers, but the ratings are the game's own estimates, not official statistics, and the app page states that no league, team, players' association or athlete is affiliated with, endorses or licenses the game. A buyer would want to understand how the ratings were compiled; a media partner would need to present them as the game's own.

**Which assets come with the store listing?** The Play transfer covers the listing and its history (see the final section). Source code, art and anything outside Google Play are a matter for the written agreement.

**Are there features the app does not have?** Yes: no hands-on match play, and nothing in the listing about online multiplayer. We would rather confirm gaps early.

## Routes short of a sale

Ownership is only one option. Others we are open to discussing:

1. **Licensing or white-label** — a media brand's own edition, where technically feasible.
2. **Publishing and distribution** — a publisher handling marketing and store presence in chosen regions.
3. **Co-development** — new quiz packs, challenge formats or club-management depth with a partner who knows the game's audience.
4. **Cross-promotion and sponsorship** — with complementary products, where they suit the game.

## Formats for fantasy groups and football media

The quiz, player-guessing and tournament modes are natural segments for a podcast or newsletter, and the draft itself is a debate starter for audiences who read about [how league tables work](/blog/how-soccer-league-tables-work/) or [expected goals](/blog/expected-goals-xg-explained/). The site's roundup of [manager games for short sessions](/blog/best-sports-manager-games-for-short-sessions/) shows where the game sits for players who want quick runs.

A worked example for a community draft: an XI has 11 slots. If a group voted on two positions a day, filling the side would take 11 ÷ 2 = 5.5, so six days, leaving the seventh day of a week for the simulated season and the reveal. Defensive picks could then be judged against the site's explainer on [clean sheets](/blog/clean-sheets-explained/).

## Play Console mechanics after signing

Google sets out the transfer route on its [help page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). The seller submits a request from Play Console; the buyer, as target developer, approves it; Google's support team reviews it within two business days. Before that, the seller should export any reports needed later, since bulk export, estimated sales and earnings reports do not move. Because the app sells in-app products, the buyer needs an active payments profile, and with a different default currency the app stays unpublished until prices are checked and it is republished. Ad SDKs must be updated for the new account, and closed testing groups must be rebuilt because test groups cannot move.
