Cooked leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge, and raw ground meat and raw poultry only 1 to 2 days, according to the US government's food-safety guidance. This guide sets out the key figures from the **Cold Food Storage Chart** on FoodSafety.gov, a site managed by the Department of Health and Human Services with USDA, FDA and CDC as partners, along with the rules that make the numbers work.

## Two temperatures first

Every storage time assumes the right temperature:

- **Fridge: 40°F (4°C) or below.**
- **Freezer: 0°F (-18°C) or below.**

FoodSafety.gov explains that the short fridge limits keep food from spoiling or becoming dangerous to eat, while its freezer guidelines are **for quality only**: food stored continuously at 0°F or below can be kept indefinitely. USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service suggests keeping an appliance thermometer in the fridge and freezer to check the temperature.

## Raw meat, poultry and fish

| Food | Fridge | Freezer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground beef, turkey, chicken, pork, lamb and mixtures | 1 to 2 days | 3 to 4 months |
| Steaks, chops and roasts (beef, veal, lamb, pork) | 3 to 5 days | 4 to 12 months |
| Chicken or turkey, whole | 1 to 2 days | 1 year |
| Chicken or turkey, pieces | 1 to 2 days | 9 months |
| Raw sausage (chicken, turkey, pork, beef) | 1 to 2 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Fatty fish, such as salmon, mackerel or catfish | 1 to 3 days | 2 to 3 months |
| Lean fish, such as cod, flounder or haddock | 1 to 3 days | 6 to 8 months |
| Shrimp | 3 to 5 days | 6 to 18 months |

## Deli foods, bacon and eggs

| Food | Fridge | Freezer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Luncheon meat, opened or deli sliced | 3 to 5 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Luncheon meat, unopened | 2 weeks | 1 to 2 months |
| Hot dogs, opened | 1 week | 1 to 2 months |
| Bacon | 1 week | 1 month |
| Egg, chicken, ham, tuna and macaroni salads | 3 to 4 days | Does not freeze well |
| Raw eggs in shell | 3 to 5 weeks | Do not freeze in shell |
| Hard-cooked eggs | 1 week | Do not freeze |

## Leftovers and cooked dishes

| Food | Fridge | Freezer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cooked meat or poultry | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 6 months |
| Soups and stews, with vegetables or meat | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months |
| Pizza | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Chicken nuggets or patties | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 3 months |
| Casseroles with eggs, after baking | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months |

USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service gives the same general rule: leftovers keep **3 to 4 days** in the fridge or **3 to 4 months** in the freezer, and frozen leftovers, though safe indefinitely, lose moisture and flavour over long storage.

## A worked example: one roast chicken

You roast a chicken on Sunday evening. Its leftovers are good for 3 to 4 days in the fridge, so plan to finish them by **Wednesday or Thursday**. If Thursday's dinner is something else, freeze a portion on Monday or Tuesday instead: frozen cooked poultry keeps its quality for 2 to 6 months. The raw chicken itself had only **1 to 2 days** in the fridge before cooking, so a chicken bought on Friday for a Sunday roast should go in the freezer if plans slip.

## Safety starts before storage

Storage times only apply to food that went into the fridge promptly. FSIS advises refrigerating leftovers within **2 hours** of cooking, or within 1 hour if the air is above 90°F, dividing large amounts into **shallow containers** so they cool quickly, and covering or sealing them.
