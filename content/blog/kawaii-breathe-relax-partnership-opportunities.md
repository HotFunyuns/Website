A breathing app is a small thing: a minute or two, a guide on the screen, a slower rhythm. [Kawaii Cute Breathe Relax Care](/apps/kawaii-breathe-relax/) makes that minute friendly, with cute characters doing the counting. This page is for **wellness publishers, character brands and workplace wellbeing programmes**, and it covers what the app does, where collaboration fits, and the line every wellness partnership has to hold.

It is not medical advice, and the app is not a treatment for anything.

## How a session works

The user picks a guide: **a cloud that grows on the inhale and shrinks on the exhale, or one of several cute animals**, in a matching scene. A **timer and cues** show the rhythm, including any hold, so nobody has to count seconds. The listing presents the app as a gentle start to breath-focused meditation, for a pause at a desk, a break in a stressful day or a few minutes before sleep, with **no experience assumed and no score to beat**.

The store screenshots show the range: a **session timer**, a **breath counter**, pause and resume, **multi-step routines**, **gentle breath holds**, **equal breathing**, a **one-minute reset** and a **bedtime wind-down**. Store images feature guides such as a whale, a capybara and an axolotl.

The app is free with ads, and Google Play lists no in-app purchases. Inside the app and its store images it is titled Kawaii Cute Breathe Relax Calm.

## Character brands: the natural fit

The whole design rests on a character doing a job. That makes **character and kawaii brands** the clearest partners. Ideas Reign Creative LLC would discuss:

- **a licensed guide**, where a brand's character leads the breathing animation;
- **themed scenes and seasonal collections**;
- **cross-promotion** between the app and a brand's own channels;
- **sponsorship** of a routine, clearly labelled.

Rights, artwork and approvals would be agreed in writing; nothing here implies a licence already exists.

## Wellness publishers

Publishers producing relaxation books, courses or content could explore **co-developed routines**, **localisation**, **publishing and distribution**, or **licensing**. The listing's framing helps: a short pause, noticing the breath, no pressure. Content that matches that tone fits; content promising transformation does not.

## Workplace programmes

For an employer with a wellbeing programme, a one-minute reset between meetings is easy to explain and free to try. A programme could present the app in internal communications, add it to a resources page, or, with a partnership, receive co-branded materials. It should be offered as **one optional relaxation tool**, alongside, never instead of, proper support such as occupational health or an employee assistance service.

## The line every partnership must hold

The app's page on this site is clear: it is **a relaxation tool, not a treatment**. Anyone with persistent anxiety, sleep problems or a breathing condition should speak to a health professional, and **the app does not diagnose or treat anything**. The same page advises stopping and breathing normally if any pattern, **especially a breath hold, causes dizziness or discomfort**.

Partner copy follows those words. No claims about curing anxiety, improving sleep or lowering blood pressure; no suggestion that a character is a therapist.

## A worked example: a two-minute break card

A workplace programme designs a desk card: "Two minutes, three steps." Step one, open the app and choose a guide. Step two, run the one-minute reset twice. Step three, notice how you feel; if a hold makes you light-headed, skip it and breathe normally. Below the steps, the card lists the company's real support routes. A partnership could produce the card in the app's style; the support information stays the employer's.
