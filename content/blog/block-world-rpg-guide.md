[Block World RPG Craft & Build](/apps/block-world-rpg/) is two games sharing one world. One is a block-building sandbox: gather resources, craft what you need and build a place to return to. The other is an RPG: head out to explore, fight monsters, find dungeons and face dragons, earning EXP as you go. Its in-game title reads **Anime Craft — Build Block World**, and the art leans on cherry blossoms and anime styling. This guide takes you through the first hours, using what its Google Play listing says and what [our app page](/apps/block-world-rpg/) records from its store screenshots.

## What kind of game it is

Wikipedia defines a **sandbox game** as one that gives players broad freedom to experiment with interacting systems and pursue self-chosen goals, and adds that sandbox design may minimise predetermined objectives. Block World is a sandbox first: nothing tells you what to build. The RPG layer gives exploration a reason rather than a script. Combat earns EXP, the game tracks stats and skills, and so your character progresses as well as your buildings.

The Google Play listing frames the rhythm as yours to set: "Some sessions are for building; others are for exploration."

## Choosing a mode for your first world

The first decision comes on the new-world settings screen shown in the store images. **Creative** play gives you unlimited blocks and flight: ideal for learning how building works, or for a project with no interruptions. **Survival** brings the stakes: you gather what you build with, and combat is part of the main loop. The **peaceful** survival option sits in between, letting you gather and craft without combat pressure.

A sensible plan for a new player is two worlds. Because more than one world can be saved, you can keep a creative world for practising builds and a survival world for the adventure. [Creative versus survival mode](/blog/creative-vs-survival-mode/) compares the two styles in more detail.

## Your first base

The "how it works" steps on our app page start with a world and then a base: craft tools and build somewhere to return to between trips. In survival, the base is where your work accumulates. As general advice for survival-style building, keep the first base simple and close to the resources you are gathering; the listing itself says only to "build a base before heading farther into the block world." Our app page describes the payoff of the loop as building bigger and travelling further. [Building your first base](/blog/block-game-starter-base/) covers layout, storage and sizing.

## Your first trip out

Once the base stands, the world opens up. According to the screenshots summarised on our app page, worlds are large and divided into regions, and a world map and quest journal give longer play some structure. As general advice, a first trip works best with a clear purpose: a resource you need, a region you have not seen, or a quest to follow. The screenshots show tools and weapons and an inventory and backpack, so check what you are carrying before you leave.

The screenshots show first-person combat and archery, and our app page says combat earns EXP while the game tracks stats and skills. Pets appear in the screenshots too.

## Your first dungeon

Dungeons, monsters and dragons wait beyond familiar ground. The loop on our app page explains how to approach them: explore and fight, earn EXP, then **come back stronger**, using what you found and learned. The listing puts it as stepping "into dungeons ready for combat." One way to read that: treat the first dungeon as a scouting trip as much as a fight, and if it goes badly, return to the base, craft and prepare, then go again.

## Progress on two tracks

What sets the game apart from a pure building sandbox is that progress runs on two tracks at once:

1. **Your buildings**, which grow as you gather and craft.
2. **Your character**, whose stats and skills grow through EXP.

Each feeds the other. In survival crafting games, Wikipedia's article on the genre notes, tools made from gathered resources let players obtain better resources in turn; in Block World, the EXP from fighting adds a second engine, and the loop on our app page ends with building bigger and travelling further. [How crafting systems work](/blog/how-crafting-systems-work/) explains the general pattern behind loops like this.

## Limits worth knowing

The listing does not describe online multiplayer or shared worlds, so treat it as a single-player game. Combat is part of regular survival, so choose creative or peaceful play if you only want to build. It is free to download on Google Play, supported by ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases.
