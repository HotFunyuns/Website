[Protein Diet Tracker](/apps/protein-diet-tracker/) is built around quick entries, plain screens and no account, and its Google Play listing describes a clean, easy tracker "without complicated screens or confusing tools". This page is written for **fitness educators, gyms and nutrition publishers** thinking about working with it, and it doubles as a briefing for anyone interested in licensing or buying the app.

It is not nutrition advice. The app is a tracking tool, and targets belong to qualified professionals.

## The app in practice

The app's page on this site describes a daily log. Users add **meals and snacks** as the day goes, and the app rolls them into totals for **protein, calories, carbohydrates and fat**. Alongside food they can **log workouts** and **record body weight**, so training and intake share one history. **Daily goals are the user's to set**: the app shows how the day tracks against those numbers and leaves the choosing entirely to them. Over weeks, **progress trends and nutrition history** show what a normal week looks like.

It is free to download, supported by ads, with optional in-app purchases.

## The question gyms ask first: where does the data go?

For a gym or employer, where member data goes is a first-order question. Here the app's page on this site, in line with our [privacy policy](/privacy/), is clear: **no account is required**, and entries are **stored locally on the device** rather than on Reign Creative's servers, staying there until the user deletes them or uninstalls the app. The same page notes the trade-off: **there is no cloud sync between phones**.

That shapes what a partnership can be. A gym can recommend the app and supply guidance; it does not get a dashboard of members' meals, because no such central store exists. Partners who want group features would be proposing new development, not a data-sharing deal, and that would be a separate conversation about scope and privacy.

## What fitness educators can do with it

- **Onboarding sheets** that show a new client how to set the goals their coach recommends.
- **Check-in routines** where clients review their own weekly trend before a session.
- **Workshop material** pairing the app with education on protein sources and meal timing, delivered by the educator.

Each keeps the professional in charge of advice and the app in the role of a log.

## What nutrition publishers might propose

Publishers produce guides, plans and recipe content. Arrangements Reign Creative LLC is open to discussing include **cross-promotion**, **sponsorship**, **co-developed content**, **localisation**, **licensing**, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, and **publishing and distribution** partnerships. A useful proposal explains the audience, what would be added to or around the app, and how claims would be checked.

## Wording a partner must avoid

The limitations stated on the app's page on this site are the boundary for any co-branded material:

- the app **does not calculate a protein target**;
- it **does not push a plan** or assume a way of eating;
- it is **not medical or dietetic advice**.

So no partner copy should promise weight loss, muscle gain or health outcomes. Language about convenience, honesty about intake and seeing a week at a glance stays within what the app does.

## A worked example: a 30-day challenge

A gym runs a 30-day "know your intake" challenge for 25 members. Each member's coach sets a personal protein goal at the start; members log in the app and bring a screenshot of their weekly trend to four check-ins. Thirty days is about four and a quarter weeks, so four check-ins cover it. The gym needs no agreement to do this; a partnership could add branded materials, a sponsor or a localised guide.

## Ownership and the Play transfer

Reign Creative LLC is open to serious discussions about **acquiring this app alone or alongside the other health and food apps**. This page gives **no prices, valuations, revenue or user numbers**, and it is not an offer.

On Google Play, an acquisition becomes a **transfer between developer accounts**. Google's Play Console help says the receiving account needs an **active payments profile** for apps with in-app products, that **users, ratings and the store listing** move across, and that the seller keeps its **earnings reports and pre-transfer orders**. For this app, users' food logs stay where they always were: **on their own phones**.
