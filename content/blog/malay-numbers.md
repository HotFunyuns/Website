Malay numbers are built from ten words and four place-value words, and they fit together the way the arithmetic does. There are no special forms to memorise beyond one small prefix. This reference covers the digits, the teens and tens, big numbers and decimals, and how to read a price in ringgit. Malay is written in the Latin alphabet, so the spellings below are the standard ones; [the Malay pronunciation guide](/blog/malay-pronunciation-guide/) covers how they sound.

## Zero to ten

| Number | Malay |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | kosong (informal), sifar (formal) |
| 1 | satu |
| 2 | dua |
| 3 | tiga |
| 4 | empat |
| 5 | lima |
| 6 | enam |
| 7 | tujuh |
| 8 | lapan |
| 9 | sembilan |
| 10 | sepuluh |

Wiktionary marks *kosong* as the informal zero, a word that otherwise means "empty", and *sifar*, borrowed from Arabic, as the formal one. It also notes that Indonesian uses *nol*. Eight is another quick marker of the two standards: Malaysian *lapan*, against the Indonesian *delapan* you will see in Wikipedia's comparison of the two.

## Belas for the teens, puluh for the tens

Two words do almost all the work:

- **belas** is "-teen". Wiktionary describes it as a combining word for eleven to nineteen: *dua belas* 12, *tiga belas* 13, through *sembilan belas* 19. Its example sentence, *Abang saya berumur lapan belas tahun*, means "My older brother is eighteen years old."
- **puluh** is "ten" as a multiplier: *dua puluh* 20, *tiga puluh* 30, *sembilan puluh* 90. Units follow, so 21 is *dua puluh satu* and 45 is *empat puluh lima*.

Wiktionary records that *puluh* is first attested in the Kedukan Bukit inscription of 683 CE, in the form that is now spelled *sepuluh*.

## Se-: the prefix that means one

Before a place-value word, "one" can be the prefix **se-** instead of *satu*. Wiktionary analyses *sebelas* (11) as *se-* plus *belas*, gives *sepuluh* as 10, and gives *seribu* as the current spelling of "one thousand". It also lists *satu belas* as an alternative form of eleven, so you may hear both. The same pattern produces *seratus*, one hundred.

## Ratus, ribu, juta: hundreds to millions

| Number | Malay |
| --- | --- |
| 100 | seratus |
| 200 | dua ratus |
| 1,000 | seribu |
| 5,000 | lima ribu |
| 1,000,000 | satu juta |

Wiktionary lists *ratus* as "hundred" and *ribu* as "thousand", both inherited words. As the worked example below shows, Malay groups large numbers in thousands, as English does: 234,000 is *dua ratus tiga puluh empat ribu*, "two hundred thirty-four thousand". Thai, by contrast, has single words for 10,000 and 100,000; compare the grouping in [Thai numbers](/blog/thai-numbers/).

## A worked example: reading 1,234,567.89

Wikipedia's comparison of Indonesian and Standard Malay gives one long number in both standards. In Malaysia and Singapore it is written **1,234,567.89** and read:

*Satu juta dua ratus tiga puluh empat ribu lima ratus enam puluh tujuh **perpuluhan lapan** sembilan.*

Broken down: *satu juta* (1,000,000), *dua ratus tiga puluh empat ribu* (234 thousand), *lima ratus enam puluh tujuh* (567), then *perpuluhan*, "decimal point", and the digits *lapan sembilan* read one at a time. In Indonesia the same number is written 1.234.567,89, with *koma* for the decimal and *delapan* for eight.

## Ringgit, sen and rounding to five sen

Malaysia's currency is the **ringgit**, symbol **RM**, divided into 100 **sen**. Wikipedia lists banknotes of RM1, RM5, RM10, RM20, RM50 and RM100 in frequent use and coins of 5, 10, 20 and 50 sen; the 1 sen coin is discontinued but still legal tender. Since 1 April 2008, Wikipedia notes, the **total bill** has been rounded to the nearest 5 sen, so:

1. A total of **RM12.43** is paid as RM12.45: *dua belas ringgit empat puluh lima sen*.
2. A total of **RM12.42** is paid as RM12.40: *dua belas ringgit empat puluh sen*.

Wikipedia also records regional words: in the northern states of Peninsular Malaysia, 10 sen is called *kupang*, so 50 sen is *lima kupang*.

## Asking how many and how much

The question word is **berapa**, which Wiktionary glosses as "how many, how much", used to ask about amount, value, time and measurement. Its examples include *Sekarang pukul berapa?*, "What time is it?", and *Adam umur berapa?*, "How old is Adam?".
