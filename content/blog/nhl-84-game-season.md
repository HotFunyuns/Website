**Each NHL team plays 84 regular-season games in 2026-27, two more than the 82 it played last season.** The longer schedule is written into the collective bargaining agreement the league and the NHL Players' Association agreed on June 27, 2025, which took effect on September 16, 2026. The league's 1,344-game schedule opened on September 29 — only the second September start in its history — and ends on April 10, 2027.

It is the first 84-game season since 1993-94 and the third in the league's 109 seasons. And to answer the question the number invites: no team has come close to going an 82- or 84-game season unbeaten — the longest run without a loss in league history is 35 games.

## What changed, and when

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| June 27, 2025 | The NHL and NHLPA sign a memorandum of understanding for a new four-year agreement |
| July 2025 | Players and the league's Board of Governors ratify it; the league announces it on July 8 |
| July 16, 2026 | The 2026-27 schedule is released: 1,344 games, 84 per team |
| September 16, 2026 | The agreement takes effect; it runs through September 15, 2030 |
| September 29, 2026 | Opening night |
| April 10, 2027 | Final day, with all 32 teams playing |

The agreement itself is specific about the schedule. Its revised clause says each club "shall play not more than 84 Regular Season Games", including at least 11 in the first 30 days — 12 if the season starts before the second week of October. Every player contract is now deemed to contemplate an 84-game schedule unless the club and player agree a lower number in writing.

The extra games did not come from nowhere. The same item of the memorandum caps each club at four exhibition games and limits training camp to 13 days for players who earned at least 50 games' pension credit the season before, and 18 days for everyone else. For scale, Buffalo's summary of the deal noted that the club was scheduled to play six exhibitions in the fall of 2025.

## Where the two games went

The new games are division games. The league's scheduling matrix for 2026-27:

| Opponents | Games against each | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 division rivals | 4 (2 home, 2 away) | 28 |
| 8 conference opponents outside the division | 3 (4 teams twice at home, 4 teams twice away) | 24 |
| 16 teams in the other conference | 2 (1 home, 1 away) | 32 |
| **Total** | | **84 (42 home, 42 away)** |

The league says each team's two extra contests are a home game and a road game against divisional opponents. Under 82 games, as the Sabres' own summary of the agreement put it, a team played its division rivals either three or four times. Now every one of the seven is a four-game series, and a division race is decided more by head-to-head games than it was a year ago.

The memorandum lists the changes rather than arguing for them, so the honest answer to "why 84" is the trade it records: two more games that count, a cap on games that don't, and shorter camps.

## The two earlier 84-game seasons

The league played 84 games in 1992-93 and 1993-94. Its notes on the 2026-27 schedule point out that clubs in both of those seasons played at least two neutral-site games, in cities including Atlanta, Cincinnati, Halifax, Hamilton, Phoenix and Saskatoon. The schedule then dropped to 48 games in 1994-95, and it has been 82 games in every season since 1995-96 except two shortened ones: 48 games in 2012-13 and 56 in 2020-21.

## What 84 games does to records

It helps to separate two kinds of record.

**Totals get easier.** Wins, points and goals over a season gain two games of opportunity — 2.4 per cent more than an 82-game year. That sounds small, but records live at the margin. The 2022-23 Boston Bruins set the league marks for wins and points with 65 wins and 135 points in 82 games. Their win rate was 65 ÷ 82 = 0.793; the same rate over 84 games is 66.6 wins. That is arithmetic, not a prediction, but it shows how a total can move without a team being any better.

**Rates don't change.** Points percentage, goals per game and save percentage are already adjusted for games played, so they compare cleanly across schedules. That is why a season total from 2026-27 should be converted to a rate before it is set beside one from an 82-game or 80-game year. The record book has always mixed schedule lengths; 2026-27 simply adds a third 84-game season to it.

There is a neat footnote here. The longest winning streak in league history — 17 straight by Pittsburgh, March 9 to April 10, 1993 — was set during the first 84-game season.

## Has any team gone unbeaten?

No — not over an 82- or 84-game season, and not over anything close to one. A team that went a whole season unbeaten would need an unbeaten run as long as the season itself, and the longest run without a loss in league history is 35 games, well under half of either schedule.

The closest anyone has come, by the records the league itself reports:

| Record | Team and season | Figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Longest run without a loss | Philadelphia Flyers, 1979-80 | 35 games (25 wins, 10 ties), October 14, 1979 to January 6, 1980 |
| Longest winning streak | Pittsburgh Penguins, 1992-93 | 17 wins, March 9 to April 10, 1993 |
| Most wins and points in a season | Boston Bruins, 2022-23 | 65 wins, 135 points (65-12-5) |

Two things stand out. The Flyers' run, which NHL.com describes as the longest unbeaten streak in North American major professional sports, included ten ties. Today's rulebook has no ties — a regular-season game still level after overtime goes to a shootout — so a modern team cannot pad an unbeaten run with draws. And even the record-setting 2022-23 Bruins lost 17 times — 12 in regulation and five after it. The 1976-77 Montreal Canadiens, whose 132 points were still the league record when NHL.com ranked that team in 2017, went 60-8-12 — eight losses in 80 games, for a .825 points percentage.

The 17-game winning streak has also held up. When Edmonton reached 16 straight in 2023-24, NHL.com was still describing Pittsburgh's run as the record.

## Why perfection does not happen

A one-line illustration: suppose a team had an 80 per cent chance of winning every game, independently — a shade above the 79.3 per cent Boston managed in its record 65-win season of 2022-23. Even so, at that rate an 84-0 season comes up roughly once in 138 million seasons, against about once in 88 million for 82-0 — two more games make perfection about 1.56 times rarer. Real games are not independent coin flips, which is the subject of [how probability works in sports simulations](/blog/understanding-sports-sim-probability/).
