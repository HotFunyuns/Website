Phnom Penh, Siem Reap, Angkor Wat, Battambang, Kampot, Kep and Sihanoukville each get named travel material in Learn Cambodian, Speak Khmer, and that specificity is one reason Reign Creative LLC thinks the course suits tourism partners. The other reasons are the script tools a Khmer heritage school could use and an English track that matters to organisations working with Khmer speakers. We are open to partnerships, licensing, distribution and acquisition; this page explains the fit.

## Cambodia-specific content, place by place

The Google Play listing describes travel material built around those destinations and around situations: airport arrivals, hotels, tuk-tuks and taxis, market bargaining, ordering food, temple etiquette, currency, pharmacies and emergencies. Culture lessons run alongside, covering history, landmarks, food, holidays, customs, proverbs and practical tips. The site's [Khmer phrases for Cambodia travel](/blog/khmer-phrases-for-cambodia-travel/) gives a sense of the ground covered.

For a **tour operator, hotel group or travel brand**, that suggests cross-promotion before a trip, a co-branded edition where technically feasible, or co-developed content for a specific itinerary, built as new work.

A worked example for an itinerary tie-in: an itinerary taking in the seven places named in the listing could pair each with its travel section, so a seven-day trip covers one place's material per day (7 ÷ 7 = 1), with temple etiquette reviewed before the Angkor Wat stop. A partner would confirm in the app exactly which material each destination has.

## Tracing and slow audio for heritage classrooms

The course starts with the Khmer script — consonants, vowels and numbers — taught with pronunciation help, romanised spelling and **tracing practice**, so reading and writing begin early. Any word or phrase can be tapped to hear, slowed down for difficult sounds and repeated after, and listening activities check recognition at normal speed. Grammar covers sentence structure, pronouns, questions, negatives, polite speech, time expressions, particles and word order.

A **Khmer heritage school** could use those tools as homework between classes. The site explains the writing system in [the Khmer script](/blog/khmer-script-explained/) and the value of tracing in [tracing practice for new scripts](/blog/tracing-practice-for-new-scripts/). Practice formats — matching, fill-in-the-blank, phrase building, self-made flashcards, pronunciation lab exercises, speaking missions, stories, roleplays, daily challenges and speed rounds — give teachers several options.

## Khmer speakers and workplace English

The course runs in the other direction too. The English track explains in Khmer and covers vocabulary, grammar, punctuation, school and workplace English, reading, writing, listening and conversation. For an **NGO** or employer working with Khmer-speaking staff or communities, that track could be the main attraction. We would discuss distribution to a team or an institutional partnership, without claiming certification or outcomes.

## Who the app page says it is for

The app's page on this site names four audiences: travellers planning a trip to Cambodia, families and partners with Khmer-speaking relatives, students and beginners starting from the alphabet, and Khmer speakers building practical English. Each lines up with one of the partner types above, which is why we think the course can support more than one kind of conversation at once — a heritage school and a tour operator would be using different halves of the same app, and neither would need the other's material changed.

## Matching partner to feature

- Tourism business → destination and situation sections, culture lessons
- Heritage school → script lessons, tracing, slow audio, flashcards
- NGO or employer → English track with Khmer explanations
- Publisher → distribution, localisation into further support languages, or acquisition

## Constraints worth knowing

The app's page describes a beginner course built around the script and everyday phrases, not a substitute for practice with fluent speakers. The app is free to download, supported by ads, with optional in-app purchases. We do not claim native-speaker certification and publish no user numbers, revenue or prices. The learner guide is [learning Khmer for beginners](/blog/learn-khmer-beginners-guide/); the app page is [/apps/learn-cambodian-khmer/](/apps/learn-cambodian-khmer/).

## Transferring the Khmer course on Google Play

A sale follows Google's [transfer instructions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). For integrated services, Google says the target account is added as an Owner on any Google Developers Console projects, Firebase projects are unlinked from the original Play Console account and linked to the target, and Google Analytics permissions are granted to the target — because linkage settings do not move on their own. An app with in-app products needs a payments profile on the receiving account, and ad SDKs must be updated. Users, ratings, reviews and the listing transfer with the app.
