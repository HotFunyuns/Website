**A quarterback archetype is best understood as a description of what you do when the play stops working.** When everything goes to plan, most quarterbacks look similar. The categories exist because plays do not go to plan roughly half the time, and the responses available then differ sharply.

## The four archetypes and what each one is actually trading

| Archetype | Primary strength | What it gives up | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pocket passer | Timing and accuracy from protection | Mobility when protection fails | Pressure arrives before the read completes |
| Scrambling playmaker | Extending broken plays | Consistency within structure | Second and third reads are required on schedule |
| Field general | Pre-snap recognition and adjustment | Raw arm talent | The throw itself is genuinely difficult |
| Dual-threat | Two credible threats | Peak in either one | Development is spread too thin to reach elite in either |

### Pocket passer

The classic build. It assumes the offensive line will hold long enough to work through a progression, and it converts that time into accurate throws.

Its virtue is repeatability. When it works, it works the same way every snap, which is exactly what a coaching staff wants. Its vulnerability is binary: a pocket passer facing pressure faster than their read is not a slightly worse quarterback, they are a quarterback with no available option.

### Scrambling playmaker

The inverse. This build accepts that plays break and specialises in the aftermath — leave the pocket, extend, create something that was not drawn up.

Its virtue is a floor under disasters: pressure becomes an opportunity rather than a sack. Its cost is inconsistency, because a play that becomes improvised is a play with a wider range of outcomes. Offences built around this archetype tend to produce more spectacular results and more three-and-outs.

### Field general

The least glamorous and the most durable. The advantage here is information rather than physical ability: recognising what the defence is showing, changing the play, and getting the offence into something that works against what is actually there.

This archetype starts slowest, because pre-snap recognition is a learned skill rather than a physical gift, and it ages best, because understanding does not decline the way speed does.

### Dual-threat

The obvious answer that is less obviously correct. A quarterback who is a real threat to run forces the defence to account for one more thing, which opens throwing lanes that would not otherwise exist.

The catch is arithmetic. Development budgets are finite. Being very good at throwing *and* running usually means being less exceptional at either than a specialist would be. In a career simulation with a fixed pool of attribute points, dual-threat is a decision, not a free upgrade.

## What actually changes in a decision-led game

In [Football Career Sim 2026](/apps/football-career-sim/), the archetype affects which responses the simulator offers you when the pocket collapses, not merely how likely each is to succeed. That distinction matters more than it sounds.

Consider the same situation — third down, pressure off the edge, primary read covered:

- A **pocket passer** is offered: step up, check down, throw it away.
- A **scrambler** is offered: escape the pocket, run for the marker, extend and throw on the move.
- A **field general** is likelier to have avoided the situation at all, by adjusting before the snap.

That is why playing against your archetype is so punishing. A scrambler who tries to win from the pocket is choosing from a menu built for someone else's strengths.

## Choosing one, honestly

The useful question is not "which archetype is best?" It is **"which failure mode can I tolerate?"**

- If you dislike sacks and turnovers on broken plays, take a scrambler or a field general.
- If you dislike inconsistency and want your good games to look like each other, take a pocket passer.
- If you want the game to reward reading the situation rather than reacting to it, take a field general.
- If you have played a career already and want the widest range of situations, take dual-threat as a second run.

An archetype only exists relative to the eleven other people on the field. Protection quality decides how long a pocket passer has; the quality of the receiving group decides whether a field general's correct read is worth anything.
