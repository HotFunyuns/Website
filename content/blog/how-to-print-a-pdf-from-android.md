You can print a PDF straight from an Android phone: turn on a **print service** for your printer in Settings, open the PDF in an app that supports printing, and choose Print. This guide walks through setting a service up, printing a PDF and checking common problems, using Google's own Android and Pixel help pages. Menus differ between phone makers and Android versions, so treat the paths below as a guide rather than exact steps.

## How Android printing works

Google's help explains that you can print from some apps on your device by **adding a printer that supports Wi-Fi or mobile data**. The link between your phone and a particular printer is a **print service**: software that Android uses to send the job (Google's Android help gives HP's service as an example). Printing is switched on and managed in Settings, and the app you print from decides how you start a print job.

There used to be another route. Google says **Google Cloud Print** was deprecated in December 2020, and from **1 January 2021** devices on any operating system can no longer print with it. Old instructions that mention Cloud Print no longer apply.

## Step 1: turn on a print service

Google's Android help and its Pixel phone help give the same path:

1. Open the **Settings** app.
2. Tap **Connected devices**, then **Connection preferences**, then **Printing**.
3. **Select a print service** and turn it on, or **add a print service** and enter the printer information.

The Android help page says some steps work only on Android 9 and up, and the Pixel page (written for Pixel phones) says some work only on Android 14 and up. Other makers can arrange Settings differently, so if the path does not match your phone, search Settings for "printing".

## Step 2: print the PDF

1. **Open the PDF** in an app that supports printing.
2. Find **Print**: Google's help says the app determines how you print; often you can tap **Menu** or **More**, then **Print** (the Pixel page also mentions **Share**).
3. **Choose your printer** from the list.
4. **Check the options** the print screen shows, such as copies, pages or paper size. Which options appear depends on the app, the printer and its print service.
5. **Print**, and check the first page before printing a long document.

## A worked example: printing part of a long PDF

You need pages 3 to 5 of a 40-page manual. Printing the whole file would use 40 sheets single-sided, or 20 double-sided. Entering a page range of **3-5** prints three pages, or two sheets double-sided. If you print these pages often, extract them into their own small PDF once; [how to split a PDF](/blog/how-to-split-a-pdf/) explains page ranges and extraction.

## Paper size and scaling

PDF pages have a fixed size, and it may not match the paper in the tray. Wikipedia's paper-size article gives ISO A4 as 210 × 297 mm and US Letter as 8.5 × 11 inches (216 × 279 mm), so A4 is slightly narrower and longer, and it notes that the United States and Canada use Letter rather than the ISO sizes. If the print preview shows edges cut off or a large margin, look for a scaling option such as fit to page, and confirm the paper size setting matches the tray. For forms that will be scanned or posted back, check that nothing near the margins, such as a reference number or signature line, has been lost.

## Draft or best quality?

If your print screen offers a quality or colour choice, draft and greyscale suit reading copies and use less colour ink; keep colour for photos, coloured charts and anything where colour carries meaning.

## When the printer does not appear

If your printer is missing from the list, these checks are worth working through:

- **Network**: Google's help says the printer must support Wi-Fi or mobile data, so check that it is switched on and connected to your network, and that your phone is connected too.
- **Print service on**: confirm the right service is turned on in Settings.
- **Printer awake**: if the printer has a sleep mode, wake it and try again.
- **Add a service**: if no service on the list matches your printer, Google's help describes tapping **Add service** under Printing and entering the printer information.
- **Restart**: turning the printer and phone off and on again is a simple next step before deeper troubleshooting.

## When the app cannot print

Not every app supports printing. Google's help suggests a workaround: **take a screenshot** of the app's screen and print the image. For a PDF there is another option: open the file in a different app that supports printing, or share it to one.

## Before you print

A little preparation saves paper. Fill in forms on screen first, so you print the final version; [filling in a PDF form](/blog/how-to-fill-in-a-pdf-form/) covers that. Rotate any sideways pages, remove blank ones, and if the PDF is a large scan, remember that printing does not need a small file: compression is for sending, not printing; [how to compress a PDF](/blog/how-to-compress-a-pdf/) explains when it helps.
