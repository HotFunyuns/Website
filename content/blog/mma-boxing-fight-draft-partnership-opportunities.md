Grappler or striker? Power or reach? The app's page on this site says 40-0 MMA & Boxing Fight Draft is aimed at "combat sports fans who argue about style matchups", and that argument is the business opportunity. Reign Creative LLC owns this draft and management game and is open to licensing, distribution, cross-promotion and sale discussions, especially with combat-sports media and fantasy communities.

## Two simulators, two rulebooks

The Google Play listing says the two sports are simulated separately. Mixed martial arts bouts resolve by knockout, technical knockout, submission or decision, weighing striking, wrestling, takedowns, submissions and ground control. Boxing adds draws and judges' scorecards and looks at hand speed, power, defence, footwork and ring control. Power, defence, cardio, reach, grappling and fight IQ all feed results, so a stylistic edge can beat a ratings edge.

That separation matters to a media partner, because the difference between the rule sets is a topic in its own right; the site's [boxing versus MMA rules](/blog/boxing-vs-mma-rules/) explainer covers the real thing.

## A stable, not a single fighter

You draft from dedicated MMA and boxing pools, weighing ratings, records, eras and weight classes, and decide whether you want finishers, technicians, wrestlers and grapplers, or a roster that covers all of it. **GM Mode** runs the business: signing fighters, managing contracts and moving contenders toward title shots, with rankings responding to your calls. Seven ways to draft — Unlimited Fight Draft, Limited Draft, Tournament, VS Computer, Draft Battle Royale, MMA Only Draft and Boxing Only Draft — are joined by a Fighter Explorer, collections, comparisons, quizzes and daily challenges. You do not throw punches; for that, Reign Creative makes [Boxing Prize Fighter Career](/apps/boxing-prize-fighter-career/).

The game is free to download, carries ads and offers optional in-app purchases. Store details are on the [app page](/apps/mma-boxing-fight-draft/).

## Combat-sports media: matchup debates as content

A podcast, channel or newsletter could build recurring segments around the simulators: draft two stables live and let the result settle a debate, or test questions like the one in the site's piece on [whether reach matters](/blog/does-reach-matter-in-boxing-and-mma/). The Fighter Explorer and comparison screens give hosts a quick reference during a show. Any sponsorship or branded segment would be new work, agreed in writing and suited to the game's audience.

A worked example of the title's arithmetic: the 40-0 target is forty fights without a loss. A stable of four fighters each going 10-0 would reach it (4 × 10 = 40), while five fighters would need eight wins apiece (40 ÷ 5 = 8). Framing a segment as "how would you split forty wins?" is the kind of hook the format invites.

## Fantasy and bracket formats

Tournament, Draft Battle Royale and VS Computer give fantasy communities competitive structures; single-sport drafts let an MMA-focused group exclude boxing, or the reverse. Daily challenges and quizzes add shorter formats. Background reading such as [boxing styles](/blog/boxing-styles-explained/) or [weight classes](/blog/boxing-weight-classes-explained/) can sit beside a community draft.

## Licensing: what we own and what we do not

We can discuss licensing the software, its two simulators and its presentation, including a white-label edition for a media brand where technically feasible, and we can discuss distribution and localisation in new regions. We cannot license fighters' names or likenesses, promotion marks or sanctioning-body belts. In the app page's words, the pool is built from real-world careers, the ratings are the game's own estimates and not official statistics, and the game has no affiliation, endorsement or licence from any league, team, players' association or athlete. A partner wanting official elements would need the rights holders' agreement first.

## Selling a fight-draft game on Google Play

A buyer would take the app through Google Play's [transfer request process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). Google lists what goes with the app — users, statistics, data, comments, ratings and subscriptions — and what does not, including bulk export, estimated sales and earnings reports, promotions and test groups. For an app with in-app products, the receiving account needs an active payments profile. Ads mean the buyer must update ad SDK integrations so traffic is credited correctly. The request goes first to the target developer for review and approval; Google then says its support team reviews and replies within two business days. Everything a sale covers beyond the Play listing itself — source code, the fighter data set, artwork, any period of support after handover — would be defined in the written agreement, because the Play transfer moves only what Google lists.
