"Basketball career game" can mean four very different things: controlling a player on the court, steering a career through decisions, building a roster, or running an entire league. Reign Creative makes one of each for Android. This comparison sets them side by side using their Google Play listings and their app pages on this site, so you can pick by how you like to play rather than by name.

## The four games at a glance

| | Basketball Career Pro League | Basketball Career Sim | Basketball Game Franchise GM | Basketball League Sandbox |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| You are | The player, on court | The player, making decisions | The general manager | The commissioner |

## Basketball Career Pro League: hands on the ball

The [Basketball Career Pro League](/apps/basketball-career-pro-league/) app page describes a player you create and then take onto the court with on-screen controls: sprint into position, shoot or move the ball, look for steals and fight for rebounds. Training in shooting, passing and defense feeds the next game, and the box score shows what your player contributed. You can also watch with Auto Play or simulate. It suits players who want the movement in their hands. [The Basketball Career Pro League guide](/blog/basketball-career-pro-league-guide/) explains it in depth, and [basketball shot selection](/blog/basketball-shot-selection/) helps with the shots you take.

## Basketball Career Sim: a career through decisions

[Basketball Career Sim](/apps/pro-basketball-my-career-sim/) starts with a player you create, position, build, playing style, archetype and attributes, and runs the career through youth basketball, high school, recruiting and college, the draft and the pro years. Games use **Live Sim**: you step in on key possessions and choose what your player does, and those choices feed coach trust, team chemistry and reputation. Its listing notes it supports offline play and needs no account, and that in-game money has no real-world value. It suits players who prefer decisions to dexterity.

## Basketball Game Franchise GM: build the roster

[Basketball Game Franchise GM](/apps/pro-basketball-draft-gm-mode/) starts at the draft board. You weigh value against fit, then run an **82-game season**, managing rotations, contracts and trades. Eight draft formats, including Unlimited, Quick, Blind, Franchise, Salary Cap, VS Computer, Battle Mode and a Daily Challenge, change the constraints. [How to build a balanced basketball roster](/blog/how-to-build-a-balanced-basketball-roster/) covers the thinking it rewards.

## Basketball League Sandbox: run the league

[Basketball League Sandbox](/apps/basketball-league-sandbox/) puts you in charge of the whole competition: rules, finances, schedule fairness, an 18-step offseason and a connected world with promotion and relegation. Games are simulated and watched as replays with shot maps and momentum charts. [Its guide](/blog/basketball-league-sandbox-guide/) explains the modes, and [league sandbox versus franchise GM](/blog/league-sandbox-vs-franchise-gm/) compares it with running one team.

## Which should you pick?

1. **You want to play the games yourself:** Basketball Career Pro League.
2. **You want a story and the big decisions, without fiddly controls:** Basketball Career Sim.
3. **You enjoy drafting and team-building puzzles:** Basketball Game Franchise GM.
4. **You want to shape a league's history:** Basketball League Sandbox.

The approaches also ask for different amounts of attention: the Basketball Career Pro League app page notes that, as a hands-on action game, it asks for more attention per session than a menu-driven career sim.

They also reward different skills. A hands-on game rewards timing and reading the court; a decision-based career rewards judgment about risk and when to take a shot; a draft game rewards valuing players and building balance; a league sandbox rewards thinking in systems, such as how a rule change spreads across every team over several seasons. If you are unsure, start with the one closest to how you already watch basketball: if you mostly follow players, try a career game first; if you follow trades and standings, try a front-office game.

## Limits to keep in mind

Basketball Career Pro League's app page notes that its listing and screenshots describe single-player play only, and the simulation-based games do not offer on-court control at all, so choose by the kind of control you want. Ratings and results in the games that use real players are each game's own estimates, and the games are not affiliated with any league, team, players' association or athlete.
