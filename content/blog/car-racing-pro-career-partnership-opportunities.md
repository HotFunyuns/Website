Car Racing - Pro Career Racer has no steering wheel and no throttle. The driver's job here is the pit wall's job too: when to attack, when to save tyres, when to come in. Reign Creative LLC built it as a motorsport career about decisions, and we think karting and motorsport communities, racing media and publishers are the partners who would get the most from it. This overview explains why, and how a partnership or sale would be arranged.

## Strategy over steering

Its Google Play listing says races are simulated while you make the calls that change the result: attack, defend or hold position; brake early, trail-brake or risk a late move; choose the line through a hairpin or a chicane; decide when to pit. Tyre wear, fuel, grip, car condition and reliability matter, and rain, cautions, traffic and safety periods can change the picture mid-race.

The race view tracks positions, time gaps, nearby cars and pit status, with standings, a minimap and commentary all reacting to the same race state. The game is free to download, ad-supported, and Google Play lists in-app purchases. For players, the [Car Racing - Pro Career Racer guide](/blog/car-racing-pro-career-guide/) explains the systems; the [app page](/apps/car-racing-pro-career/) links to the store.

## From karting upward

The career begins as an unknown rookie in karting and junior racing and climbs through professional motorsport. Strong results bring better contracts and stronger teams; mistakes and risky calls can cost positions, contracts and championships. Teammates, engineers, team management, rivals, the media, sponsors and contract negotiations shape where a driver ends up.

That ladder is why karting and grassroots motorsport communities are a natural fit. The site's explainer on the [motorsport career ladder](/blog/motorsport-career-ladder/) covers the real path the game abstracts. Possible arrangements include cross-promotion to members, co-developed karting-stage content, or a branded edition where technically feasible. We would not describe the game as driver training.

## Racing media and a race state built for commentary

Because standings, gaps, pit status and commentary all follow one race state, a race in the game already reads like a broadcast summary. A racing podcast or channel could run a simulated championship as a serial, set strategy challenges for its audience, or pair the game with explainers such as [tyre strategy](/blog/racing-tire-strategy/) and [racing flags](/blog/racing-flags-explained/). Sponsored content would need to suit the game and would be agreed in writing; the sponsors that appear in a driver's career today are part of the fiction.

## Publishers and international readiness

The audience a publisher would be marketing to is described on the app's page on this site: motorsport fans who enjoy race strategy more than steering, career-mode players who like contracts, rivals and team politics, anyone who wants each race to turn on a handful of decisions, and players who enjoy long careers that build season by season.

One small detail also matters: the listing says speeds can be shown in mph or km/h in Settings. That is a modest but real head start for markets on either system. Full localisation of text would still be a project, as the listing does not describe language options.

A worked example of the unit setting: a straight taken at 200 km/h would display as 200 ÷ 1.609344 ≈ 124.3 mph, so the same race reads naturally to players in either kind of market. Publishing in new regions, distribution, co-development, licensing and acquisition are all open for discussion.

## Fictional circuits and real brands

Every circuit is invented, each with its own layout, corners, surface and climate. The listing describes a fictional motorsport world, so there are no real circuits, series, teams or drivers in it. Bringing in a real circuit, series or manufacturer would require their owners' agreement. We do not publish prices, revenue or player numbers, and nothing here is a binding offer.

## Bringing a motorsport idea to Reign Creative

Start with the inquiry form on the [partnerships page](/partnerships/) or an email to ReignCreativeSupport@gmail.com. A few sentences about your organisation and the idea is enough. Please send nothing confidential, financial or personal at first; that comes later, with qualified parties and under NDA where appropriate. Discussions are nonbinding, and any arrangement depends on due diligence and a written agreement.

## Moving a racing game to a new account

A buyer would receive the app through Google Play's [transfer request process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). Google requires both developer accounts to be registered and active and the app to comply with policy. Users, download statistics, ratings, reviews and the store listing go with it; payout and earnings reports do not. The receiving account needs a payments profile because the game sells in-app products, and ad SDK integrations must be updated so ad traffic is credited to the buyer. If a translation project through Google Play's translation service were under way, Google says it would have to finish before the transfer could happen.
