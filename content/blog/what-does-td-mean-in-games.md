TD means tower defence. If you are looking at a game title, a store category or a subreddit name, that is the answer and there is very little nuance in it: a tower defence game is one where you stop waves of enemies reaching somewhere by building and upgrading stationary defences along their route.

The confusion is not really about the games. It is about the two letters.

## Why the abbreviation is ambiguous and the genre is not

"TD" is one of those abbreviations that is completely unambiguous inside a community and completely useless outside it. In American football it is a touchdown. In finance and business it is a bank, a job title or a set of initials. In games it is tower defence, and the two-letter form is common enough that plenty of titles use it as a suffix in place of writing out the genre.

When we checked Google's autocomplete suggestions for "td game meaning" on 15 September 2026, the completions came back split across all of those worlds at once — chat slang, a specific business-simulation game, and the tower defence sense. That is a description of what autocomplete suggested on one day, not a measurement of how many people search for anything; there is no volume figure behind it and we are not going to invent one. But it does tell you something useful about the question: people typing it are often disambiguating rather than researching.

The genre itself is far less ambiguous than its abbreviation. Tower defence is a subgenre of strategy games in which the goal is to defend a player's territory or possessions by obstructing attackers or by stopping them reaching the exits, usually achieved by placing defensive structures on or along their path.

## The three-part test

If you want a working definition rather than a dictionary one, most games that people agree are tower defence have three properties together.

1. **A route.** Enemies travel a path from an entrance to an exit. It may be fixed by the map, or shaped by what you build, but it exists and you can see it.
2. **Stationary defences.** You place structures, and they stay where you put them. You do not steer them, and generally you cannot attack with them anywhere other than where they already are.
3. **A destination-based loss condition.** You lose because enemies reached somewhere, not because a character you control died.

Games that satisfy two of the three sit on the border, and that is where most labelling disputes live. A game with a route and stationary defences but where you also control a hero is usually called a hybrid. A game with towers and no route is something else entirely.

Strategic choice and positioning of defensive elements is described as an essential strategy of the genre, which is a formal way of saying the interesting decision is *where*, not *what* — a point worked through in detail in [why placement beats damage](/blog/tower-defense-strategy-basics/).

## TD and RTS are relatives, not synonyms

Tower defence is seen as a subgenre of real-time strategy, because of its real-time origins, even though plenty of modern entries fold in turn-based elements — the between-waves planning pause being the obvious one.

The family resemblance is real and the verbs are different.

- **RTS:** gather resources, build an economy, produce mobile units, manoeuvre them, attack an opponent.
- **TD:** receive resources from kills, build stationary defences, choose positions and upgrade order, survive waves.

The clearest practical difference is that you cannot go on the offensive in a tower defence game. There is no attack button. Everything you can influence happens before the enemy arrives, which is why the planning phase between waves carries so much of the skill.

## The genre is older than the label

The lineage usually starts in the arcade. Space Invaders in 1978 had you defending the bottom of the screen, with destructible shields that could obstruct incoming fire. Missile Command in 1980 gave that defence a genuinely strategic role: multiple attack paths per wave, and targets that were cities and bases rather than a player character. Later titles carried the idea of defending non-player objects forward, and by the time the modern buildable-tower form arrived, the shape of the problem had been around for two decades.

The phrase "tower defense" and the TD abbreviation belong to the browser and mod era rather than the arcade era. Desktop Tower Defense, a Flash game released in 2007, is one of the titles usually credited with popularising the genre for a mainstream audience.

## Why our title says both

[Regal Tower Defense: TD Game](/apps/regal-tower-defense/) writes the genre out in full *and* abbreviates it, which looks redundant until you think about what a store listing title is for.

A title on a store is not prose. It is the only piece of text that is guaranteed to be indexed, shown in search results and read in a list of twenty other things, and it has to work for someone who types the genre in full and for someone who types two letters. Writing both is not stylistic indecision; it is covering two ways of naming the same category. We would rather be plain about that than pretend the naming was purely aesthetic.

What the "TD" in a title does not tell you is anything about quality, length, or whether the game is any good — it is a category label. The things worth checking before installing any of them are what the towers actually do, which [the tower-type explainer](/blog/tower-types-and-what-they-counter/) covers, and whether it plays without a connection, which [the offline guide](/blog/offline-tower-defence-on-android/) tests.

## Neighbouring abbreviations you will meet

Genre shorthand in games is dense, and two letters rarely carry enough information on their own. RTS is real-time strategy. 4X is a strategy family named for "explore, expand, exploit, exterminate". A roguelike or roguelite has its own contested definition, and [what actually makes a roguelike](/blog/what-makes-a-roguelike/) is worth reading if you have ever argued about that one.

None of these labels are precise. They are useful shortcuts for finding something like the thing you already liked, and they all break down at the edges. TD is unusual in that its edges are fairly clear and its abbreviation is not.
