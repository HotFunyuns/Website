If you publish mobile games, run a sports media brand or translate games for new markets, the useful question about Basketball Career Pro League is simple: what would you actually be working with? The answer is a thumb-controlled basketball career game that Reign Creative LLC built and owns, with a play-watch-simulate design that suits more than one kind of player. We are open to talking about publishing it, distributing it, localising it, developing it further with a partner, or selling it.

## The game in one paragraph, from its store listing

Its Google Play listing describes a basketball career you play with on-screen controls: sprint into position, shoot or move the ball, look for steals and fight for rebounds, after guided tutorial steps. Between games, training in shooting, passing and defense builds the career, and a box score shows what your player contributed. League standings keep the season in view, and the store screenshots describe a path from youth prospect through high school and college to the pro game. You can play possessions yourself, watch AI-controlled games with **Auto Play**, or simulate ahead, and selectable camera views include a wider **Broadcast** view.

It is free, supported by ads, and Google Play lists no in-app purchases. The listing describes single-player play and mentions no online multiplayer. For the player's-eye view, see the [Basketball Career Pro League guide](/blog/basketball-career-pro-league-guide/) or the [app page](/apps/basketball-career-pro-league/).

## Matching partnership models to the features

| Partner | What they might bring | Feature it builds on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobile publisher | Marketing, store presence, user acquisition planning | A complete career loop with tutorial onboarding |
| Sports media brand | Audience, editorial voice, sponsored events | Box scores and standings that generate talking points |
| Localisation partner | Translation, cultural review, regional store copy | Play built around on-screen controls and stats |
| Complementary app | Cross-promotion | The Play / Auto Play / simulate choice for different session lengths |

The comparison with its sibling matters here. Our [comparison of basketball career games](/blog/basketball-career-games-compared/) explains that [Basketball Career Sim](/apps/pro-basketball-my-career-sim/) is built around decisions at key moments, while this game puts movement in your hands. A publisher assembling a sports slate could treat the two as a pair aimed at different tastes, or take on one.

## Sports media: stats as content

A media partner is likely to look for material its audience can discuss. The box score after each game and the skills the listing names give a sponsor or editorial team hooks such as shooting form or [what counts as a double-double](/blog/double-double-triple-double/). Sponsored in-game events or themed challenges would be new work scoped together; nothing like that exists in the game today, and we would say so plainly in any proposal.

## Translating the court for new markets

The listing does not describe language options, so localisation would be a project rather than a setting to switch on. A translator would handle menus, tutorial prompts and store copy, and a regional publisher might advise on how a basketball career framed around high school and college reads in markets where the youth pathway looks different.

A rough scoping example: the listing names six on-court actions — move, shoot, pass, sprint, steal and rebound — taught with guided tutorial steps. If each of those six needed one tutorial prompt translated into three target languages, that is 6 × 3 = 18 prompt translations before menus and store text are counted. Real scoping would come from the actual strings, shared under NDA.

## If a publisher wants to own it outright

An acquisition moves the app through Google Play's own [transfer process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). Google says the request is submitted from Play Console after both developer accounts are registered and active, then approved by the receiving developer, with Google's support team replying within two business days. Users, ratings and reviews, statistics and the store listing travel with the app. Google also notes that test groups cannot be transferred and closed testing groups must be recreated, and that for apps using Play App Signing both parties may wish to consider the security of the upload key and app signing key. Because this game is ad-supported, Google's page says ad SDK integrations must be updated after the move so ad traffic is credited correctly.
