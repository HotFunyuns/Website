If you look up the Second World War's death toll in three places you will get three different answers, and none of them is a lie. The National WWII Museum publishes worldwide figures of 15,000,000 battle deaths, 25,000,000 battle wounded and 45,000,000 civilian deaths, and attaches a caution in the same breath: "Worldwide casualty estimates vary widely in several sources." Commonly encountered totals run from roughly 60 million to over 80 million. The gap between those numbers is not sloppiness. It is a measurement of how much of this war was fought in places where counting had stopped working.

## A demonstration, from one museum's own page

The clearest illustration is on the museum's page itself. Directly after the worldwide civilian figure of 45,000,000, it adds: "The number of civilian deaths in China alone might well be more than 50,000,000."

Those two sentences cannot both be treated as components of a single consistent table. That is the point, and the museum is being transparent rather than careless: the aggregate and the regional estimate come from different bodies of research with different definitions, and placing them side by side is more honest than reconciling them into a tidy total that no evidence supports.

The country rows behave the same way. The museum gives the Soviet Union 8,800,000 to 10,700,000 military deaths and 24,000,000 total; Germany 6,600,000 to 8,800,000 total; Poland 240,000 military deaths and 5,600,000 total; Greece 300,000 to 800,000. A range that wide for Greece is telling you something specific about the surviving records, not hedging for its own sake.

## The four things that actually vary

**Definition.** Does a war death mean someone killed by enemy action, or anyone who died as a consequence of the war? The second definition includes deaths from malnutrition, exposure, untreated illness and displacement, and it is far larger than the first. Two honest counts using the two definitions will differ by millions.

**Famine and disease.** War-caused famine killed enormous numbers of people in several theatres. Whether those deaths appear in a war total is a methodological decision, and different research traditions make it differently.

**Borders.** Frontiers moved before, during and after the war, and populations were expelled, transferred and reclassified. A person counted in one national column in 1939 may belong to a different column in 1946, and national totals that add to a world total therefore depend on which map you use.

**Capacity and willingness to count.** Some states had functioning registration systems throughout. Others had their administrative machinery destroyed, or were occupied by powers with no interest in recording the deaths they caused, or later revised published figures for political reasons. The largest uncertainties sit exactly where the largest losses were.

Military deaths are generally firmer than civilian deaths for a mundane reason: armies keep personnel registers, and occupied villages do not. This is the same evidentiary asymmetry our guide to [how historians date events](/blog/how-historians-date-events/) describes in other contexts — what survives determines what can be known, and what survives is not random.

## The Holocaust is not a line in a statistics table

The systematic murder of European Jews has its own documented figures, its own evidence base and its own historiography, and it must be stated as such rather than absorbed into a discussion of estimation method.

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum records that six million Jewish people died in the Holocaust, and publishes a breakdown: approximately 2.7 million murdered with poison gas in killing centres; about 2 million killed in mass shooting operations across occupied eastern Europe; between 800,000 and 1,000,000 who died in camps and ghettos through deliberate deprivation and brutality; and at least 250,000 murdered outside detention sites.

It also documents the other groups the Nazi state and its collaborators murdered: around 3.3 million Soviet prisoners of war, around 1.8 million ethnic Poles, at least 250,000 and possibly as many as 500,000 Romani people, more than 310,000 Serb civilians, and 250,000 to 300,000 people with disabilities.

On method, the museum is explicit: "There is no single Nazi German document that accounts for every death in the Holocaust." The figures are reconstructed from hundreds of thousands of pages of German documentation, prewar demographic studies, survivor testimony and archival records. Incomplete records do not mean unknowable facts; they mean facts established by convergence from several independent directions, which is a stronger method than any single ledger would have been.

The same insistence on separate treatment applies to the Nanjing massacre, to the Bengal famine of 1943, and to the deliberate bombing of civilian populations by both Axis and Allied forces. Each has its own documentary situation and its own contested figures, and each loses meaning when dissolved into a global aggregate.

## Where the records live

Part of why the numbers keep moving is that archives keep opening.

The Japan Center for Asian Historical Records, run through the National Archives of Japan, publishes digital images of official documents from the national archives, the diplomatic archives of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the National Institute for Defense Studies, covering Japan's relations with neighbouring Asian countries. The Bundesarchiv holds German federal records across 23 locations, with roughly 530 kilometres of material and around 15 million images. National archives in the successor states of the Soviet Union, in Poland, and across occupied Europe have released material at different rates and under different conditions since 1991.

Every one of those releases has the capacity to revise a figure. A published total is therefore a statement about the state of the evidence at a date, not a permanent fact, which is why citing the source and the year matters as much as citing the number. The alternative is the pattern documented in our article on [what caused the Bronze Age collapse](/blog/what-caused-the-bronze-age-collapse/), where a striking number was repeated until it acquired the authority of a count, and then largely failed re-examination.

## Keeping a range intact on a timeline

[World History Timeline Sim](/apps/world-history-timeline-sim/) is our own app, built by Reign Creative. Its Second World War material includes a war record among the app's 209 wars, a boss battle and a lesson among its 189 lessons, and the lesson presents the war's death toll as a range rather than as a single figure.

That is the correct treatment, and it is worth saying why a product would be tempted to do otherwise. A single number fits in a card, sorts in a table and looks authoritative. A range does none of those things and is more accurate. Our position is that the range stays, and that the app's content is in any case a learning and exploration tool with no citations and no external review — not a substitute for teachers, scholarship, archives or museums. Use it to see where the war sits against everything else happening in the same decade, then take any figure you intend to repeat from an institution that publishes its method.

## What to take away

**A range is a finding, not a failure.** It tells you how much of the war happened where counting had broken down.

**Never collapse it for readability.** A single round number imports a precision the evidence does not contain, and it hides the most interesting thing about the figure.

**Never use it to rank suffering.** These are estimates of destroyed lives, and comparison is for understanding the war's geography and policy, not for adjudicating whose loss was greater.
