Learn Lao Language Fast & Easy is a beginner Lao course that starts with the script and builds toward everyday conversation. Reign Creative LLC built it, owns it, and sees three kinds of organisation as natural partners: Lao diaspora and community organisations, NGOs whose staff or volunteers work in Laos, and travel businesses. This page takes each in turn, then covers the app's limits, how to contact us and how a sale would work.

## What the Lao course contains

The Google Play listing says the course starts with the **Lao script**: recognising characters, learning the sounds attached to them and reading useful words, through interactive practice rather than a chart. Vocabulary follows for greetings, family, food, shopping, transport, numbers, time, directions, work and daily life. Phrase lessons place it in restaurants, hotels, airports, markets and conversations with local speakers, and grammar and sentence-building activities cover Lao sentence structure.

Practice formats include listening, matching, vocabulary review, sentence building, dictation, quizzes and pronunciation activities, with mistake review and the option to revisit lessons. Advanced topics cover expressions and cultural lessons. Lao speakers can switch direction to study English vocabulary, phrases, pronunciation and grammar with Lao translations. The app page is [/apps/learn-lao/](/apps/learn-lao/).

## For diaspora and community organisations

The app page's audiences include families and partners with Lao relatives. A community association running language classes could use the app as a shared reference for the script, which the site explains in [the Lao alphabet](/blog/lao-alphabet-explained/), or as revision between sessions. The English direction may matter just as much to the same organisation, for members who speak Lao and want everyday English.

*What we would discuss:* co-promotion to members, a branded edition for the organisation where technically feasible, or co-developed cultural content built with the community's knowledge. Background on how a course for a less-served language is put together is in [building a Lao course from scratch](/blog/building-a-lao-course-from-scratch/).

## For NGOs and their teams

An NGO preparing staff or volunteers for work in Laos would find the most direct value in the phrase lessons and the greetings vocabulary; the site's guide to [Lao greetings and politeness](/blog/lao-greetings-and-politeness/) gives the cultural background a briefing pack might borrow. The script lessons matter here too: the course puts the alphabet first rather than relying on romanisation alone, which suits a team that expects to read Lao words as well as say them. One practical point for budgets: Google Play lists **no in-app purchases** for the app; it is supported by ads.

*What we would discuss:* an institutional partnership, distribution to a team, or licensing of course content. We would not claim the app prepares anyone to work in the language; it is a beginner foundation.

A worked example for a pre-departure plan: a team member who completes two lessons on each of 5 weekdays for 6 weeks before travel would finish 2 × 5 × 6 = 60 lessons. That is a scheduling sketch for planning, not a measure of what anyone will learn.

## For travel businesses

Tour operators and travel brands could point customers to the travel phrases before departure; the site's [Lao phrases for travellers](/blog/lao-phrases-for-travellers/) shows the kind of situations covered. Cross-promotion, a co-branded edition where feasible, or distribution through a travel partner are possibilities.

## Limits to plan around

The app's page makes two points clearly. The course teaches Lao, not Thai; the languages are related but use different scripts. And it is a beginner foundation, not a replacement for speaking with fluent speakers. We do not claim native-speaker certification, and we publish no user numbers, revenue or prices.

## Contacting us about Lao learning projects

The [partnerships page](/partnerships/) has a short form, or you can email ReignCreativeSupport@gmail.com. Describe your organisation and idea in a few sentences. For a first message, please send nothing confidential, financial or personal — and no details about beneficiaries, staff or members. If there is a fit, a call follows; non-public information is shared later with qualified parties, under NDA where appropriate. Discussions are nonbinding, and any arrangement depends on due diligence and a signed agreement. Acquisition of the app alone or with other apps is also open for discussion.

## A transfer without in-app products

If the app were sold, Google Play's [transfer process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) would apply. Because it has no in-app products, Google's payments-profile requirement for paid apps and apps with in-app products would not come into play; Google's tip says that for free apps you move to the next step once the earlier ones are complete. Those steps still apply: policy compliance, downloading reports, two active developer accounts and the target's registration transaction ID. Because the app shows ads, ad SDK integrations must be updated so traffic is credited to the new owner. Users, ratings, reviews and the listing move across.
