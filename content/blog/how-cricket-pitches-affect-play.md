In cricket the ball normally bounces off the playing surface before it reaches the batter, which makes the **pitch**, the strip between the wickets, a factor in almost every delivery. Wikipedia's article on the cricket pitch puts it simply: because almost all deliveries bounce off it, the state and type of a pitch can significantly affect the outcome.

## The strip itself

Law 6 of the Laws of Cricket, in MCC's 2026 edition, defines the pitch as a rectangle **22 yards (20.12 m) long and 10 ft (3.05 m) wide**, bounded by the bowling creases at each end and by lines 5 ft either side of the line joining the middle stumps. The Ground Authority chooses and prepares it before the match; once play starts, the umpires control its use and are the sole judges of whether it is fit for play.

## Three broad types

| Pitch | Looks like | What Wikipedia says it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green (a "green top" or "green seamer") | Grass longer or moister than usual | Favours the bowler: the ball can move unpredictably off the seam and through the air |
| Dry and dusty | Little grass, a dry surface | Often favours spin bowling, because the ball grips more |
| Hot, dry climate or season | Less grass | Makes batting easier |
| Turner | A surface producing significant turn | Helps spin; an extreme one is a "raging turner" |

Wikipedia adds that, because these conditions change the relative difficulty of batting and bowling, captains weigh expected pitch behaviour carefully when deciding, after winning the toss, whether to bat or bowl first. [Pace versus spin bowling](/blog/pace-vs-spin-bowling/) explains why each type of bowler wants different conditions.

## How a pitch changes over five days

A Test pitch is not the same surface on day five as on day one. Wikipedia describes the usual arc:

1. **Early:** the surface still holds its early moisture.
2. **Middle:** as that moisture disappears, batting becomes easier.
3. **Late:** the pitch dries, cracks and may crumble into a "dust bowl", favouring spinners who can extract sharp turn.

The Laws push the pitch along this path. **Law 9 forbids watering the pitch during the match.** Rolling is limited: the batting captain may ask for up to **7 minutes** of rolling before each innings except the first, and before each subsequent day's play. Wikipedia notes rolling compresses the soil, reducing moisture and changing pace or bounce, so it can favour either batter or bowler depending on conditions. A wearing last-day pitch is part of the challenge in [batting last for a draw](/blog/how-a-test-match-ends-in-a-draw/).

## Footmarks and the protected area

Wikipedia notes that the ball normally bounces in the central part of the pitch, and that if bowlers' footmarks scuff it, the damage can give the bowling side an unfair advantage. To protect it, Law 41 defines a **protected area**: a strip starting 5 ft in front of each popping crease and running 1 ft either side of the line between the middle stumps. A bowler who runs on it in the follow-through without reasonable cause is cautioned the first time and given a final warning the second; a third offence in the same innings means the bowler is suspended from bowling for the rest of it, and another bowler finishes the over. Deliberately damaging the pitch is unfair play too, and Wikipedia notes players can face disciplinary action for it. [Spin bowling variations](/blog/spin-bowling-variations/) explains how spinners use rough.

## Weather and covers

Wikipedia notes cricket was originally played on uncovered pitches, which after rain could produce unpredictable bounce and sharp turn, the classic "sticky wicket": a wet pitch drying quickly in hot sun. Modern first-class pitches are usually covered against rain and dew. Law 10 makes covering before the match the Ground Authority's responsibility.

## Who judges the pitch

Wikipedia notes the pitch matters so much that home teams can be fined or docked points if it is deemed unfit or dangerous, with balls behaving unpredictably. At international level, the ICC rates every pitch and outfield in Tests, ODIs and T20 internationals. Its pitch-ratings page explains that the match referee marks the pitch after the match; a rating of unsatisfactory or unfit counts as substandard, the home board and venue must explain why, and the ICC may apply sanctions to the venue.
