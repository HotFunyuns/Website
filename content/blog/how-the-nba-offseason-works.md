The NBA season ends in June, but the league's year does not stop. Between the last game of the Finals and opening night, teams draft, re-sign, trade and rebuild, and almost every step has a published date. This guide puts the offseason in order, using the dates NBA.com published for 2026 and the rules in its free agency explainer.

## The 2026 offseason, in order

| Date (2026) | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 24 April | Deadline for early-entry players to apply for the draft |
| 10 May | Draft lottery; the Draft Combine runs 10–17 May in Chicago |
| 27 May | Deadline to withdraw from the draft and keep NCAA eligibility |
| 13 June | Early-entry withdrawal deadline; Game 5 of the Finals |
| 14 June | Teams may negotiate with their own upcoming free agents |
| 23–24 June | Draft: first round, then second round |
| 29 June | Last day for player and team options; qualifying offers due |
| 30 June, 6 p.m. ET | Negotiations open with all free agents |
| 1 July | New salary cap takes effect; the moratorium begins |
| 6 July, 12:01 p.m. ET | Free agents can be signed |
| 9–19 July | NBA Summer League in Las Vegas |
| 28–29 September | Most veterans report; training camps open |
| 3 October | Preseason begins |
| 20 October | Regular season starts |

## Before the draft

The offseason begins for the draft's prospects before it begins for teams. In 2026, NBA.com listed 24 April as the deadline for early-entry players to apply, the lottery on 10 May, the Combine in Chicago from 10 to 17 May, 27 May as the last day to withdraw while keeping NCAA eligibility, and 13 June as the final early-entry withdrawal deadline. [NBA draft eligibility rules](/blog/nba-draft-eligibility-rules/) explains who has to declare, and [how draft lotteries work](/blog/how-draft-lotteries-work/) why the lottery exists. The draft itself took two nights: the first round on 23 June and the second on 24 June.

## The first negotiating window

Free agency starts in two stages. NBA.com's 2026 primer explains that from **the day after the last game of the Finals**, 14 June this year after the Knicks won the title in five games, teams could negotiate with **their own** upcoming free agents. Everyone else had to wait until **6 p.m. ET on 30 June**.

The same window decides who will be on the market at all. A team that wants to keep the right to match offers for a player must submit a **qualifying offer**, a standing one-year guaranteed contract, between the day after the Finals and **29 June**. If it does not, the player becomes an unrestricted free agent. Options are due the same day: NBA.com noted that 29 June was the last day for a player or team option to be exercised. A player who declines a player option, or whose team declines a team option, becomes a free agent on 1 July. [Free agency explained](/blog/free-agency-explained/) compares restricted and unrestricted free agency.

## The moratorium

From 1 July until signings open, the league runs a **moratorium**. NBA.com describes it as a negotiation period in which teams may not sign most free agents or make trades. The reported "signings" of early July are agreements, and NBA.com notes they do not count toward team salary and are **not binding**: either side can back out. Wikipedia records the best-known case. In 2015, DeAndre Jordan agreed to join the Dallas Mavericks, changed his mind and re-signed with the Los Angeles Clippers, and a year later the moratorium was shortened from 10 days to 5.

A few deals can still be done during the moratorium, NBA.com explains:

- first-round picks signing rookie-scale contracts;
- second-round picks accepting a required tender, the one-year offer a team must make to keep their rights;
- restricted free agents accepting qualifying offers, or signing offer sheets with other teams;
- one- or two-year minimum-salary contracts, and two-way contracts;
- waiving players and claiming them off waivers.

The moratorium is also when the new **salary cap** arrives. NBA.com reported that the 2026-27 cap and tax level took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on 1 July:

| 2026-27 figure | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Salary cap | $164.961 million |
| Tax level | $200.428 million |
| First apron | $209.015 million |
| Second apron | $221.686 million |
| Minimum team salary | $148.465 million |
| Non-taxpayer mid-level exception | $15.044 million |

[The NBA apron explained](/blog/nba-apron-explained/) sets out what each line restricts. Signings opened at 12:01 p.m. ET on 6 July.

## Summer League and the quiet months

July brings Summer League. In 2026, NBA.com listed the California Classic in San Francisco and Sacramento from 3 to 6 July, the Salt Lake City Summer League on 4, 6 and 7 July, and the NBA Summer League in Las Vegas from 9 to 19 July, with all 30 teams playing at UNLV's Thomas & Mack Center and The Pavilion.

Some rules from the summer carry into the season. Wikipedia notes that a free agent signed in the offseason cannot be traded until 15 December or until three months have passed, whichever is later; [NBA trade rules](/blog/nba-trade-rules/) covers the restrictions. And NBA.com explains that an unsigned qualifying offer expires on 1 October unless it is extended.

## Back to work

The calendar closes where the next season begins. For 2026-27, NBA.com listed 28 September as the first day most veterans could report and media day for 28 teams, 29 September for camps opening, 3 October for the first preseason games, 19 October for setting opening-day rosters and **20 October** for the start of the regular season. Teams playing preseason games outside North America started a week earlier, with camps opening on 22 September.
