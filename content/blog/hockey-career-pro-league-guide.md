## Career Single Control or Team Control

**Career Single Control** puts you in your own role on the ice.

**Team Control** lets you direct the wider action, and the listing says you can switch between the two.

The listing stresses **off-puck play**: following the puck, finding your position as play moves, and contributing away from the puck as well as with it. [Hockey positions explained](/blog/hockey-positions-explained/) describes where each position should be.

## Coach objectives and shift grades

This is the game's most distinctive idea. Each shift has **coach objectives**, the job your player is asked to do, and you receive a **grade from A to F** for how well you did it.

The app's page puts the point of the grades plainly: they show whether you did your job, not just whether you scored, so a shift without a goal or an assist can still be judged on what the coach asked for. Reading them shift by shift is a way to see what your role is being measured on.

Real hockey shifts are short too: [how long a hockey shift lasts](/blog/how-long-is-a-hockey-shift/) summarises what sports scientists have measured.

## Raising your grades

Because grades follow the coach's objectives, the way to improve is to treat each shift as a small task with a clear brief. Before a shift, read the objective and decide what doing it well looks like for your position. During the shift, the listing's emphasis on following the puck and finding your position is the practical advice: be where the play needs you, rather than where the puck was a second ago. Afterwards, compare the grade with what you tried to do. A pattern of lower grades on one kind of objective suggests what to take into the next practice drill.

## Practice drills, goals and assists

Between games, **training and practice drills** are where technique improves. In league matches, goals and assists build the case for a bigger role. The store screenshots add multiple career saves and playoffs to the picture.

## A 32-team league and an 84-game schedule

According to the listing, the league has **32 teams and an 84-game regular schedule**, followed by playoffs and a championship chase. That format is the game's own, and it is not affiliated with any real league, team or players' association. If you are new to the sport, [hockey rules for beginners](/blog/hockey-rules-for-beginners/) covers the basics, and [offside in hockey](/blog/offside-in-hockey/) explains the offside rule.

## A first season, step by step

1. **Learn the controls in a few games** before worrying about grades; the app's page notes that controlling a skater well takes practice.
2. **Read the coach objectives before each shift.** They tell you what an A looks like.
3. **Track your grades, not just your points.** A run of B grades is real progress.
4. **Use practice drills** on whatever keeps lowering your grade.
5. **Keep a second save** to try a different position.
