The app's Google Play listing names heritage learners, families preserving Marathi across generations and new residents of Maharashtra among the people it is designed for. That makes community organisations, heritage schools and publishers natural partners for [Learn Marathi Language Fast](/apps/learn-marathi/). This page sets out what the app is, the kinds of arrangement Reign Creative LLC is open to discussing, and how a conversation would start.

## What learners get from the app today

The app's page on this site describes a course that starts where a genuine beginner starts: **the Marathi alphabet and Devanagari**, with **romanised text and English meanings** beside every line so a learner can read and say a sentence before recognising every character. Topics are chosen for use: greetings, introductions, family, food, shopping and prices, transport and directions, work, travel, health, numbers and time, with **cultural notes and etiquette** alongside.

Practice comes in **ten formats**: quizzes, listening drills, sentence building, matching, dictation, vocabulary review, speaking activities, roleplay conversations, timed modes and targeted review of missed questions. Streaks and achievements track progress. The course also **runs in reverse**, so Marathi speakers can study English with explanations in Marathi. The app is free to download, supported by ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases.

## Why community organisations are the obvious fit

The listing names heritage learners reconnecting with Marathi language and culture, travellers and new residents in Maharashtra, and Marathi speakers who want to learn English. A **mandal**, a temple or cultural association, or a weekend school is one way to reach those learners. Ideas that fit the app as it exists:

- **A suggested home-practice path** for weekend-school pupils, using the script lessons between classes.
- **Parent and grandparent sessions** built on the reverse course, for relatives who want English.
- **Event-linked vocabulary**, such as greetings and food words before a festival gathering.

Each of these depends on the organisation's own judgement about its learners; the app is a beginner foundation, not a replacement for a teacher.

## Publishing, localisation and licensing

For publishers and education businesses, the conversation can be broader. Reign Creative LLC is open to discussing **publishing and distribution**, **localisation** of the interface or supporting material, **licensing**, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, **co-development** of new units, **sponsorship** and **cross-promotion**. A good first proposal says which learners you serve, what you would change or add, and what you would bring: content expertise, distribution, teachers or reviewers.

## Acquiring the Marathi course

Reign Creative LLC is also open to serious discussions about the **acquisition of this app on its own, as part of the language set, or with the wider portfolio**. Those conversations begin with fit, not figures. This page does not publish prices, valuations, revenue or user numbers, and nothing here is an offer to sell.

## What the app does not claim

Partners should know the limits the app's page on this site states. **Romanised text helps a learner start, but reading real-world Marathi still means learning Devanagari.** Ads support the free version, and Google Play lists in-app purchases. Any partnership material that describes the app should keep to those claims; overstating what a learner will achieve helps nobody, least of all a school that has to answer to parents.

## What a first call would cover

A first conversation is about fit, and it stays general:

- **Your learners**: children in a weekend school, adult heritage learners, newcomers to Maharashtra or Marathi speakers learning English.
- **What you would use**: the script lessons, the topic units, the reverse course, or something new.
- **What you would add**: teachers, reviewers, recordings, distribution or funding.
- **The shape of the arrangement**: a recommendation, a co-branded project, a licence or a sale.

Specific terms, and any confidential information on either side, come later and only under an agreement both sides are comfortable with.

## How a transfer on Google Play works

If an agreement led to a change of ownership, the app would move through **Google Play's formal transfer process**. Google's Play Console help describes the main conditions:

1. **Both developer accounts must be registered and active** before a request can be submitted.
2. The request needs the **registration transaction IDs** of both accounts.
3. **Users, ratings and reviews, content ratings and store listing information** transfer with the app; **earnings and payout reports do not**, so the original owner may want to download them first.
4. Apps with ads need their **ad SDK integrations updated** so that ad traffic is credited to the new account.
5. The target developer reviews and approves the request, and Google's support team replies **within 2 business days**.
