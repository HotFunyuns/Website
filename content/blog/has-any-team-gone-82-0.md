No NBA team has ever gone 82-0. The 2026-27 season, which opens on 20 October, is the league's 81st regular season, and in the 80 before it no team finished unbeaten. The best record on the books is 73-9, set by the 2015-16 Golden State Warriors. The same team owns the best start, 24-0, which means no NBA team has ever reached 25-0, let alone 82-0.

So "82-0" is a thought experiment rather than a record anyone has threatened. It is still a useful one, because the distance between 73-9 and perfection says a lot about what an 82-game season actually measures.

## The marks that frame the question

Every honest answer to "has anyone gone 82-0?" comes back to the same handful of records. Each figure below comes from the league's own history pages, apart from the winning percentages, which are simple division.

| Record | Mark | Season and team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best regular-season record | 73-9 (.890) | 2015-16 Golden State Warriors |
| The record it broke | 72-10 (.878) | 1995-96 Chicago Bulls |
| The record that one broke | 69-13 (.841) | 1971-72 Los Angeles Lakers |
| Best start to a season | 24-0 | 2015-16 Golden State Warriors |
| Next-best starts | 15-0 | 1948-49 Washington Capitols, 1993-94 Houston Rockets, 2024-25 Cleveland Cavaliers |
| Longest winning streak | 33 games | 1971-72 Los Angeles Lakers |

Three details in that table do more explaining than the numbers themselves.

**The best start covers less than a third of a season.** On NBA.com's list of the best starts, the 24-0 run stands alone and the next three all reached 15-0. Cleveland, the only one still unbeaten when that list was published in November 2024, lost its 16th game, at Boston, according to Basketball-Reference's game log. A perfect season needs that 24-game run more than three times over without one bad night.

**The longest streak sat inside a season with 13 losses.** The Lakers' 33 straight wins began on 5 November 1971 and ended when the Milwaukee Bucks beat them on 9 January 1972. That team finished 69-13, which was the best record in league history until 1995-96.

**The best regular season did not win the title.** The 73-9 Warriors lost the 2016 Finals. The 72-10 Bulls, whose record they broke, did win the 1996 championship. A regular season sorts teams for the playoffs; even the best one on record guaranteed nothing after it.

## Why the season is 82 games

### The step from 81 to 82

The 82-game schedule is younger than the league. In 1966-67 ten teams played 81 games each, according to Basketball-Reference's season summary. For 1967-68 the league added the Seattle SuperSonics and San Diego Rockets, and the league's own season review records that the NBA, "now a 12-team league, played its first 82-game schedule."

That review records the change without explaining why the number is 82, and we will not invent a reason. What the record does show is that the figure has stuck. Every season since has been 82 games except the four below: two lockouts, one mid-season suspension and one schedule announced at 72 games.

### The seasons that came up short

| Season | Games per team | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1998-99 | 50 | A lockout wiped out the first three months; play began in February 1999 |
| 2011-12 | 66 | A lockout that began on 30 June 2011 lasted 149 days; the season opened on Christmas Day |
| 2019-20 | Suspended in March | Play stopped on 11 March 2020 after a player's preliminary positive COVID-19 test |
| 2020-21 | 72 | The league announced a 72-game season in November 2020; it began on 22 December |

The 1998-99 season was, in NBA.com's words, "the first time in league history that games were cancelled due to labor issues." It also matters for record comparisons: a shortened season can produce a high winning percentage on a low win total, which is why winning percentage, not wins, is the fairer measure across eras.

### How the 82 are filled now

The modern schedule has one more wrinkle. The league now fixes dates and opponents for 80 of each team's 82 games when the schedule is released, and assigns the last two from the results of Emirates NBA Cup group play; for 2026-27 those two games fall between 4 and 10 December. Cup games count in the regular-season standings, with one exception: NBA.com's Cup explainer says the Championship "will not be counted as a regular-season game." The two Cup finalists therefore play an extra game, but their record is still out of 82.

## Just how unlikely is a perfect season?

Going unbeaten means winning every single game, and probabilities of independent events multiply. Multiply a number slightly below one by itself 82 times and it collapses.

Here is a deliberately simple illustration. Take a team exactly as good as the best record ever, one that wins 89% of the time (73 out of 82), and pretend every game is an independent 89% chance:

- **24 straight wins** comes out at about 6%, or roughly once every 16 seasons.
- **82 straight wins** comes out at about 0.007%, or roughly once in 13,800 seasons.
- **For 82-0 to be a coin flip**, the team would need to win each game about 99.2% of the time.

That model is wrong in useful ways. Real games are not identical coin flips: opponents differ, and schedules, travel and availability change from night to night. Its only job is to show the shape of the problem. A 24-0 start is what a great team's hot stretch can look like; 82-0 asks for that hot stretch to last six months. [Understanding probability in sports simulations](/blog/understanding-sports-sim-probability/) works through why season length, more than team quality, makes perfection so rare.

## Where basketball teams have gone unbeaten

Perfect seasons do happen in basketball, just not over 82 games. NCAA.com's history, published in February 2026, counts seven men's college teams that won the national championship with an undefeated record. The most recent was Indiana in 1975-76, which finished 32-0. The same history lists Gonzaga's 24-0 regular season in 2021 as the most recent unbeaten regular season; that team then lost the national championship game.

Run the same toy model over a 32-game season and an 89% team comes out unbeaten about 2.4% of the time, against 0.007% over 82 games. Season length is doing most of the work.
