[Hero TD Idle Tower Defense](/apps/hero-td-idle-tower-defense/) combines idle-style upgrade progress with a hero who fights beside the towers. This page is for **idle and strategy publishers** and **live-ops partners**: the people who run events, missions and seasonal content. It covers the game, the content calendar it already has, collaboration models and the route to a sale.

## A hero in the defence

The listing describes waves that grow harder and a question each run asks: **how to turn the power you have into a better plan**. The hero is the twist. Alongside towers, players use **hero skills, spells such as fireballs and ultimate abilities**, plus **combat items and loadouts**, **castle wall upgrades** and **emergency repairs**, as the app's page on this site describes from the store screenshots.

The listing's own advice is to **spend with a purpose**, read how each tower performs against the enemies faced, and treat a hard battle as information: review upgrades, rethink the setup and return.

## A content calendar already in place

From the app's page on this site, which describes the store screenshots:

| Layer | What it includes |
| --- | --- |
| Modes | Six, including a campaign, endless defence, and daily and weekly challenges |
| Missions | Daily, weekly and lifetime |
| Roster | Heroes to discover |
| Armoury | Weapons, armour, helmets, boots, rings and relics |

For a live-ops partner, that structure matters: daily and weekly challenges and missions are existing slots for new content rather than systems that would need building. The game is free with ads, and Google Play lists in-app purchases.

## Idle and strategy publishers

A publisher might discuss **publishing and distribution**, **localisation**, **licensing** and **white-label versions where technically feasible**. The audience described on the app's page on this site, tower defence fans who like **a controllable hero**, players who enjoy **planning upgrades between attempts**, and those who want **missions and challenges on top of a campaign**, is the profile a publisher would compare with its own catalogue.

## Live-ops partners

Event content could take several forms:

- **themed weekly challenges** with a defined enemy mix;
- **a limited hero** introduced through an event;
- **armoury sets**, such as a relic and ring pair, tied to a season;
- **mission chains** that walk players through a new tactic.

Each would be co-developed, balanced against existing upgrades, and agreed in writing.

## Limits partners should keep

The app's page on this site notes that the listing describes **no multiplayer or co-op**, and that **progress leans on upgrades, so later waves are much harder without them**. Marketing should therefore not promise co-op events, and should describe progression honestly rather than implying that later content is quick to reach.

## A worked example: a four-week event season

A live-ops partner plans a four-week season. Each week brings one themed weekly challenge, so the season has 4 challenges in total, while daily missions continue as normal. Week two introduces a limited hero; week four adds a relic for players who complete all four challenges. With 7 daily mission refreshes a week, the season spans 28 daily refreshes alongside the 4 weekly challenges, a steady rhythm that fits the game's existing structure without new systems. The partner supplies themes and art; balancing is checked against existing upgrade paths before anything goes live.

## Its sibling, Regal Tower Defense

Reign Creative also publishes **Regal Tower Defense**, a lane-defence campaign built around tower types and placement. Hero TD adds a hero, spells, gear, several modes and missions. A partner could consider either game, or both as a tower defence pair.

## Buying Hero TD

Reign Creative LLC is open to serious discussions about **acquiring Hero TD, both tower defence games, or the portfolio**. No **prices, valuations, revenue or player numbers** are published, and this page is not an offer.

## Hero TD's move to a new Play account

A sale would go through Google Play's **request to transfer the app** to the buyer's developer account. Google's Play Console help notes that users, statistics, ratings and reviews move with the app; that **bulk export, payout and earnings reports** do not, so the seller may want to download them first; that test groups cannot be transferred; and that ad SDK integrations must be updated so ad traffic is credited to the correct account. The buyer's account also needs an active payments profile, because the game has in-app products.
