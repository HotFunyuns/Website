[Boxing Prize Fighter Career](/apps/boxing-prize-fighter-career/) is a boxing game with pixel-art fights and hands-on controls. Its Google Play listing describes creating a fighter, customising how they look, and taking them into a career where results and preparation decide how far they go. This guide explains how it plays and links to the boxing guides that explain the real sport behind it.

## Creating a fighter

You start by creating a fighter and customising their look. The store screenshots show the main menu, headed "Create a fighter, build a legacy", with tiles for career mode, quick play, a practice gym, daily and weekly challenges and the Boss Ladder. Quick Play setup screens add **weight classes, difficulty levels, custom rounds and timers**; [boxing weight classes explained](/blog/boxing-weight-classes-explained/) covers the real divisions.

## In the ring

The listing names the four punches you throw, **jabs, crosses, hooks and uppercuts**, mixed between head and body, plus **blocking** the reply. [Boxing punches explained](/blog/boxing-punches-explained/) describes each one, when it is used and what it leaves open.

The listing says the game rewards attention to **stamina, distance and defense**.

- **Stamina.** In the listing's words, an aggressive start still needs careful energy management.
- **Distance.** The listing suggests practising moving into range without spending all your stamina, and says distance and punch selection affect your approach.
- **Defense.** A knockout attempt can leave you exposed if you forget to protect yourself.

That emphasis is why the listing calls it a boxing simulator despite the pixel-art look.

## Training and sparring

Between bouts, **training and sparring** are places to work on a weakness without risking a career result. If your stamina fades, train that; if you are getting caught, spar to practise defense. In the real sport, a team also works with the boxer during the bout itself; [what a boxing corner does](/blog/what-does-a-boxing-corner-do/) explains the people in a fighter's corner.

## Variety: Quick Play, challenges and the Boss Ladder

Variety comes from opponents and challenges:

1. **Quick Play** offers single fights and lets you change opponents without starting a new save.
2. **Daily and weekly challenges** rotate.
3. The **Boss Ladder** sets a run of increasingly tough fights.

The store screenshots also show opponent profiles and a fighter comparison screen, useful for spotting a mismatch before you take a fight.

## A sensible first career

1. **Learn the punches in Quick Play** before your first career fight.
2. **Pace your stamina.** Win rounds without emptying the tank in the first.
3. **Keep your guard up after big punches**, the moment you are most exposed.
4. **Work the body.** Head and body punches are both part of the game's design, so mix your targets.
5. **Use training and sparring** on whatever loses you rounds.

## Choosing a style

Because the game rewards stamina, distance and defense, your fighter's style is really a decision about those three. Our guide to [boxing styles](/blog/boxing-styles-explained/) describes the standard classification: the out-boxer, who wins from long range with footwork, feints and the jab; the swarmer or pressure fighter, who closes the distance; the slugger, who looks for the big punch; and the boxer-puncher, who mixes both. In the game, an out-boxer approach means managing distance and picking shots, while a pressure approach spends more stamina to stay close. Neither is "right"; what matters is that your training supports the style you choose, so the habits you build in sparring hold up when a career fight gets hard. Trying both in Quick Play is the cheapest way to find out which suits you.

How fights are actually scored is explained in [boxing scoring explained](/blog/boxing-scoring-explained/), and the difference between the amateur and professional versions of the sport in [amateur versus professional boxing](/blog/amateur-vs-professional-boxing/).
