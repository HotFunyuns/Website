Picture a strategy publisher, a fantasy-basketball community or a sports-data company weighing up a draft game. Basketball Game Franchise GM is a candidate any of them could take on: a drafting and front-office title in which the season simulator is the judge of your roster. Reign Creative LLC owns it and is open to acquisition talks as well as partnerships, and this page explains what a buyer or partner would be looking at.

## What an owner of this game would hold

The app's page on this site sets out the loop. Every run begins at the draft board, where you weigh value against fit and build a lineup that works as a unit; it says balance and depth carry more weight than any single marquee pick. The season then runs 82 games, with rotations, contracts, trades and a playoff push. Eight formats change the constraints: Unlimited Draft, Quick Draft, Blind Draft, Franchise Draft, Salary Cap Draft, VS Computer, Battle Mode and a Daily Challenge. Finished drafts can be compared and shared, which the app page calls "where the replay value lives".

The game is free to download, ad-supported, and offers optional in-app purchases. Matches are simulated; there is no on-court play. The consumer side is covered in the [draft and GM guide](/blog/basketball-draft-gm-guide/), and the app's own page is [/apps/pro-basketball-draft-gm-mode/](/apps/pro-basketball-draft-gm-mode/).

## The player-data question, answered up front

Any data licensor, and any careful acquirer, will ask about players first. The app page says the pool is built from real-world players' careers, with ratings that are the game's own estimates rather than official statistics, and that the game is not affiliated with, endorsed by or licensed by any league, team, players' association or athlete. That shapes the partnership:

- A **data provider** could discuss supplying statistics under its own licence, but integrating a feed would be new development, and any use of names or likenesses beyond what the game does today would need clearance from whoever holds those rights.
- An **acquirer** should treat the current rating set as the game's own editorial work, and diligence would cover how it was put together.

We would rather state these limits now than have them surface late.

## Fantasy communities and the Daily Challenge

Fantasy players are named in the app page's own audience list ("fantasy players who prefer the draft to the season"), and the mechanics suit organised play. A Daily Challenge gives a community a shared prompt; Battle Mode and VS Computer give a head-to-head structure; shared drafts let members compare results. A community partner might run a weekly draft theme, co-design a challenge format, or cross-promote the game to members who already read pieces like [how fantasy draft strategy works](/blog/how-fantasy-draft-strategy-works/) and [how to build a balanced basketball roster](/blog/how-to-build-a-balanced-basketball-roster/).

A worked example of a community calendar: rotating through the eight formats one per week gives an eight-week cycle, and two cycles fill 16 weeks. Running the 82-game season simulation once per format across one cycle means 8 × 82 = 656 simulated games of results to compare. These are planning numbers, not engagement claims.

## Integration chores after a Play transfer

Moving the app to a buyer's developer account uses Google's [transfer request process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247). A few of the steps Google lists are directly relevant here:

- **Reports.** Bulk export, payout and earnings reports do not move, so the seller downloads what is needed and new reports start in the buyer's account.
- **In-app products.** The receiving account needs an active payments profile. If its default currency differs, Google says an app with in-app purchases is unpublished after the transfer until the new owner verifies prices and publishes again.
- **Ads.** Ad SDK integrations, including AdMob, must be updated in the app so ad traffic is credited to the right account.
- **Orders and promotions.** Orders placed before the transfer remain with the original account; promotions do not transfer, although previously issued promo codes should still work.

Google says the request is reviewed and approved by the target developer, and its support team replies within two business days. The commercial scope — code, assets, support during handover — is for the written agreement, not the Play process.
