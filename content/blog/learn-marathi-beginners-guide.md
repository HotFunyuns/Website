Start the script in the first week and start speaking on day one. That is the whole answer, and almost every stalled Marathi beginner has refused one of those two things — either sitting on the alphabet for a month before saying a word, or leaning on romanised spellings so hard that six months later the letters are still shapes.

Marathi rewards the double-track approach more than most languages do, for a reason that only becomes obvious once you have tried it.

## Why the script is a shorter job than it looks

Marathi is written in the Balbodh variant of Devanagari, an abugida of 36 consonant letters and 16 initial-vowel letters. *Abugida* is the important word. Each consonant carries a built-in vowel, and you change that vowel by hanging a mark — a *matra* — on the letter rather than writing a separate character. Once that clicks you are not learning fifty-odd arbitrary symbols, you are learning a grid.

The grid is also phonetically honest. Devanagari was built for the sound system it writes, so a letter sequence tells you the pronunciation with very little ambiguity. Compare English, where *though*, *through* and *tough* share four letters and no logic.

There is a Marathi-specific bonus here that catches Hindi readers out. Marathi often preserves a written schwa that Hindi drops in speech: रंग is *ranga* in Marathi and *rang* in Hindi. If you are coming to the script fresh, that is good news — what is written sits closer to what is said. The [Marathi alphabet and Devanagari](/blog/marathi-alphabet-and-devanagari/) breakdown covers the matras and the handful of letters Marathi uses that Hindi does not.

## The part that actually costs you time

It is not the alphabet. It is gender.

Marathi has kept three grammatical genders — masculine, feminine and neuter — where most modern Indo-Aryan languages have collapsed to two or lost the system entirely. Nouns carry one. Adjectives ending in -आ agree with it. Verbs agree with it too.

This is not an advanced topic you meet in month four. It is in the second sentence you will ever want to say. "How are you?" is तू कसा आहेस? (*tu kasā āhes*) to a man and तू कशी आहेस? (*tu kashī āhes*) to a woman; the polite form shifts again to आपण कसे आहात? (*āpaṇ kase āhāt*). Even the ordinary spoken goodbye is येतो (*yeto*) from a man and येते (*yete*) from a woman — literally "I come", used the way English uses "I'll be off".

Marathi also splits the English word *we* into an inclusive form and an exclusive one, a feature it shares with its Dravidian neighbours rather than with Indo-Aryan. You will pick the wrong one for a while. Nobody minds.

## What to learn, in what order

1. **Sounds and greetings, day one.** नमस्कार (*namaskār*) and both forms of "how are you". You now have a conversation opening and your first encounter with gender agreement, which is exactly the right moment to meet it.
2. **Vowels and their matras, week one.** Ten or so independent vowel letters plus their attached forms. This is the highest-leverage hour you will spend on Marathi.
3. **Consonants in families, weeks one to three.** They are laid out by place of articulation, so learn them in rows rather than in the accidental order of a wordlist.
4. **Numbers, weeks two to four.** Marathi numbers are largely irregular and deserve separate treatment; [Marathi numbers 1 to 100](/blog/marathi-numbers-and-counting/) explains why rote counting fails here.
5. **Situational vocabulary, continuously.** Food, shopping, prices, transport, directions, family — the topics you will actually be standing in.
6. **Verbs, from week three.** Present tense with the आहे family first, then the past, which brings in a split-ergative pattern that behaves nothing like English.

## What to skip, at least for now

- **Perfect retroflexes.** Marathi contrasts an alveolar *l* (ल) with a retroflex *ḷ* (ळ), and the distinction is real. It is also not what stops people understanding you in month one. Note it, approximate it, come back to it.
- **The literary register.** Marathi has some of the oldest literature of any modern Indian language and a formal register thick with Sanskrit borrowing. None of it helps you buy vegetables.
- **The Modi script.** Marathi was written in Modi for administration from the 13th century until the 19th, and it is enjoying a small revival. It is a lovely thing to learn second and a terrible thing to learn first.

The design decision worth explaining is this one: **romanised text and the English meaning stay visible on every line, in every unit, permanently.** They are not training wheels that come off after the alphabet chapter.

That is a trade-off and it is not free. Some learners will read the romanisation, never fully move across to the script, and plateau on reading speed. We know that. We chose it anyway, because the opposite failure is worse — a learner who hits a wall of unglossed Devanagari in unit three, cannot decode it, and quits. Marathi is a language most people come to for family or for living in Maharashtra rather than for a certificate, and those learners are unusually likely to stop at a wall and unusually likely to keep going if they are never fully blocked.

If you want the script to stick, the counter-move is yours to make: cover the romanisation with a thumb before you read the line and uncover it only when you are stuck. The app will not force you to. That is the honest account of what it does and does not do.

## What a first month actually gets you

Roughly this: slow reading of Devanagari, numbers to a hundred, greetings, family terms, food and market vocabulary, and simple present-tense sentences with the right gender agreement most of the time.

What it does not get you is conversation at speed, because Marathi speakers, like everyone, do not talk at textbook pace. [How long it takes to learn Marathi](/blog/marathi-study-plan-first-month/) sets out a week-by-week plan and is deliberately unambitious about the end state.
