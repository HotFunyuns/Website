This guide puts figures on familiar breakfasts, so you can see what each one actually provides and how small changes move the total. Every value is worked out from the US Department of Agriculture's **SR Legacy** data in FoodData Central, using the portion weights USDA lists, and every figure is per breakfast as described, not per 100 g. The entries used are "Egg, whole, cooked, scrambled" (61 g per large egg), "Bread, whole-wheat, commercially prepared" (32 g slice), "Cereals, oats, regular and quick, not fortified, dry" (81 g per cup) and the matching cooked-with-water entry (234 g per cup), "Milk, reduced fat, fluid, 2% milkfat, with added vitamin A and vitamin D" (244 g per cup), "Tofu, firm, prepared with calcium sulfate and magnesium chloride (nigari)" (126 g per half cup), "Cheese, cottage, lowfat, 2% milkfat" (226 g per cup), "Yogurt, Greek, plain, nonfat" (170 g container) and "Cheese, cheddar" (28.35 g per ounce).

## Common breakfasts, compared

| Breakfast | Protein | Calories | Protein per 100 kcal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 cup oats cooked in water + 1 medium banana | 7.2 g | 271 | 2.7 g |
| ½ cup dry oats cooked in 1 cup 2% milk | 13.4 g | 275 | 4.9 g |
| 2 slices whole-wheat toast + 2 tbsp peanut butter | 15.1 g | 353 | 4.3 g |
| Tofu scramble (½ cup firm tofu) + 1 slice whole-wheat toast | 15.4 g | 179 | 8.6 g |
| 2 scrambled eggs + 1 slice whole-wheat toast | 16.2 g | 262 | 6.2 g |
| Plain bagel + 2 tbsp cream cheese + 2 oz smoked salmon | 22.6 g | 429 | 5.3 g |
| Plain nonfat Greek yogurt (170 g) + 1 oz almonds | 23.3 g | 264 | 8.8 g |
| 1 cup 2% cottage cheese + 1 cup blueberries | 24.7 g | 267 | 9.2 g |
| Burrito: 48 g flour tortilla, 2 scrambled eggs, ½ cup black beans, 1 oz cheddar | 30.2 g | 556 | 5.4 g |

The spread is large: the burrito has more than four times the protein of oats with a banana. The yogurt and cottage cheese bowls stand out for protein per calorie, giving about 23–25 g for roughly 265 calories.

## Small swaps that add protein

Many breakfasts can gain protein without changing much:

- **Cook oats in milk instead of water.** A cup of 2% milk adds about 8.1 g of protein, and a cup of fortified soymilk about 6.3 g, according to USDA data.
- **Choose whole-wheat bread.** A 32 g whole-wheat slice has about 4.0 g of protein, against 2.6 g in a 29 g white slice.
- **Add a second protein source.** Beans and cheese turn two eggs in a tortilla into a 30 g breakfast.
- **Use Greek yogurt as a base.** A 170 g container of plain nonfat Greek yogurt has about 17.3 g on its own.

**A worked example.** Start from oats cooked in water with a banana: 7.2 g. Make the oats with half a cup of dry oats and a cup of 2% milk instead, then add the banana back, and the bowl rises to about 14.7 g, roughly double, for about 380 calories. Stir in two tablespoons of peanut butter and it reaches about 21.8 g, at about 570 calories. Each step adds protein, but also calories, which is why protein per 100 calories is a useful column.

## What a target per meal looks like

Some people aim for a set amount of protein at each meal, such as 20 or 30 g; [protein per meal explained](/blog/protein-per-meal-explained/) looks at what research says about that idea. As a point of reference, the FDA's Daily Value for protein on food labels is **50 g** a day; it is a labelling reference, not a personal target, so ask a health professional about your own needs. On the table above, seven of the nine breakfasts clear 15 g, and four clear 22 g.

## Without meat or dairy

Plant-based breakfasts can reach similar numbers with soy and beans. The tofu scramble on toast gives about 15.4 g for under 180 calories. Half a cup of dry oats cooked in a cup of fortified soymilk gives about 11.6 g for about 258 calories, and adding half a cup of black beans to a tortilla adds about 7.6 g more.

## Reading the numbers sensibly

The figures are reference values. Branded yogurts, breads and tortillas differ from USDA's generic entries, and a home "cup" of oats or cottage cheese may not weigh what USDA's portion does. USDA's scrambled egg entry also reflects eggs cooked as scrambled, not raw. If you track protein closely, weigh your usual portions once and log those.
