Airports, hotels, restaurants, transport, shopping, directions, health and emergencies: those are the headings under which Regal - Learn Thai Language groups its travel material, and they read like a list of the businesses that might partner with it. Reign Creative LLC owns the course and would like to talk with travel and hospitality companies, language schools and publishers. This page explains what the app does in both directions, which partnerships fit, and how talks and any sale would proceed.

## Travel phrases grouped by situation

According to the Google Play listing, the course is built for people who need Thai for a trip, for family or for everyday conversation and who have not studied it before. Lessons are short and follow a set path, so each session has a clear beginning and end. Travel content is arranged by situation so a learner can revisit only the section they need before a trip. Politeness is built into the grammar lessons too, through polite particles; the site covers related ground in [Thai greetings](/blog/thai-greetings/) and [Thai polite particles](/blog/thai-polite-particles/).

For a **tour operator or travel company**, that structure suggests simple collaborations: cross-promotion before departure, a branded edition for a travel brand where technically feasible, or co-developed content for a particular itinerary, scoped as new work.

## The script comes early

Rather than leading with romanised phrases, the course teaches reading from the start. Alphabet practice covers consonants, vowels, tone marks and syllables, with the basics of reading and writing, before longer sentences appear. Pronunciation practice handles tones and sounds, and speaking practice asks learners to say phrases aloud. The app's page is honest that this is slower at first for learners who only want romanised phrases.

For a **language school**, that order matches a structured curriculum. Grammar lessons cover word order, polite particles, questions, negation, classifiers, numbers and time, and the mistakes English speakers tend to make, comparing Thai and English in plain terms. Review modes, a sentence builder, dictation, listening practice, learned words, missed questions, streaks and achievements handle revision. The site's [guide to learning the Thai script](/blog/learn-thai-script-beginners-guide/) shows the material a school would be supplementing.

## Thai speakers learning English

The app turns around. Thai speakers can study English with Thai-language support, on a track covering letters, sounds, spelling, articles, verbs, questions, negatives, prepositions and conversation. For a **hospitality employer** whose Thai-speaking staff want everyday English, this second course may be the more useful half of the app. A partnership could promote that track to staff; we would not present it as a certified qualification.

## A pilot in numbers

A worked example for a hotel or school pilot: if 20 learners each complete one short lesson on each of 5 working days a week for 4 weeks, the group logs 20 × 5 × 4 = 400 lessons. That is a planning figure to size a trial, not a result we promise. Whether the pilot uses the Thai or English track would depend on the partner.

## Other routes, and the limits

Publishers could consider distribution, localisation into further support languages, or licensing of course content. Acquisition of the app alone, with others, or as part of the portfolio is also open. The app's page describes the course as a beginner foundation — script, pronunciation, core grammar and realistic phrases — not a replacement for speaking with people, and we do not claim native-speaker certification. For a sense of how learners approach the language, see [is Thai hard to learn?](/blog/is-thai-hard-to-learn/). The app page is [/apps/learn-thai/](/apps/learn-thai/).

The app is free to download, supported by ads, and offers optional in-app purchases.

## How the app would move to a buyer

Google Play's [transfer help page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) covers a sale. The seller must be able to sign in to the original account and the buyer's registration must be complete. Users, ratings, reviews, content ratings and the store listing move across, while payout and earnings reports stay put. With in-app products, the receiving account needs a payments profile, and if its default currency differs the app is unpublished until prices are checked and it is republished. Ad SDKs need updating so ad traffic is credited to the buyer.
