[Kawaii Cute Notes Journal Plan](/apps/kawaii-cute-notes-journal/) is built for people who like their planning tools to look good: its Google Play listing invites users to make their notes, journal, diary, planner and calendar "feel like cute stationery". This page is for **stationery brands, character brands and productivity companies** considering a collaboration, and it also covers licensing and acquisition.

## The app at a glance

For a partner, the product breaks down into writing tools and the decoration around them. The writing side covers a notepad that saves itself, a diary with prompts and streaks, a daily mood log, and a planner whose reminders open the note they belong to. The decoration side is where brands come in, and it is described in the next section. Home screen widgets extend both onto the phone's front screen, and the essential writing and planning features run without a connection. The app is free with ads; the listing says every customisation feature is available with ads, and Google Play lists no in-app purchases.

## Design surfaces a brand could use

The listing names the elements that carry the app's style: **pastel and patterned themes, stickers, notebook covers, custom backgrounds and widget themes**. Each is a natural place for a collaboration:

| Surface | Collaboration idea |
| --- | --- |
| Stickers | A brand's characters or motifs as a sticker set for journal and mood entries |
| Notebook covers | Seasonal or product-linked cover designs |
| Themes and backgrounds | A coordinated palette matching a physical stationery range |
| Widget themes | Home-screen designs that echo the same range |

Any such pack would follow agreed artwork rights and approvals, and would be labelled if sponsored.

## Stationery brands

A company selling notebooks, stickers and pens could link its physical and digital ranges: the same cover design on paper and in the app, for instance, or a sticker set released with a new collection. **Cross-promotion** and **sponsorship** fit here, alongside **co-development**.

## Character brands

The app's audience includes, in the words of the app's page on this site, "people who like their apps to look like cute stationery". A character licensor could explore a themed collection across stickers, covers and widgets. **Licensing** in either direction, the brand's characters into the app or the app's style into a brand's products, can be discussed.

## Productivity companies

For companies building productivity tools, the interest is different: a friendly planner with **recurring tasks, reminders and widgets**. Possible routes include **integrations supported by existing capabilities**, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, **localisation** and **distribution**.

## Privacy is not part of the deal

Journals are personal, and the listing treats them that way: the app or a notebook can be **locked where supported**, and **private text can be hidden** from widgets and notification previews. A collaboration is about design, content and promotion. It would not involve access to anyone's notes, journal or moods, and proposals that depend on that are not ones Reign Creative will pursue.

## A worked example: a back-to-school collection

A stationery brand launches a back-to-school range with three cover designs. In the same week, the app offers a matching set: three notebook covers, a 24-sticker pack for planner entries, and a countdown widget theme for the first day of term. The countdown widget is a listed feature; the brand supplies artwork; the pack is marked as a collaboration. Students who already use the app see a range that matches their paper notebooks. If the collection does well, a spring set can follow; if it does not, nothing in the app depends on it, because the brand pack sits alongside the standard themes rather than replacing them.

## Buying the journal app and moving it on Play

Reign Creative LLC is open to serious discussions about **acquiring this app alone, alongside the other utilities, or with the full portfolio**. No **prices, valuations, revenue or user numbers** appear here, and nothing on this page is an offer.

A sale would complete through Google Play's **transfer process between developer accounts**. Google's help confirms that the store listing, ratings, reviews and users move with an app, while **promotions and test groups do not**. It also asks owners to update linked services, such as Firebase or Google Analytics, and to update **ad SDK integrations** so ad traffic is credited to the correct account.
