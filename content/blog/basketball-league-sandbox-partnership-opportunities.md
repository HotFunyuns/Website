Basketball League Sandbox is not an action game, not a forecasting tool and not an official league product. Starting with what it is not saves partners time, because what remains is unusually specific: a league-office simulator where the rules, finances and history are the playthings. That is why Reign Creative LLC thinks the best conversations about it are with league-simulation communities, educators who teach sports management, and publishers of strategy games.

## Three limits a partner should know first

The app's page on this site is direct about these:

1. **No hands-on play.** Games are simulated and then watched as replays.
2. **No predictions.** Simulated outcomes are the game's own and are not predictions of any real season.
3. **A learning curve.** The depth of options takes time to learn compared with a quick draft game.

On players, the listing says leagues start with real-player rosters and fictional players fill spots once expansion exhausts the pool. The app page adds that ratings and results are the simulation's own, and that the game is not affiliated with, endorsed by or licensed by any league, team, players' association or athlete. Anything involving official marks would need that rights holder's licence.

## What the commissioner's chair actually controls

With those limits stated, here is the material a partner would work with, grouped by what each piece is good for.

- **Governance levers.** In the commissioner role you set the rulebook, look into each club's money and judge whether the schedule treats teams fairly. That is the core of the app and the part with the clearest teaching or community value.
- **Experiment tools.** The sandbox side offers ready-made scenarios, a way to define triggers yourself, and open-ended what-if tests. A separate unlimited drafting mode exists for anyone who simply wants to assemble a roster.
- **Watchable output.** Every simulated contest can be reviewed afterwards with a map of shots, a chart of momentum swings and variable playback, and you can skip to the closing quarter or the last two minutes.
- **Continuity.** Between seasons an eighteen-stage offseason moves rosters and develops players; standings, power rankings and statistics track each year; a newsroom and timeline remember the upsets and dynasties. Leagues can also link up, with promotion and relegation, loans, national sides and a custom tournament builder.

It is free to download, ad-supported, and Google Play lists in-app purchases. For the consumer explanation, read the [Basketball League Sandbox guide](/blog/basketball-league-sandbox-guide/) or visit the [app page](/apps/basketball-league-sandbox/).

## Where a classroom conversation could start

Educators in sports management are a natural fit, and the reason is Commissioner Mode's subject matter: rules, finances and scheduling are classroom topics. A lecturer might ask students to change one rule and explain what the simulation does with it, or to compare the 18 offseason steps with how a real offseason runs, a topic the site covers in [how the NBA offseason works](/blog/how-the-nba-offseason-works/). The responsible framing is that the sandbox is a discussion prompt; its outputs are not evidence about real leagues.

A worked example of a term plan: with 18 offseason steps and a 12-week module, a class covering one step per week would reach step 12 by the end and need 18 − 12 = 6 more weeks for the rest, so pairing steps (two a week) would finish in 9 weeks with 3 to spare for a final project. Any real pilot would be shaped with the institution.

## League-sim communities and strategy publishers

League-sim communities tend to run long saves and share the stories they produce. The newsroom, timeline, tournament creator and connected leagues give a community partner material for shared leagues or themed scenarios built with the trigger builder. A strategy publisher might see the game as a complement to franchise titles; the site's [league sandbox versus franchise GM comparison](/blog/league-sandbox-vs-franchise-gm/) sets out the difference. Licensing, white-label where feasible, co-developed scenario packs, localisation and distribution are all things we would discuss.

## Starting the conversation, and what a sale involves

The [partnerships page](/partnerships/) has a form that composes an email; or write directly to ReignCreativeSupport@gmail.com. Introduce yourself and the idea in a few lines, and keep confidential, financial or personal details out of a first message. A call follows if there is a fit. All discussion is nonbinding, and any licence or sale depends on due diligence and a signed agreement.

If an organisation wants to buy the app, Google Play's [transfer process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) applies. Both developer accounts must be registered and active, and the seller needs the target account's registration transaction ID for the request. Google says integrated services such as Google Analytics, Firebase and Play game services need their permissions and links updated, because those linkage settings do not transfer, and ad SDK integrations must be updated so ad traffic is credited correctly. Apps with in-app products need an active payments profile on the receiving account.
