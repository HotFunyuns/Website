A Google Play listing can carry a short line near the install button: **In-app purchases**. It tells you before you download that the app sells something inside it. This guide explains what it means, the kinds of purchase it covers and how to avoid paying for something by accident, using Google Play's own help.

## What counts as an in-app purchase

Google defines in-app purchases as additional **content or services you can buy within an app or game**, and gives three examples:

- **a sword** that gives you more power in a game;
- **a key** that unlocks more features of an app;
- **virtual currency** that can be used for purchases.

Google's key example, unlocking more features, is the same idea as a premium upgrade, and Google's subscription help covers recurring plans bought through Google Play.

## Where the label appears

Google says you can check before downloading:

- in the **Google Play Store app**, the in-app purchases notice appears **by the Price or Install button**;
- on **play.google.com**, you will see **"Offers in-app purchases"** below the app's name.

The label tells you purchases exist, not what they cost, so check the price during checkout before you confirm. Some listings also describe their paid options in the description.

## One-off purchases and subscriptions

In-app purchases come in two broad kinds:

| | One-off purchase | Subscription |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you pay | Once | At the start of every billing cycle |
| Example | A lifetime premium unlock, a coin pack | A monthly or yearly premium plan |
| Ends when | It is used or kept for good | You cancel |
| Where to manage | Google Play's Budget & order history | Google Play's subscriptions page |

Google's subscription help is clear on the second kind: subscriptions are for an **indefinite term** and are charged according to their terms, such as weekly or annually, **unless you unsubscribe**, and **uninstalling an app does not cancel its subscription**. [The Google Play refund guide](/blog/google-play-refund-guide/) covers cancelling and refunds.

## A worked example: three ways to pay for one upgrade

Some apps offer the same upgrade more than one way. Our own [Video Player All Formats PRO](/apps/regal-video-player/), for example, has a free version that covers local playback and is supported by ads; an optional **Premium** purchase removes ads and unlocks extra themes, with **monthly, yearly and lifetime** options shown by Google Play. To compare them, work out what you would pay over the time you expect to use the app. Suppose, purely for illustration, a plan costs $2 a month, $10 a year or $20 for life. Over three years that is $72, $30 or $20. If you expect to keep using the app for three years, the lifetime option costs least in this example; if you are unsure, a single month costs least to try. Always check the actual prices shown at checkout.

## Avoiding accidental purchases

Google's help includes one tip worth acting on: **check that purchase verification is turned on** for your device, which helps protect against accidental or unwanted in-app purchases by requiring authentication. It is worth checking on any phone you share, including with children. Google also notes that some apps let you **pre-order** items; you are charged on the date the developer specifies, and you can cancel any time before you are charged.

## Checkout, promotions and promo codes

Google's help describes the checkout for an in-app purchase: find the item in the app, start the checkout, apply any promotion on offer, confirm the payment method, then follow the on-screen instructions. You can change the promotion or the payment method at that stage, and some in-app purchases accept **promotional codes**, redeemed from the payment method menu at checkout. If a purchase does not arrive or work, Google's help on problems with in-app purchases, and the app's developer, are the places to start.

## In-app purchases versus ads

The in-app purchases label is separate from the **Contains ads** label. An app can have either, both or neither. [Why free Android games show ads](/blog/why-free-android-games-show-ads/) explains the ad side. Where an app sells an upgrade that removes ads, as Video Player All Formats PRO does, the two labels appear together.

## What to check before you install

1. **Look for both labels**: ads and in-app purchases.
2. **Read the description** for what the free version includes.
3. **Check the content rating**, especially for children; [Google Play content ratings](/blog/google-play-content-ratings/) explains the labels.
4. **Note whether upgrades are one-off or subscriptions** before paying.
