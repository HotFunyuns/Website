A basketball box score lists every player who played, with one row each and one column per statistic: minutes, shots made and attempted, free throws, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers, fouls, plus-minus and points. Read each row from left to right as a story of one player's game: how long they played, how they scored, what else they did, and what happened to the score while they were out there.

The fastest way to learn the columns is to read one line slowly. The line below is invented for illustration; it does not describe any real player or game. The definitions come from NBA.com's stats glossary, the league's own reference.

## One line, read slowly

| MIN | FGM-FGA | 3PM-3PA | FTM-FTA | OREB | DREB | REB | AST | STL | BLK | TOV | PF | +/- | PTS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 34 | 9-17 | 3-7 | 4-5 | 2 | 6 | 8 | 7 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 2 | +6 | 25 |

### Minutes

**MIN** is minutes played. It is the first number to read because it is the denominator for everything else: 25 points in 34 minutes is a different performance from 25 points in 44.

### Shooting

**FGM-FGA** is field goals made and attempted. The glossary is explicit that each "includes both 2 pointers and 3 pointers", so the 9-17 above contains the three-pointers too. **3PM-3PA** is three-point field goals made and attempted, and **FTM-FTA** is free throws made and attempted.

Box scores show percentages, but the pairs matter more. The attempts tell you how much to trust the percentage: 3-7 from three is 42.9%, but on seven shots a single make either way would have moved it by more than ten points.

### Rebounds

**OREB** counts rebounds collected while on offence, **DREB** rebounds collected while on defence, and **REB** the total. Offensive rebounds are the rarer kind: in 2025-26 they were about a quarter of all rebounds, 11.4 of 43.8 per team-game in Basketball-Reference's league averages. The glossary adds a nuance: "an Offensive Rebound does not create another possession, it simply makes the existing possession longer."

### Playmaking and mistakes

**AST** counts "passes that lead directly to a made basket". **TOV** counts each time the player "on offense loses the ball to the defense". Assists divided by turnovers gives the assist-to-turnover ratio, here 7 ÷ 3 ≈ 2.3.

### The defensive columns

**STL** counts steals, **BLK** blocked shots and **PF** personal fouls. These, plus defensive rebounds, are nearly all the defence a box score records.

### Plus-minus and points

**+/-** is "the point differential when a player or team is on the floor". Here the team outscored its opponent by six during this player's 34 minutes. **PTS** is total points.

## Checking the arithmetic

A box score is internally consistent, and checking it is the best way to learn it.

- **Points** equal 2 × two-point makes + 3 × three-point makes + free throws made. There were 9 field goals and 3 of them were threes, so 6 twos: 2 × 6 + 3 × 3 + 4 = 25.
- **Rebounds** equal offensive plus defensive: 2 + 6 = 8.
- **Two-point shooting** is not printed, but you can derive it: (9 - 3) makes from (17 - 7) attempts is 6 for 10, or 60%.

## Three numbers worth calculating yourself

The printed field-goal percentage, 9 ÷ 17 = 52.9%, treats a three as no more valuable than a two. Three quick calculations fix that, using the formulas in NBA.com's glossary:

| Measure | Formula | This line | 2025-26 league average |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Effective field-goal % (eFG%) | (FGM + 0.5 × 3PM) ÷ FGA | (9 + 1.5) ÷ 17 = 61.8% | 54.6% |
| True shooting % (TS%) | PTS ÷ [2 × (FGA + 0.44 × FTA)] | 25 ÷ [2 × 19.2] = 65.1% | 58.1% |
| Assist-to-turnover | AST ÷ TOV | 7 ÷ 3 = 2.3 | n/a |

The league averages come from Basketball-Reference's per-game table for 2025-26. By either shooting measure, this invented line is well above average. To run both shooting formulas on any line of your own, the [true shooting percentage calculator](/tools/basketball/true-shooting-percentage-calculator/) shows TS% and eFG% side by side. True shooting is the more complete of the two, because it also counts what the player did at the free-throw line.

## Double-doubles and triple-doubles

NBA.com's glossary defines a double-double as a "double-digit number total in two of the five categories in a game", and a triple-double as the same in three. The example line, with 25 points, 8 rebounds and 7 assists, is neither. Two more rebounds would have made it a double-double.

The labels are thresholds, not quality judgements. A 10-point, 10-rebound game counts, while a 40-point game with 9 rebounds, 9 assists and single figures elsewhere does not. Read them as a description of a stat line's shape, not as a ranking.

## Same stats, different labels

Basketball-Reference abbreviates several columns differently. The numbers mean the same; only the headings change.

| NBA.com | Basketball-Reference | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FGM / FGA | FG / FGA | Field goals made / attempted, twos and threes together |
| 3PM / 3PA | 3P / 3PA | Three-point field goals made / attempted |
| FTM / FTA | FT / FTA | Free throws made / attempted |
| OREB / DREB / REB | ORB / DRB / TRB | Offensive, defensive and total rebounds |
| TOV | TOV | Turnovers |
| +/- | +/- | Points scored minus points allowed while on the floor |

Basketball-Reference's glossary also notes when each column begins in league records: three-pointers from 1979-80, steals, blocks and offensive rebounds from 1973-74, and turnovers from 1977-78. Older box scores are missing those columns because the league did not keep them.

## Three things a box score cannot tell you

**Most of defence.** Steals, blocks, defensive rebounds and fouls are all it records. The author of Box Plus/Minus puts it bluntly: on defence "the box score is quite limited", while on offence it misses things like screen-setting. A defender who forces a bad shot without touching the ball leaves no trace.

**Context.** Totals depend on minutes, on the team's pace and on the player's role. Twenty-five points mean more on a slow team than a fast one. [Basketball advanced stats explained](/blog/basketball-advanced-stats-explained/) shows how per-possession numbers correct for that.

**Who caused the plus-minus.** A single game's +/- counts every point scored while the player was on the floor, by all ten players. It is a lineup result first and a personal one a distant second; over many games it becomes more informative, but one night's number is noisy.

## A 30-second reading routine

1. Find the minutes, and set expectations by them.
2. Read the shooting pairs and note the attempts before the percentages.
3. Check free-throw attempts, which show how often a player got to the line.
4. Look at assists against turnovers for anyone who handles the ball.
5. Glance at rebounds by position: a guard's 8 rebounds and a centre's 8 are different nights.
6. Read plus-minus last, and lightly.

Positions change what a good line looks like, which [basketball positions explained](/blog/basketball-positions-explained/) covers.
