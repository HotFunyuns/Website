Reign Creative LLC would welcome three kinds of conversation about Football Career Pro League: an arcade sports publisher or studio that wants to co-develop it, a regional distributor that wants to carry it into new markets, and a buyer who wants to own it. This page takes those in turn, then covers what we cannot offer and how an ownership change would run on Google Play.

## The game behind the conversation

Here is the short version, drawn from its Google Play listing. You create an American football player, train passing, running and defense, prepare for the draft and fight for a role. On the field you read the defence as a quarterback, find space for passes, attack openings on running plays, and switch to defense to stop the other side. The store screenshots describe arcade 11-on-11 football and a 17-game season.

Beyond the career, the listing names season, commissioner and Team Play modes, so the league can be seen from a team's side as well. A depth chart, the draft, training and equipment, daily rewards and trophies frame the progression. Standard, Wide and Full Field cameras change how much of the play you see, and separate career saves let players run different journeys. It is free, ad-supported, and Google Play lists no in-app purchases. Its decision-driven sibling is [Football Career Sim 2027](/apps/football-career-sim/); the site's [comparison of football career games](/blog/football-career-games-compared/) sets the two side by side.

## Conversation one: co-development with an arcade sports studio

The game's systems are the obvious place a co-developer would plug in. Candidate projects, all of which would be new work agreed in writing:

- **Equipment and training content.** The listing says equipment supports player development; a partner could design new items or training drills within that structure.
- **Mode depth.** Commissioner and Team Play modes exist; a partner with management-game experience could extend them.
- **Camera and presentation.** With three framings already in place, a studio with broadcast-presentation skills could refine how plays read on a phone.

The listing mentions no online multiplayer, so anything head-to-head would be built from scratch, and we would scope it honestly as such.

## Conversation two: a regional distributor

A distributor brings store presence, marketing and local knowledge. American football's draft-and-depth-chart vocabulary may need explaining in markets where the sport is less familiar, which is the kind of advice a regional partner gives; pieces like [how the NFL draft works](/blog/how-does-the-nfl-draft-work/) show the concepts involved. Localising menus and store text would be a project, since the listing does not describe language options.

A worked example of release planning: with the screenshots' 17-game season, a distributor running a launch promotion that tracked one in-game week per calendar day would cover a full season in 17 days, or 2 weeks and 3 days. That is a promotional sketch, not a commitment.

## Conversation three: buying the app

Acquisition of this app on its own, with related titles, or as part of the portfolio is open for discussion. The usual sequence applies: a first email, a call to test the fit, confidential detail under NDA for qualified parties, then diligence and a signed agreement. Everything before signature is nonbinding. A buyer would also want to look at the app alongside its decision-led sibling, since the two cover the same sport from opposite ends — one built on calls, one on controls — and could be acquired separately or together.

## What we will not put on the table

We do not publish prices, valuations, revenue or user figures, and we do not make binding offers on a website. We cannot license real leagues, teams or players, because the game uses none. And we will not describe features the game does not have; the player-facing [Football Career Pro League guide](/blog/football-career-pro-league-guide/) and the [app page](/apps/football-career-pro-league/) describe what it does today.

## Moving the listing to a new owner

Google's [help page on transferring apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) governs the handover. Its payments-profile requirement applies to paid apps or apps with in-app products; Google's tip says that if only free apps are being transferred, you move on once the earlier steps are done. Those earlier steps still matter: downloading reports you may need, since bulk export, payout and earnings reports stay behind, and confirming both developer accounts are registered and active. Because the game shows ads, the new owner must update ad SDK integrations, including AdMob, so traffic is credited to the right account. Test groups cannot be transferred and closed tests would be recreated. The request is approved by the target developer, and Google's support team replies within two business days.
