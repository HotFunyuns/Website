**Soccer has one position defined by the rules and about ten defined by habit.** The Laws of the Game distinguish the goalkeeper — [Law 3](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-players/) requires each team to have one — and say nothing about anybody else. Everything from "centre-back" to "false nine" is vocabulary the sport invented to describe what people were already doing.

That is worth knowing, because it explains why positional names keep changing while the underlying jobs stay recognisable.

## The positions, by defensive responsibility

The most reliable way to understand a position is to ask what it is responsible for when the other team has the ball. Attacking freedom varies with the system; defensive duty is much more stable.

| Position | Defensive job | Attacking contribution | Attributes that carry it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goalkeeper | Last line; organise the defence | Distribution | Goalkeeping, positioning |
| Centre-back | Defend the central area, win aerial duels | Build-up, set pieces | Defending, strength, positioning |
| Full-back | Defend the wide area | Overlap, cross | Defending, pace, stamina |
| Wing-back | Wide area, but higher up | Provide width for the whole team | Pace, stamina, crossing |
| Defensive midfielder | Screen the back line, intercept | Recycle possession | Positioning, passing, awareness |
| Central midfielder | Cover ground, press | Progress the ball | Passing, stamina, vision |
| Attacking midfielder | Press from the front | Create chances | Vision, dribbling, finishing |
| Winger | Track back on their flank | Beat the full-back, cross or cut in | Pace, dribbling |
| Centre-forward | Press the centre-backs | Score, hold the ball up | Finishing, strength, movement |

### Full-back is the position that changed most

A full-back was once a defender who occasionally crossed. In most modern systems they are a primary source of attacking width, because the wingers have moved inside. The stamina requirement is now among the highest on the pitch, and the position asks a player to be genuinely good at two opposite jobs.

The **wing-back** is the version of this that gives up on defending as the primary duty. It requires a back three behind it, because someone has to cover the space the wing-back vacates.

### Centre-forward is the position that fragmented

There is no longer one job at the top of the pitch. Depending on the system, a forward may be asked to:

- hold the ball with their back to goal while the midfield joins;
- run in behind a high defensive line;
- drop into midfield to drag centre-backs out of position (the "false nine");
- lead a press rather than score.

Those need different players. A team that signs a target man and asks them to press is not going to get what it paid for.

## Offside changes what forwards can do

[Law 11](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/offside/) is why forward play is a timing problem rather than a positioning one. A forward cannot simply stand behind the defence; being in an offside position when the ball is played toward them makes the pass useless.

That single rule generates most of the movement you see at the top of the pitch — runs across defenders, dropping short before spinning, holding the line before breaking it. Understanding it makes forward play legible in a way that watching without it does not.

## Positions versus roles

The last thirty years of football tactics have mostly been about splitting positions into roles. A "central midfielder" might be a deep-lying playmaker, a ball-winner, a box-to-box runner or a late-arriving goalscorer, and those four have almost nothing in common besides where they stand at kick-off.

The practical rule when reading a lineup: **the shape tells you where people start, not what they will do.** [Soccer formations explained](/blog/soccer-formations-explained/) covers what each shape is trying to achieve and what it concedes, which is the missing half of this picture.
