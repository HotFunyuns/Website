Google Play lets you update Android apps one at a time, all together or automatically, and the right choice depends on your data plan and how much you want to approve. This guide explains how updating works on Android, how to set it up so it happens at sensible times, and which updates can safely wait, using Google's own Play Store and Android help.

## Why updates matter

Google's help gives the reasons in one line: updating apps to the latest version gives you **access to the latest features** and **improves app security and stability**. It also notes that if Google determines an update fixes a **critical security vulnerability**, it may make certain app updates **regardless of update settings** in the app or on your device.

## Updating apps manually

Google's steps for the Play Store app:

1. Open the **Google Play Store**.
2. Tap your **profile icon** at the top right.
3. Tap **Manage apps & device**.
4. Under **Updates available**, tap **See details**.
5. Tap **Update** next to an app, or **Update all**.

Two things can interrupt an update. Google notes some updates **require new permissions**, and you may be asked to accept them; [Android app permissions explained](/blog/android-app-permissions-explained/) covers what they mean. And some updates need a **restart** of the phone.

## Setting up automatic updates

Google's path for automatic updates: in the Play Store, tap your **profile picture**, then **Settings, Network preferences, Auto-update apps**, and choose one of:

| Option | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| Update over Wi-Fi only | Limited data plans, or anyone happy to wait for Wi-Fi |
| Update over Wi-Fi or mobile data | Unlimited data plans |
| Update with limited amount of mobile data | A middle ground; Google says it is available to a limited group and always tries Wi-Fi first |
| Don't auto-update apps | People who want to approve every update |

You can also switch automatic updates **per app**: in **Manage apps & device**, tap **Manage**, open the app's details page, tap **More** and turn **Enable auto update** on or off. That is useful when you want most apps automatic but one app updated only when you choose. [Stopping apps using mobile data](/blog/stop-apps-using-mobile-data/) covers Wi-Fi-only settings more broadly.

## When automatic updates stop working

If updates are not arriving, check:

- that **auto-update** is on, and you are on **Wi-Fi** if you chose Wi-Fi only;
- whether any account on the phone has a **sign-in error**, which Google says can stop automatic updates;
- whether **Data Saver** is on, which Google notes can affect the Play Store when Wi-Fi is not available;
- whether the phone has enough **storage**; [freeing up storage](/blog/free-up-storage-on-android/) helps if not.

## Reading what changed

Before approving an update you are unsure about, open the app's page in Google Play and read any notes the developer has published about the latest version. If an update also asks for new permissions, compare the request with what the notes say the app now does. A note-taking app asking for the microphone after adding voice notes makes sense; one asking for contacts with no explanation is worth a closer look.

## Update the Play Store itself

The Play Store app has its own update. Google's path: in the Play Store, tap your **profile picture**, then **Settings, About, Update Play Store**. You will see whether it is up to date; if not, the update downloads and installs in a few minutes.

## System updates are separate

Android itself updates separately from apps. Google's Android help says you get notifications when updates are available, and that **most system updates and security patches install automatically**. To check manually, open **Settings, System, Software updates**; Google notes that update schedules vary by device, manufacturer and mobile carrier, and menus can differ on other makers' phones. Google adds that updates can be large and suggests charging your device to **at least 75%** first.

## A worked example: when an update can wait

You are on a train with a 10 GB data plan and the Play Store shows twelve updates. With **Wi-Fi only** selected, they simply wait until you are home. One of them, though, is your banking app, which you need now; open its details page and update it alone, accepting the mobile data cost. Meanwhile, security updates Google judges critical may install anyway. The result: routine updates on Wi-Fi, urgent ones when you need them.
