PDF is the format paperwork travels in. Wikipedia describes it as a file format developed by Adobe in 1993, standardised as **ISO 32000**, that presents a document the same way regardless of software, hardware or operating system. That fixed layout suits forms, reports and records, and it is also why changing a PDF needs dedicated tools. [PDF All in One Tool No Limit](/apps/pdf-all-in-one-tools/) puts the common PDF jobs in one Android app; its listing counts **82 tools**. This guide maps each everyday job to the right tool.

## What the app is for

The listing names the paperwork it is meant for: school notes, office reports, business forms, receipts, invoices, applications, contracts and personal records. The app's page on this site describes a four-step flow:

1. **Open or create a PDF**: from a file on your device, by scanning paper pages, or from images, photos or text.
2. **Pick the tool** the job needs.
3. **Make the changes**, then review the result.
4. **Save and share**: compress if needed, rename and organise in the PDF manager, then send.

## Fixing pages before anything else

Some PDF problems are page problems: a scan saved sideways, an extra blank page, two files that should be one. The page tools cover them: **rearrange, rotate, remove and extract pages**, **merge** several files into one, or **split** a long document into smaller ones. Work in that order when preparing a document: fix individual pages, combine files, then split off anything that should travel separately.

## From paper to PDF

The **document scanner** turns paper pages such as notes, receipts, forms and letters into PDFs. The app's page on this site notes one limit: pages are photographed with your phone camera, so **lighting and focus affect the result**. Flat pages, even light and a steady hand help.

## Forms, signatures and sending

For forms, the app lets you **complete fields, add text and sign**, which covers applications, school forms, work documents and agreements. Before sending, **compress** the file if it is large: Google notes that personal Gmail accounts have a **25 MB** attachment limit, and Gmail turns bigger attachments into Drive links. A worked example with assumed figures: if a 30-page scan averaged 1.5 MB a page, it would total 30 × 1.5 = 45 MB, too big for that limit. To fit under 25 MB, the pages would need to average under 25 ÷ 30 ≈ 0.83 MB each after compression. Real sizes depend on the pages, so check the finished file's size before you send.

## Keeping documents tidy

The **PDF manager** lets you review, organise and rename files before sharing. Renaming is worth the few seconds: "2026-10 lease signed.pdf" is easier to find later than "scan_0042.pdf". The **protection tools** help you manage sensitive paperwork; for how PDF passwords work in general, and what they can and cannot do, see [password-protecting a PDF](/blog/how-to-password-protect-a-pdf/).

## Three common jobs, start to finish

**An expense claim.** Scan each receipt with the document scanner, merge the scans into one file in date order, rename it with the month and purpose, and compress it if the claims system has an upload limit.

**A school assignment.** Create a PDF from photos of handwritten pages, rotate any sideways pages, remove blurred duplicates, then merge the result with a typed cover page before submitting.

**A signed form.** Open the form, complete the fields, add text where there are no fields, sign it, then extract only the pages the recipient asked for and send that file, keeping the full copy for your records.

Each job uses three or four tools in sequence, which is the reason to have them in one app.
