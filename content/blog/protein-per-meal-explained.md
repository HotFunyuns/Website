**"The body can only use 30 grams of protein per meal" is a simplification of a narrower and more careful finding.** The research it comes from is about the muscle-building response to a single dose — not about digestion, and not about whether protein above that amount is useful.

Understanding what the claim actually says makes it far less anxiety-inducing, and changes what you should do about it.

## What the research is about

The relevant question in the literature is roughly: *how much protein in a single feeding maximises the acute rate of muscle protein synthesis?* Studies find the response rises with dose and then plateaus — there is a point beyond which more protein in one sitting does not further increase that particular measure.

The review by Schoenfeld and Aragon, [published in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29497353/), examines this question directly and considers what it implies for how protein should be distributed across a day.

Two things the finding does **not** say:

**It does not say the extra protein is wasted.** Digestion and absorption are not the limiting step being measured. Protein above the plateau is still absorbed and used — for the body's other protein needs, or with its components used for energy.

**It does not fix a universal number.** The dose at which the response plateaus varies with body size, the protein source, what else is in the meal and the population studied. A single figure quoted without those qualifications is doing more work than the evidence supports.

## What matters more

Across the literature on protein and training, **total daily intake is consistently the larger factor**. The meta-analysis by [Morton and colleagues in the British Journal of Sports Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/) examined protein supplementation and resistance-training outcomes and is a standard reference on the size of the effect.

The practical ordering that follows:

1. **Get your daily total consistent.** This is where the effect is.
2. **Then consider distribution.** Spreading intake across meals is a reasonable practice, and the reviews treat it as such.
3. **Then consider timing around training**, which is the smallest of the three factors.

Most people are still working on step one. Optimising step three while step one is inconsistent is effort in the wrong place.

## Why the "per meal" idea persists

Two reasons, both understandable.

**It is actionable.** "Eat 30 grams per meal" is a rule you can follow. "Total daily intake matters most, and the per-meal plateau varies" is accurate and harder to act on.

**It sells things.** A rule that implies you need protein at every meal creates a use case for supplements. That does not make the underlying research wrong, but it explains why the simplified version travels further than the careful one.

## The practical version

If you want a defensible way to think about it:

- **Aim for a daily total** that suits your circumstances. [How much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day/) covers what the published reference intakes actually say and who they apply to.
- **Spread it roughly evenly** across the meals you already eat, because that is easy and the reviews consider it reasonable.
- **Do not restructure your eating** around hitting a per-meal number if that makes the daily total harder to sustain. Consistency beats optimisation.
- **Do not conclude that a large protein meal was wasted.** It was not.

## Populations where this is discussed differently

The research on older adults is a genuine exception worth flagging. Some position papers argue for higher per-meal amounts in older people specifically, on the basis that the muscle-building response is less sensitive at a given dose. [Protein for older adults](/blog/protein-for-older-adults/) covers that literature and what it does and does not establish.

If you are older, managing a health condition, pregnant, or taking medication that affects kidney function, none of the general discussion above is a substitute for advice about you specifically.
