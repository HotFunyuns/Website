Google says millions of sites and apps use its ad technology to fund their content, and those ads can be **personalised**: chosen using activity and information linked to you. Android lets you limit that in a few places, and none of them is the whole answer on its own. This guide covers the advertising ID, Google's My Ad Center and what you can expect to see afterwards.

## The advertising ID

Google describes the **advertising ID** as a unique, **user-resettable and user-deletable** ID for advertising, provided by Google Play services. Google Play's developer policy requires apps to use it, rather than other device identifiers, for advertising purposes. That makes it the main lever on your phone.

You have two options, both in Android Settings:

| Action | Path | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reset | Settings, Privacy, Ads, Reset advertising ID | You get a new ID in place of the old one |
| Delete | Settings, Privacy, Ads, Delete advertising ID | The ID is removed; apps that ask get a string of zeros |

Google notes that on some **older versions of Android**, the path is **Settings, Privacy, Advanced, Ads**. It also explains that since a Google Play services update rolled out from late 2021 to April 2022, deleting the ID means any attempt to access it receives **a string of zeros** instead of the identifier.

## Step by step

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Privacy**, then **Ads** (or **Privacy, Advanced, Ads** on some older phones).
3. Choose **Delete advertising ID** and confirm, or **Reset advertising ID** if you prefer a fresh one.

Google adds a caveat worth knowing: your advertising ID will be deleted, but **apps may have their own settings**, which can also affect the ads you see.

## Google's own ad settings

The advertising ID is one input. Google's **My Ad Center** explains that personalised ads on Google services can be based on **activity saved to your Google Account**, including searches, YouTube videos watched, apps installed on your Android device and ads you have interacted with, along with your general area and the choices you make in My Ad Center itself.

Two details matter:

- Choices in My Ad Center, such as preferred topics and brands, **only affect ads on Google services**.
- Ads on **partner sites and apps** that use Google's technology have a separate **partner ad settings** control.

In the European Economic Area, Google notes that using account activity for ad personalisation is also subject to your linked services settings.

## What you will still see

Turning personalisation off does not turn ads off. Google explains that **non-personalised ads** are chosen by **contextual factors**: the time of day, the topic of the site or app you are using, your current location or what you are actively searching for. Its examples include a lunch-special ad at noon and a plumber's ad while you search for plumbing. Context can still be specific: Google notes that if you search for "umbrellas" soon after searching for "patio furniture", you might see ads for patio umbrellas rather than golf umbrellas.

## Why partner settings matter in apps

Apps you install are not Google services, but they can still carry ads served through Google. Google explains that websites and apps often reserve space for ads and partner with Google or other ad technology providers to fill it, and that millions of sites and apps use the **Google Display Network** to fund their content. That is why the **partner ad settings** control matters for games and free apps, not just for Google Search and YouTube.

## A worked example: one phone, three settings

Suppose you want fewer targeted ads in games. You delete the advertising ID, so apps that ask for it receive zeros. While signed in to your Google Account, you turn off personalised ads in My Ad Center; Google says this also turns off personalised ads on partner sites and apps. If you also browse signed out, you set partner ad settings separately, because Google says that choice applies only to the browser or device you are using. You will still see ads in free games, now based on context rather than a profile, as far as Google's ads are concerned. Google notes that partner ad settings do not affect ads not shown by Google or ads from other ad platforms, and that apps may have their own settings, so check those too.
