This overview is for language schools, publishers and distributors weighing whether [Regal - Learn Russian Language](/apps/learn-russian/) could carry their name, reach their learners or join their catalogue. It covers the course as it stands, the arrangements Reign Creative LLC will discuss, and the practical route if a licence or sale went ahead.

## The course in one paragraph

The app's page on this site describes a beginner course that "does not run out of road". It **starts with the Cyrillic alphabet**, then moves through greetings, numbers, family, food, travel, work and everyday life, introducing vocabulary in context. **Cases, gender, agreement and verb forms** arrive in stages, and the exercises explain why an answer works. Pronunciation stays tied to listening. Past the core path sit **stories, documents, roleplays, culture lessons and history**, with saved words, missed-question practice, daily goals and achievements for review. Russian speakers can **reverse the course** and study English.

The app is free to download and supported by ads; optional in-app purchases include **premium learning packs**.

## Which licensing models fit this material

Three features shape what a licensee could do with it.

**Grammar sequencing.** Because cases and verb forms are staged and explained, a school could map its own syllabus onto the app's order and assign units as homework between lessons.

**Longer reading material.** The stories, documents and roleplays give teachers something beyond drills. A publisher with graded readers might see a pairing: the app for vocabulary and grammar, the books for extended reading.

**Two directions.** The English course for Russian speakers means one product serves two audiences, which matters to a distributor working in both directions.

The arrangements Reign Creative LLC is open to discussing include **licensing**, **publishing and distribution**, **white-label versions where technically feasible**, **localisation**, **co-development** of new units, **integration** with tools the app already supports, **sponsorship** and **cross-promotion**.

## Distribution partners

For distributors, the useful questions are where your learners are and what they need first. A partner focused on travellers might lead with the restaurant, airport and hotel situations the listing mentions; a partner serving exam candidates might lead with grammar. Localising the interface or support material into another language is a conversation worth having early, because it affects every other plan.

## Claims a partner can safely repeat

Marketing that describes the app should stay inside what the app's own pages say. The app's page on this site is direct on two points: **no app can promise fluency**, and the course aims at **a solid beginner foundation**: the alphabet, working grammar, useful vocabulary and phrases for everyday situations. It also notes that **some premium learning packs are paid in-app purchases**. A school that recommends the app to students, or a publisher that bundles it, should describe it in the same terms, so that what learners are promised matches what they get.

## Questions worth bringing to a first conversation

- Which learners would see the app: adult evening classes, university beginners, travellers or Russian speakers learning English?
- Would you use it as it stands, rebrand it, or need new units, such as exam vocabulary?
- What would you contribute: teachers, recorded audio, editorial review, distribution or marketing?
- Which markets and app stores matter to you?

Answers to these let both sides see quickly whether a licence, a distribution deal or something else makes sense.

## The Google Play transfer, step by step

A sale would mean moving the app to the buyer's developer account through **Google Play's formal transfer process**. Google's Play Console help sets out the main points:

- Both the original and target **developer accounts must be registered and active**; a buyer creating a new account pays Google's **US$25 registration fee**.
- The request needs each account's **registration transaction ID**.
- For apps using **Play App Signing**, the new owner can request a **new upload key**, and Google describes a key-upgrade route for the app signing key.
- **Users, download statistics, ratings and reviews** move with the app; **orders created before the transfer stay** with the original account.
- **Test groups cannot be transferred** and must be recreated.
- Google's support team **replies to transfer requests within 2 business days**, after the target developer approves.
