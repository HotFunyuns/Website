From hits and RBIs to ERA and WHIP, Baseball Career Sim 2026 keeps a full statistical record for one player you create. Reign Creative LLC built the game and owns it outright, and we are looking for conversations with baseball training programmes, game publishers and sports media. This page explains the game, the fit for each partner, and how a transaction would move through Google Play.

## The game, position by position

Its Google Play listing describes a career that starts with choices of position, archetype, batting side, throwing hand and starting strengths. Supported paths are hitter, pitcher, catcher, closer, utility player and two-way star. The career runs through high school, college, the draft, development and the professional game, and each stage depends on the last.

Games resolve through a simulator that the listing calls position-aware: innings, outs, counts, runners, batting order, pitch counts, bullpen calls, extra innings and walk-offs, with a catcher and a closer asked different questions in the same game. Contact, power, plate vision, speed, baserunning, fielding, arm strength, control, velocity, stamina and baseball IQ develop with position, performance, age and training. Season lines — hits, home runs, RBIs, stolen bases, average, on-base percentage, slugging, ERA, WHIP, strikeouts, wins and saves — roll into career totals, awards, playoff runs, retirement and the Hall of Fame.

It is free to download, ad-supported, with optional in-app purchases. Every league, team and player is fictional. Players can read the [Baseball Career Sim 2026 guide](/blog/baseball-career-sim-guide/); the [app page](/apps/baseball-career-sim/) has the store details.

## Training programmes: a careful fit

A baseball programme might find the game useful as a conversation starter about roles: why a closer's job differs from a starter's, or how a utility player's value works. The site's explainer on [pitching roles](/blog/baseball-pitching-roles-explained/) covers the real-world side. The listing's note that development depends on age, performance and training also connects to topics like [aging curves](/blog/aging-curves-in-career-sims/).

We would be clear in any partnership that the game is entertainment. It does not teach mechanics and we would not claim it improves anyone's play. What a programme could reasonably discuss with us is a themed edition, co-written scenarios, or cross-promotion to families.

## Sports media and the stat line

For a media partner, the full statistical record is the hook. Season and career lines give a newsletter or channel something to analyse — for instance, a "simulated career of the month" feature. Sponsored segments or events would be new work and would have to suit the game's audience. Because the game uses no real teams, a partner's own branding could appear only in ways we agree in writing, and any real league marks would need their owners' licence.

A worked example a media partner could use when explaining the stats: batting average is hits divided by at-bats, as the site's [beginner's guide to baseball stats](/blog/baseball-stats-explained-for-beginners/) sets out, so a simulated season of 162 hits in 540 at-bats comes to 162 ÷ 540 = .300. Showing readers how the game's season lines are built from the same formulas fans already argue about is an easy first piece, and the simulated player's career totals give the series somewhere to go once the first season is done.

## Publishers: six paths in one title

A publisher gets a single game with six distinct ways to play it, which helps marketing reach both hitters' and pitchers' fans. Publishing in new regions, distribution, localisation and co-development of further content are all open for discussion, as is acquisition of this app, a group of apps or the portfolio. Beginners in new markets may need the basics; the site's [baseball rules for beginners](/blog/baseball-rules-for-beginners/) shows the kind of explanation involved.

## Google's transfer steps, in order

Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) lists preparation before a transfer request:

1. **Policy and fee tier.** The original account and the app must comply with policy; Google also explains how earnings count across Account Groups for its service-fee tier.
2. **Reports.** Download bulk export, payout and earnings reports you may need, since they stay behind.
3. **Active accounts.** Both developer accounts must be registered and active.
4. **Transaction ID.** Find the target account's registration transaction ID.

Because this app has in-app products, the target needs a payments profile, and ad SDK integrations must be updated after the move. The target developer then approves the request, and Google's support team responds within two business days.
