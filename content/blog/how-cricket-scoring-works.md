A cricket score such as 250/6 packs a lot into a few characters: runs scored, wickets lost and, on a full scorecard, the overs used. This guide goes through every way a run can be added under the Laws of Cricket in MCC's 2026 edition, then scores one over ball by ball.

## Runs from the bat

**Running.** Law 18 counts a run each time the two batters cross and each reaches the other end. A run is "short" if a batter fails to make good their ground in turning for a further run; the umpire calls and signals it, and that run is not scored. If a boundary follows, the short running is disregarded, unless the boundary came from an overthrow.

**Boundaries.** Law 19 awards:

| Boundary | How | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four | The ball crosses the boundary having touched the ground | 4 |
| Six | The ball is hit over the boundary without touching the ground inside it | 6 |

If the batters have already run more than the boundary allowance, they keep whichever is greater, the boundary or the runs completed.

## Extras: runs not credited to a batter

Some runs are added to the team's total but not to any batter's score. These are **extras**:

| Extra | When | Scored as | Charged to the bowler? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wide | Ball out of the striker's reach for a normal stroke, or passing over head height after landing | One-run penalty plus any runs taken, all as wides | Yes |
| No ball | An illegal delivery, such as overstepping the crease | One-run penalty; runs off the bat go to the batter | Yes |
| Bye | Ball passes without touching bat or body; batters run | Byes | No, credited to the team as byes |
| Leg bye | Ball hits the batter's body; batters run | Leg byes | No, credited to the team as leg byes |

Three details from the Laws are easy to miss:

- **The no ball penalty stands even if a batter is out.** Law 21 awards the one run the moment no ball is called.
- **Wides and no balls do not count as one of the six balls**, so they are bowled again.
- **Leg byes need effort.** Law 23 allows them only if the striker attempted to play the ball with the bat or tried to avoid being hit.

[No ball and wide rules](/blog/no-ball-and-wide-rules/) explains when each is called.

## Penalty runs

The Laws punish certain offences with **5 penalty runs**. Law 28, for example, awards 5 penalty runs to the batting side if a fielder stops the ball illegally, such as with a cap or other equipment. Penalty runs can go to either side: some batting-side offences, such as deliberate short running, cost the batting side.

## Overthrows under the 2026 Laws

MCC's summary of the 2026 changes calls the new overthrow Law the most significant overhaul in some time, because for the first time the Laws define an overthrow. Under Law 19.8:

- It is an overthrow if a fielder, trying to run a batter out or prevent further runs, **directs the ball towards the wickets** and the ball then crosses the boundary.
- It is **not** an overthrow if the ball goes over the boundary from a fielder trying to stop it, or from a short pass between fielders near the boundary. That is a misfield, and a normal boundary.

For an overthrow, the batting side gets the boundary allowance, plus the runs completed when the ball was thrown, plus the run in progress if the batters had already crossed.

## A worked over

| Ball | What happened | Runs added | Counts in the over? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pushed for a single | 1 | Yes (1) |
| 2 | Defended, no run (a dot ball) | 0 | Yes (2) |
| 3 | Driven for four | 4 | Yes (3) |
| – | Wide, no run taken | 1 | No |
| 4 | Two runs | 2 | Yes (4) |
| – | No ball, batter hits it and takes one run | 2 (1 penalty + 1 to the batter) | No |
| 5 | Caught | 0, one wicket | Yes (5) |
| 6 | Hit for six | 6 | Yes (6) |

The over took eight deliveries to complete six legal balls and added **16 runs** for one wicket. The bowler is charged with all 16, because wides and no balls are debited to the bowler. The batters' scores add up to 14: the extras are the one wide and the one no-ball penalty.

## Reading the scorecard

A score is usually written runs-then-wickets, such as 250/6; Wikipedia's article on cricket scoring notes that Australia switches the order, writing 6/250. Overs are written in overs and balls: 45.3 means 45 overs and three balls, not 45.3 tenths of an over, which matters when working out run rates.
