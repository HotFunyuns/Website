A new phone is exciting until you discover what did not come across. Android's setup can copy most of your phone in one go, but Google's own help lists what it leaves behind, and some of those gaps matter, especially for apps that keep your data only on the device. This guide covers how the transfer works, what moves and what does not, and a checklist before you reset the old phone.

## Before you start

Google's help gives a short preparation list:

1. **Charge both phones.**
2. Make sure you can **unlock the old phone** with its PIN, pattern or password.
3. **Sign in to your Google Account** on the old phone, which Google says transfers the most data.
4. **Back up** your data.
5. Connect to **Wi-Fi**; Google says you need an internet connection, and recommends Wi-Fi for both phones.
6. **Set aside time**: a transfer can take **from a few minutes to a few hours**.
7. **Check storage** on the new phone; if space is short, you can choose what to transfer.

## The transfer, step by step

When the new phone asks whether to set up using another device, choose **Android device**. Google describes scanning a QR code to pair the phones, entering the old phone's PIN, pattern or password to bring over Wi-Fi credentials and Google accounts, then choosing to **copy apps and data** and selecting what to bring. A cable or a wireless connection both work, and Google says both methods support the same data.

Two rules are easy to miss. Google says you can **only perform the data transfer once**, and recommends doing it **during setup**, because the option may not be available afterwards. And if an organisation such as a school or employer manages the phone, you may not be able to transfer everything.

## What copies and what does not

Google lists both sides:

| Copies during setup | Does not copy during setup |
| --- | --- |
| Apps and certain app data | Downloads, such as PDF files |
| Music, photos and videos | Photos, videos and music in hidden folders |
| Contacts stored on the phone or SIM | Apps not from the Google Play Store |
| Text messages and their multimedia | Data from apps that do not use Android backup |
| Call history | Accounts other than Google Accounts, and their data |
| Wallpaper | Contacts and calendars synced to services other than Google |
| Most phone settings | Ringtones and certain settings |

Two lines on the right deserve particular attention before you switch: **downloads**, and **data from apps that do not use Android backup**. A folder of downloaded PDFs, such as tickets, statements or scanned forms, stays on the old phone unless you move it yourself, for example to cloud storage or a computer.

## Apps that keep data only on your phone

Google's list draws the line at **Android backup**: apps and certain app data copy, but data from apps that do not use Android backup will not. Some apps have their own route instead. Google's example is WhatsApp: chats and data are restored from the cloud after you sign in on the new phone, but only if WhatsApp backups were turned on on the old one. So before you switch, check each important app for one of three things: its own cloud backup or account, an export option, or nothing at all.

## A worked example: a switch checklist

You are moving phones on Saturday. On Friday evening: back up, move your Downloads folder of PDFs to cloud storage, note which apps hold on-device data, turn on any in-app backups such as WhatsApp's, and charge both phones. On Saturday: run the transfer during setup, then open your messaging app, notes, tracker apps and photos on the new phone. Only when everything checks out, and ideally after a few days of using the new phone, sign the old one out of your accounts and reset it.
