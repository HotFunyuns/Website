**The NHL salary cap is the Upper Limit of a "Team Payroll Range": every club's payroll, counted by each contract's average annual value, must sit between a Lower Limit and an Upper Limit.** For 2026-27, the league and the NHL Players' Association set the range at $76.9 million to $104.0 million, with a midpoint of $90.4 million. Only two exceptions let a club go above the Upper Limit — long-term injury relief and a cushion for performance bonuses — and from this season the cap applies in the playoffs too.

The rules come from the collective bargaining agreement first signed in 2013, as amended by memorandums of understanding in 2020 and June 2025. The 2025 changes took effect on September 16, 2026. Three items in the published 2025 memorandum are available only to players, so anything they change is not reflected here.

## The 2026-27 numbers

| Figure | 2026-27 | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upper Limit (the cap) | $104.0 million | League and union announcement, May 6, 2026 |
| Midpoint | $90.4 million | Same announcement |
| Lower Limit (the floor) | $76.9 million | Same announcement |
| Most one contract can pay in a season | $20.8 million | CBA: 20 per cent of the Upper Limit |
| Minimum NHL salary | $850,000 | 2025 memorandum |
| Entry-level maximum | $1,025,000 | 2025 memorandum: the minimum salary plus $175,000 |
| Longest contract | 7 years for a player on the club's reserve list since the last trade deadline; 6 years otherwise | 2025 memorandum |

The minimum salary rises to $900,000 in 2027-28, $950,000 in 2028-29 and $1 million in 2029-30. From 2028-29, the memorandum ties the Upper Limit to a formula — half of Hockey Related Revenue from two seasons earlier, less projected benefits, divided among the clubs and multiplied by 115 per cent — and says it can never fall below $104 million. The Midpoint is then $15 million below the Upper Limit and the Lower Limit $15 million below that.

## How a contract counts: the cap hit

A contract counts against the cap at its **Averaged Amount**, known as the cap hit: total salary and bonuses divided by the number of seasons. A three-year deal paying $9 million, $8 million and $7 million counts $8 million in each year, whatever cash changes hands.

Averaging invites front-loading, so the agreement limits how much salary can move between years. For a contract whose first half pays more on average than the whole deal — a "front-loaded" contract in the CBA's test — two rules now apply:

- **Year to year**, salary and bonuses may not change by more than 20 per cent of the first year's figure.
- **No year** may fall below 71 per cent of the highest year.

Those limits were 25 and 60 per cent under the 2020 memorandum, so the room for front-loading has narrowed. Worked example: the $9 million, $8 million, $7 million deal above passes, because each step is $1 million — under 20 per cent of $9 million — and $7 million is more than 71 per cent of $9 million. A $10 million, $7 million, $7 million deal fails twice: its first step, $3 million, is more than $2 million, and $7 million is below 71 per cent of $10 million.

Two more limits shape new deals: signing, roster and reporting bonuses together may not exceed 60 per cent of a contract's total, and new contracts may not include deferred compensation.

## One-way, two-way and "burying" a contract

A **two-way** contract pays one salary in the NHL and a lower one in the minors; a **one-way** contract pays the same wherever the player is. Entry-level contracts are automatically two-way.

Sending a player to the minors does not always clear his cap hit. For a one-way contract, the CBA keeps counting the cap hit minus the minimum salary plus $375,000 while the player is loaned. With the 2026-27 minimum at $850,000, that relief is $1,225,000: a buried $3 million one-way contract still counts $1,775,000. For a two-way contract, minor-league pay above the same threshold counts.

The 2025 memorandum also ended "paper" loans. A player sent to the minors must now play in a minor-league game before his club can recall him, with an exception for a goaltender when the club would otherwise not have two available. As the Sabres' summary of the deal explained, clubs can no longer free roster space by loaning a player who keeps travelling with the team and recalling him a day later.

## Long-term injured reserve (LTIR)

The CBA's "Bona-Fide Long-Term Injury/Illness Exception", known as LTIR, is the main way a club can exceed the Upper Limit:

1. **The threshold.** The club's physician must believe the player will be unfit to play for at least 24 days and 10 regular-season games.
2. **The relief.** The club may add replacements whose salaries push its payroll above the Upper Limit, but only by up to the injured player's own salary and bonuses.
3. **The catch.** The injured player's money still counts against the cap, and before he can return the club must get back under the Upper Limit.

The 2025 memorandum adds a limit: a replacement's cap hit may not exceed the previous season's average league salary — unless the league and the union approve a larger one based on how long the injury is likely to last, in which case the injured player is ineligible for the rest of that season, playoffs included.

The Carolina Hurricanes' transaction primer, written under the previous agreement, explains two practical consequences that still follow from the CBA's structure. A club's LTIR spending room depends heavily on its payroll on the day it uses the exception, so clubs try to have payroll as high as possible that day. And unlike ordinary cap space, which accrues as a season goes on, LTIR room does not grow.

## The playoff cap

This is the biggest change in the new agreement for contending teams. The Sabres' summary of the deal described the old situation plainly: long-term injury relief let a team spend above the cap during the regular season and then, come the playoffs, activate its injured players and exceed the cap.

From 2026-27, for **every playoff game**:

- each club lists 18 skaters and two goaltenders;
- the cap hits of those 20 players — counting salary and signing, roster and reporting bonuses, but not performance or games-played bonuses, and net of any salary another club retained — are added up;
- the club's buyout charges, retained-salary charges, one-way loan charges, cap recapture and similar items are added on top;
- the total must not exceed the Upper Limit.

Rosters go to the league's Central Registry before each game. The parties agreed to review the rules after the first playoffs under them, and either side may seek changes after the second.

## Buyouts

A club can end a contract early in set windows — mainly from the later of June 15 or 48 hours after the Stanley Cup Final until June 30 — after placing the player on unconditional waivers. If he clears, he becomes an unrestricted free agent and the club owes him:

- **one-third** of his remaining base salary if he is under 26 when the buyout takes effect, or
- **two-thirds** if he is 26 or older,

paid over **twice** the remaining term. Signing bonuses are not part of that base-salary calculation.

Worked example: a 28-year-old has three years left at a flat $3 million salary, which is also his cap hit. Two-thirds of the remaining $9 million is $6 million, paid over six years: $1 million a year. Following the CBA's method, each of the first three years counts the $3 million cap hit minus the $2 million saved — $1 million — and each of the next three counts the $1 million payment. The club frees $2 million a year now and carries $1 million a year later. Contracts with uneven salaries produce uneven charges, and every buyout charge also counts against the new playoff cap.

## Entry-level contracts

| Age when signing the first contract | Entry-level term |
| --- | --- |
| 18 to 21 | 3 years |
| 22 to 23 | 2 years |
| 24 | 1 year |
| 25 or older | Not in the entry-level system |

Age is measured on September 15 of the year the contract is signed. An 18- or 19-year-old who plays fewer than 10 NHL games in the first season of his contract generally has it extended by a season, and a player who signed at 18 can have it extended a second time. The 2025 memorandum removed a special rule that had put some European players aged 25 to 27 into the system for a year.

For 2026-27, an entry-level player's salary, signing bonuses and games-played bonuses together may not exceed the greater of the minimum salary plus $175,000 — $1,025,000 — and a figure set by draft year. Signing bonuses are capped at 10 per cent of that maximum, and minor-league pay is capped by draft year too: $87,500 for a player drafted in 2026.

## What the cap means for a GM

The mechanics add up to a few habits. Plan in cap hits, not cash. Leave room for injuries rather than relying on LTIR, which now stops at the playoffs. Treat buyouts as borrowing against future seasons. And value entry-level players for their capped pay — one reason drafting well matters so much under a hard cap.
