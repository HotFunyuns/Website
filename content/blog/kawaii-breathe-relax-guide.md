[Kawaii Cute Breathe Relax Care](/apps/kawaii-breathe-relax/) is a breathing app that does the counting for you. You breathe along with an animated guide, a cloud that grows as you inhale and shrinks as you exhale or one of several cute animals, while a timer and cues show the rhythm, including any hold in the pattern. Inside the app its title reads **Kawaii Cute Breathe Relax Calm**. This guide explains how to choose a routine and turn a few minutes of breathing into a habit.

## The animated guide, timer and cues

The Google Play listing presents it as a welcoming start to mindfulness and breath-focused meditation: a small pause at your desk, a calm pause during a stressful day, or a few moments before sleep. It says you do not need meditation experience to start, with "no perfect performance to achieve", and the app's page on this site adds that there is **no score to beat**.

| Feature | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Animated guide | A cloud or a cute animal grows on the inhale and shrinks on the exhale |
| Timer and cues | Show when to breathe in, hold and breathe out, so you do not count seconds |
| Breath counter | Shown in the store screenshots |
| Pause and resume | Shown in the store screenshots; pause whenever you need to |
| Characters and scenes | Pick a guide and a matching scene you enjoy coming back to |

The four steps on the app's page on this site are simple: choose a guide, start the timer, stay with the rhythm, and return when you need it.

## Five routines in the store screenshots

The store screenshots show five kinds of routine:

- **One-minute reset**: a one-minute session for a quick pause during the day.
- **Equal breathing**: an equal-breathing pattern, paced by the timer.
- **Gentle breath holds**: patterns that include a hold, which the timer cues. Box breathing and 4-7-8 are two well-documented patterns that use holds.
- **Multi-step routines**: routines made of several steps.
- **Bedtime wind-down**: a routine for the evening, before sleep.

The NHS's basic exercise for stress has no hold at all: breathe in gently, counting steadily from 1 to 5 if that helps, then let the breath flow out for the same count. The technique guides on this site cover two patterns that use holds, [box breathing](/blog/box-breathing-explained/) and [4-7-8 breathing](/blog/4-7-8-breathing-explained/), and one that does not, [diaphragmatic breathing](/blog/diaphragmatic-breathing-explained/).

## Anchoring a daily breathing break

The NHS advises that you get the most benefit from its breathing exercise if you do it **regularly, as part of your daily routine**, and suggests repeating it for at least five minutes. Cleveland Clinic's integrative medicine specialist makes a similar point about 4-7-8 breathing: picking set times of day makes the habit easier to stay with, and it matters less when you practise than that you actually do. So pick one or two moments you already have:

1. **A daytime anchor**: after lunch, or between two tasks, use the one-minute reset.
2. **An evening anchor**: in bed, use the bedtime wind-down.

A worked example of what that adds up to: two five-minute sessions a day make 10 minutes; over a week, 70 minutes. Even the one-minute reset, used three times a day, adds up to 21 minutes a week of paced breathing. Because the app keeps no score, a missed day costs nothing; just start again.

## What the 2023 breathwork meta-analysis found

The research is limited. A 2023 meta-analysis in *Scientific Reports* pooled 12 randomised controlled trials with 785 adults and found that breathwork was associated with lower self-reported stress than control conditions, a small-to-medium effect, and concluded that breathwork "may be effective" for stress and mental health. Its authors also judged most studies to be at moderate risk of bias and urged caution "to avoid a miscalibration between hype and evidence". [Slow breathing and relaxation](/blog/slow-breathing-and-relaxation/) looks at that research in more detail.

## When to stop, and what the app is not

The app's page on this site sets out its limits:

- It is a **relaxation tool, not a treatment**. It does not diagnose or treat anything, and it should not replace care for anxiety, sleep or breathing conditions.
- If any pattern, especially a breath hold, makes you **dizzy or uncomfortable, stop and breathe normally**.
- Anyone dealing with persistent anxiety, sleep problems or a breathing condition should speak to a health professional.
