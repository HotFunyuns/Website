Every shift gets a letter grade. That single design choice is the most distinctive thing about Hockey Career Pro League Game, and it is a good place for a publisher or a hockey community to start thinking about a partnership. Reign Creative LLC owns the game and welcomes publishing, distribution, community and acquisition conversations.

## Shift grades as a design hook

According to the Google Play listing, coach objectives set what your player should do on each shift, and a grade from **A to F** says how it went. The point, as the app page's FAQ puts it, is that you can tell whether you did your job even on a shift without a goal or an assist. Off-puck play is part of the assessment: following the puck, finding position as play moves and contributing away from it.

For a publisher, that is a marketing message in itself — a hockey game that rewards positional play. For a community, it is a ready-made scoring system for challenges. Real-world context sits in the site's explainer on [how long a hockey shift is](/blog/how-long-is-a-hockey-shift/).

## What an arcade publisher would be publishing

The core loop, per the listing: choose **Career Single Control** to play your own role or **Team Control** to direct the wider action; take shifts against coach objectives; build the case for a bigger role with goals and assists; and use training and practice drills between games to improve technique. The league has 32 teams and an 84-game regular schedule, then playoffs and a championship chase. The app's page on this site says the format is the game's own and that the game is not affiliated with any real league, team or players' association.

Who is it for? The app's page names hockey fans who want to play their position rather than only manage it, players who like being graded on their role rather than their points, anyone who wants to switch between one skater and the whole team, and people who enjoy a long regular season with a playoff race. A publisher's audience research would start from those four descriptions rather than from any figures, which we do not publish.

The game is free to download, ad-supported, and Google Play lists in-app purchases. The app's page describes no online multiplayer and notes that controlling a skater well takes practice before the grades improve, which a publisher's onboarding plans would want to address. The player-facing walkthrough is the [Hockey Career Pro League Game guide](/blog/hockey-career-pro-league-guide/); the [app page](/apps/hockey-career-pro-league/) has store links.

Publishing in new regions, distribution, localisation, co-development and cross-promotion are all on the table. A publisher could also pair it with [Hockey Career Sim 2027](/apps/hockey-career-sim/), our decision-led hockey career; the site's [comparison of hockey career games](/blog/hockey-career-games-compared/) explains how they differ.

## Community formats built on grades and drills

Hockey communities could run:

- **Grade challenges**, where members try to post the most A grades over a set of games.
- **Drill weeks**, focused on the practice drills between games.
- **Season races** across the 84-game schedule.

Each would be organised around existing features; prizes, sponsorship or branded events would be scoped separately and agreed in writing.

A worked example for a season race: covering the full 84-game regular schedule in one week means 84 ÷ 7 = 12 games a day, while a more relaxed four-week race needs 84 ÷ 28 = 3 a day. Organisers would pick the pace that suits their members.

## Two titles for one game

The app's page notes that the in-game title reads **Hockey Pro Career League**, while the Google Play title is Hockey Career Pro League Game. A publisher would likely want to settle on one name for marketing, and a community partner should mention both so members can find it.

If a publisher wants to own the game, Google's [transfer process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) moves it between developer accounts. Google notes that subscriptions, comments and ratings travel with the app, that promotions do not (though issued promo codes should still work), and that orders placed beforehand stay with the original account. The receiving account needs an active payments profile for an app with in-app products, and every ad SDK, AdMob included, has to be updated to credit the new owner. The target developer approves the request, after which Google's support team reviews it.
