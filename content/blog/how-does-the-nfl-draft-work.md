Every spring, the NFL turns college prospects into professionals in a single televised event: the **draft**. It decides which team holds a player's rights, sets up rookie contracts and shapes rosters for years. This guide explains the mechanics as they stand for the 2026 draft, drawing on Wikipedia's article on the NFL draft. How the order of picks is decided has its own guide, [how draft order works](/blog/how-football-draft-order-works/).

## Stan Kostka and the 1936 origin

Wikipedia traces the draft to a bidding war in 1935 over a prospect, Stan Kostka. The league's response, instituted in 1936, was to give every team an equal opportunity to sign eligible players. The first draft began at the Ritz-Carlton Hotel in Philadelphia on 8 February 1936, and it has been held every year since.

The economic effect was immediate. Wikipedia records that the Giants' owner, Tim Mara, cut his salary offer to the player Ken Strong from $6,000 to $3,200 a year for 1936, because he expected the draft to change the league's salary structure. With the draft in place, a prospect has one team holding his rights rather than several bidding for him, the situation Kostka had exploited.

## Who can be drafted

The eligibility rule is short: players who have been **out of high school for at least three years** are eligible. Wikipedia notes the rule requires only that a player be three years removed from high school graduation, regardless of what they did in that time, and that a redshirt year in college counts towards the three years.

Before the draft, prospects are evaluated. Wikipedia describes the **NFL Scouting Combine**, a six-day assessment held in late February or early March in Indianapolis, and university **Pro Days**, where NFL scouts watch drills on campus. College all-star games also showcase prospects; Wikipedia notes that since the 2024 draft, juniors may take part in three of them, the Senior Bowl, the East–West Shrine Bowl and the HBCU Legacy Bowl. [How sports scouting works](/blog/how-sports-scouting-works/) explains what evaluators look for.

## Seven rounds over three April days

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Rounds | Seven |
| Picks per round | 32, one per team before trades |
| Compensatory picks | 32 in total, based on free-agency losses and gains |
| Total selections | 256, before any extra awarded picks |
| Dates | Since 2026, the fourth Thursday to the fourth Saturday of April |
| Days | Round 1 on day one; rounds 2–3 on day two; rounds 4–7 on day three |

Wikipedia records that the three-day format began in 2010, and that from 1965 the draft stayed in New York City for 50 years, until 2015, after which host cities have been chosen by a yearly bidding process.

## The clock on each pick

Each team has a time limit for its pick:

| Round | Time per pick |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 8 minutes |
| 2 | 7 minutes |
| 3–6 | 5 minutes |
| 7 | 4 minutes |

The first-round limit was 15 minutes until 2007, then 10 minutes from 2008, and was cut to 8 minutes starting in 2026, according to Wikipedia.

**A worked example of the clock.** If every first-round pick used its full eight minutes, the 32 picks alone would take 32 × 8 = 256 minutes, more than four hours. The limits are ceilings: a team can submit its pick before the clock runs out.

## Trading picks three drafts ahead

Picks are currency. Wikipedia notes teams may trade picks only for the **next three draft cycles**, with the following year's picks becoming tradeable once a draft starts. During the 2022 draft, for example, picks through 2025 could be traded. Teams use charts to compare what picks are worth; [the NFL draft pick value chart](/blog/nfl-draft-pick-value-chart/) explains how.

## Contracts, undrafted rookies and Mr. Irrelevant

- **Drafted players** sign rookie contracts under a rookie salary cap whose mechanism, Wikipedia notes, is set out in the collective bargaining agreement with the NFLPA; [the NFL rookie contract explained](/blog/nfl-rookie-contract-explained/) covers the details.
- **Undrafted players**, Wikipedia notes, may sign a contract with any team in the league after the draft.
- **The last pick** is nicknamed "Mr. Irrelevant", a name Wikipedia attributes to former receiver Paul Salata in 1976.

## From draft night to a 17-game schedule

The draft feeds a league built around a fixed schedule: the NFL's football operations site explains that each team plays 17 games over an 18-week season, with a formula determining all 272 matchups. [How sports schedules are made](/blog/how-sports-schedules-are-made/) explains that formula.
